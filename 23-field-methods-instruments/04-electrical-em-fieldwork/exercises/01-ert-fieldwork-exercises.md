# Exercises — 23-04-01 ERT Fieldwork: Electrode Layouts, Contact Resistance, Reciprocity

Attempt these before reading the
[solutions](./01-ert-fieldwork-solutions.md). The lesson is
[01 ERT Fieldwork](../01-ert-fieldwork.md). All values are **SYNTHETIC**.

## ★ Foundational

**★1.** Compute the geometric factor of a Wenner array with $a = 10\ \mathrm{m}$,
first from the closed form and then from the exact four-electrode formula using
positions $A = 0$, $M = 10$, $N = 20$, $B = 30$ m. State the unit of $K$.

**★2.** An apparent resistivity of $120\ \Omega\,\mathrm{m}$ is obtained from
$\Delta V = 20\ \mathrm{mV}$ and $I = 4\ \mathrm{mA}$. Compute $K$ and the relative
error in $\rho_a$ for a 3 % voltage error and a 1 % current error. Give the absolute
error in $\Omega\,\mathrm{m}$.

## ★★ Applied

**★★3.** A transmitter has 200 V compliance and a ground resistance of 30 Ω.
Compute the injected current for a contact resistance of 500 Ω and for 50 kΩ, and
the ratio of the two measured voltage signals in dB.

**★★4.** A reciprocal check is run with a measured voltage noise of 4 %.
Compute the tolerance for the relative difference between the normal and reciprocal
apparent resistivities, and state one defect that a difference of 25 % would
indicate.

## ★★★ Research-oriented

**★★★5.** Design an ERT layout for a target at 40 m depth over a 300 m line:
state the electrode spacing and count, the resulting maximum current–potential
separation, the configurations you would include and why, the contact-resistance
and reciprocity checks with their tolerances, and the effect of the design on
acquisition time. Show the arithmetic and state one assumption you cannot verify
without a synthetic model.

**★★★6.** Specify the field quality-control procedure for an ERT survey: the
measurements taken at every electrode before acquisition, the checks repeated during
the day, the acceptance tolerances with their derivations, the actions each failure
triggers, and the metadata archived with the data. A procedure that could not be
executed by a two-person crew is a failed answer.
