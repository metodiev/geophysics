# Exercises — 23-04-04 GPR Fieldwork: Antenna Choice, Grids, Coupling, Calibration

Attempt these before reading the
[solutions](./04-gpr-fieldwork-solutions.md). The lesson is
[04 GPR Fieldwork](../04-gpr-fieldwork.md). All values are **SYNTHETIC**.

## ★ Foundational

**★1.** Compute the propagation velocity, wavelength and $\lambda/4$ resolution for
$\varepsilon_r = 6$ at 400 MHz. State the unit of each.

**★2.** A target's diffraction hyperbola has its apex at $t_0 = 8.0$ ns and a time
of 11.3 ns at a horizontal offset of 0.75 m. Compute the velocity, the implied
relative permittivity and the depth.

## ★★ Applied

**★★3.** Using the velocity of ★2, compute the trace spacing required for a
migration, and the number of traces a 30 m line requires at that spacing. Then
compute the acquisition time at 4 s per trace.

**★★4.** A material has a one-way attenuation of 2.5 dB/m and the system's dynamic
range is 90 dB. Compute the maximum depth of the budget, and the depth at which the
two-way loss is 60 dB.

**★★5.** A target at a known 2.00 m depth is measured at 26.0 ns two-way time.
Compute the true velocity. Then compute the depth that would be reported for that
measurement using $\varepsilon_r = 9$, and the error as a percentage.

## ★★★ Research-oriented

**★★★6.** Design a GPR survey for a buried-utility map over a 30 m × 30 m site with
a service corridor expected between 0.5 m and 2.5 m depth: state the antenna
frequency and its resolution justification, the trace spacing in both directions,
the number of traces and the field time, the velocity calibration method, the
coupling and surface-condition records you would keep, and the two situations that
would force a change of frequency. Show the arithmetic.
