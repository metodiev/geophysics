# Exercises — 23-01-04 Permits, Land Access, Cultural Heritage and Ethics

Attempt these before reading the
[solutions](./04-permits-and-ethics-solutions.md). The lesson is
[04 Permits and Ethics](../04-permits-and-ethics.md). All counts, probabilities
and widths are **SYNTHETIC**, and none of this is legal advice.

## ★ Foundational

**★1.** A survey area is divided into 40 parcels, each agreed with probability
0.85 within the planning horizon. Compute $P(\text{all agreed})$, the expected
number of refusals, and the probability that at least one parcel is refused. Give
each to three significant figures.

**★2.** For the same 40 parcels, compute the per-parcel agreement probability
that would be needed for a 95 % chance that every parcel is agreed.

**★3.** A protected zone is 20 m wide and the rule mandates a buffer of
$\pm 15\ \mathrm{m}$. Compute the excluded width, the factor by which the
excluded area grows relative to the zone alone, and the excluded area as a
fraction of a survey area where the zone coverage is 0.8 %.

## ★★ Applied

**★★4.** A line is 1500 m long and is modelled as 150 segments of 10 m. The
protected coverage is 0.8 %. Compute the probability that the line clips a zone,
and the expected number of clipping lines out of 90.

**★★5.** Twelve parcels remain unresolved. The per-week resolution probability is
0.35 and parcels resolve independently. Compute the expected number still
unresolved after 8 weeks and the probability that all twelve are resolved after
8 weeks. Explain why these two numbers convey different things.

**★★6.** A consent question is put to 30 parties, each consenting independently
with probability 0.90. Compare the publishable fraction under an all-or-nothing
protocol and under a parcel-level protocol, and give the ratio.

## ★★★ Research-oriented

**★★★7.** Design the engagement process for a survey on land where a community
holds recognised rights: state the sequence of steps, what free, prior and
informed consent requires at each step, the documents that result, and the three
metrics you would report to the funder. State two things the process cannot
guarantee, and one design decision that must be taken *before* consent is
negotiated.

**★★★8.** Design a cultural-heritage risk assessment for a 3 km² survey: name the
data sources, define the metric (expected clipped line-km), compute it for a
stated mask and line geometry, and state how you would choose the buffer width
by trading line-km lost against risk. State three limitations of the assessment.
