# Exercises — 23-06-01 Sample Preparation, Coring and Drying Protocols

Attempt these before reading the
[solutions](./01-sample-preparation-solutions.md). The lesson is
[01 Sample Preparation](../01-sample-preparation.md). All values are **SYNTHETIC**.

## ★ Foundational

**★1.** A plug has a diameter of 25.0 mm. Using an end-effect model of $0.10\,D$,
compute the velocity bias at L/D = 2 and L/D = 3 as a percentage and in m/s for a
4.5 km/s rock, then state which ratio you would cut for a 1 % velocity target.

**★2.** A wet sample is 118.40 g and its dry mass is 110.20 g. Compute the water content
as a percentage of the wet mass and of the dry mass.

## ★★ Applied

**★★3.** The plug of ★2 has a saturated mass of 120.05 g and an immersed mass of
68.30 g, with $\rho_w = 0.998$ g/cm³. Compute the pore volume, the bulk volume, the bulk
density and the porosity, then compute the grain density and the porosity from the
grain-density route as a cross-check.

**★★4.** Saturation is performed under a 2 kPa vacuum. Compute the residual air
saturation and the porosity bias it produces on a 20 % porosity sample, and state
whether a gravimetric check on a second specimen would detect it.

**★★5.** A clay-rich specimen dries with a 12 h time constant and has a water content of
7.441 % of dry mass. Compute the mass change per 24 h as a function of elapsed time, and
the earliest time at which a 0.01 % per 24 h criterion is met.

## ★★★ Research-oriented

**★★★6.** A formation is expected to have a porosity of about 22 % with a
between-specimen standard deviation of 1.5 points. You must report a mean with a 95 %
half-width no larger than 0.4 points. Compute the number of specimens required, state
the cost statement that follows, and then state what you would do instead if only 10
plugs can be cut from the core — including the exact wording of the uncertainty you
would report.
