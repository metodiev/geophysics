# Exercises — 23-06-04 Laboratory Resistivity, IP and Dielectric Measurements

Attempt these before reading the
[solutions](./04-electrical-lab-measurements-solutions.md). The lesson is
[04 Electrical Laboratory Measurements](../04-electrical-lab-measurements.md). All values
are **SYNTHETIC**.

## ★ Foundational

**★1.** A specimen has $\rho = 5$ Ω·m, $A = 10.0$ cm² and $L = 50.0$ mm. Compute its
resistance, then the apparent resistivity a two-electrode measurement with 200 Ω per
contact would give.

**★2.** Compute the geometric factor for the specimen of ★1, then the spacing error that
would produce a 1 % resistivity error.

## ★★ Applied

**★★3.** A Cole-Cole model has $\tau = 0.5$ s and $c = 0.8$. Compute the frequency of the
imaginary-part peak, and state why the phase at that frequency is the observable to report
rather than the magnitude.

**★★4.** A Debye relaxation has $\varepsilon_s = 30$, $\varepsilon_\infty = 6$ and
$\tau = 2.0$ µs. Compute the frequency of the $\varepsilon''$ peak, the frequency of the
$\tan\delta$ peak, and $\tan\delta$ at its peak.

**★★5.** A resistivity budget has terms of 0.4 % (voltage), 0.4 % (current), 0.1 %
(length), 0.2 % (area), 1.5 % (temperature) and 3.0 % (pore-fluid conductivity). Compute
the combined relative uncertainty, each term's variance share, and state which single
improvement would most reduce the total.

## ★★★ Research-oriented

**★★★6.** You must measure the resistivity and the spectral IP of 15 saturated sandstone
plugs with a target of 2 % (95 %) on the resistivity and 0.5 mrad on the phase, and the
result will be compared with a field survey.

Design the measurement: the electrode configuration and why; the frequency range and how
it is chosen from the expected relaxation time; the calibration and reference-specimen
checks; the full uncertainty budget showing that the targets are met or that they cannot
be; and the statement about the field comparison that the laboratory cannot support.
