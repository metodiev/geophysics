# Exercises — 23-07-04 Case Study: A Marine Geophysical Cruise

Attempt these before reading the
[solutions](./04-case-study-marine-cruise-solutions.md). The lesson is
[04 Case Study: A Marine Geophysical Cruise](../04-case-study-marine-cruise.md). All values are
**SYNTHETIC**.

## ★ Foundational

**★1.** For a 3 km target at 2200 m/s, compute the two-way time, the record length with a 0.8 s
migration tail, and the far offset from the 1.4× depth rule.

**★2.** For 360 channels at 12.5 m group interval and a 20 m shot interval, compute the cable
length, the fold and the bin size.

## ★★ Applied

**★★3.** Compute the survey time for 30 lines of 45 km at 5.0 knots with a 0.4 h turn per line;
then add 25 % weather downtime and 100 h of transit. State the two terms a plan most often
omits, and their size here as a fraction of the steaming time.

**★★4.** Compute the data volume for 70 000 shots with 360 traces of 800 samples at 4 bytes per
sample. Then compare it with a vessel day rate of 150 k units over 35 days, and state which is
the budget and what that implies for decisions taken at sea.

**★★5.** Compute the cross-line displacement at a 2400 m offset for feather angles of 3°, 7° and
12°. Then state how the tolerance choice trades against schedule, using the lesson's rejection
fractions as a guide.

## ★★★ Research-oriented

**★★★6.** You must acquire 1500 line km over a target at 2.5 km below the seafloor with a
required resolution of 20 m, in a 30-day vessel window (including transit), in a season with
expected 25 % weather downtime.

Produce the acquisition plan: the record length and cable length with the arithmetic; the group
and shot intervals, the bin and the resulting fold, with the argument for each; the line
direction and spacing with a reason; the schedule including transit and turns, and what the
25 % downtime does to the line count; the feather tolerance and its cost; the at-sea QC and the
repeat line; and the two claims the cruise will not be able to support.
