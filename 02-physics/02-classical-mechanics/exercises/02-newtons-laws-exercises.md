# Exercises — 02-02-02 Newton's Laws and Free-Body Analysis

Attempt these **closed-book** before opening the
[solutions](02-newtons-laws-solutions.md). Difficulty:
★ foundational · ★★ applied · ★★★ research-oriented.

Use $g = 9.82025\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$ at the mean radius,
$\omega = 7.292\,115\times10^{-5}$ rad s⁻¹ and $R = 6\,371$ km unless told
otherwise. Nominal densities: crust $2700$, mantle $3300$, water $1025$,
air $1.225\ \mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$. State a regime of validity with every
numerical answer in ★★ and ★★★.

---

## ★ Foundational

**★1.** For each situation, list every force on the named body, name the body
exerting it, and identify the third-law partner of each force: (a) a book on a
table; (b) a block sliding down a rough incline; (c) the Moon in its orbit.
Which listed forces are *not* partners of each other, and why does that matter?

---

**★2.** A block rests on an incline. (a) Derive $\tan\theta_c = \mu_s$. (b) With
$\mu_s = 0.55$, compute the threshold angle. (c) Does the answer depend on the
block's mass? Justify with the equations.

---

**★3.** A 70 kg person stands in a lift. (a) Compute the normal force when the
lift accelerates upward at 1.2 m/s² and when it accelerates downward at
1.2 m/s². (b) What is the normal force in free fall? (c) Which of these is the
person's *weight*, and which is the reading of a scale in the lift?

---

**★4.** Write the dimensions of force, torque and energy, and explain in one
sentence why torque and energy share dimensions but cannot be added.

---

## ★★ Applied

**★★5.** An Atwood machine has $m_1 = 6$ kg and $m_2 = 4$ kg. (a) Derive the
acceleration and the tension. (b) Evaluate both. (c) Verify the result with the
work–energy relation after the heavier mass has fallen 1 m. (d) State what the
tension would be if the string were cut.

---

**★★6.** (a) Compute $\omega^{2}R$ at the mean radius and express it as a
percentage of $g_r = GM/R^{2}$. (b) Compute $g_{\text{eff}}$ and the deflection of
the plumb line at 60° latitude using the centrifugal term only. (c) State two
things the calculation omits, and say whether they are larger or smaller than the
deflection you computed.

---

**★★7.** A plateau stands 2 km above the reference level. (a) Using
$\rho_c = 2700$ and $\rho_m = 3300\ \mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$, compute the Airy root.
(b) Compute the pressure at the base of the root under the load and away from it,
and confirm they agree. (c) If the density contrast is uncertain by
$50\ \mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$, what is the uncertainty in the root?

---

**★★8.** A 0.1 mm diameter quartz grain ($\rho = 2650\ \mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$)
settles in water at 20 °C ($\eta = 10^{-3}$ Pa s). (a) Compute the Stokes
terminal velocity. (b) Compute the Reynolds number and check whether the Stokes
regime assumption is self-consistent. (c) Express the settling speed in m/day and
comment on what that means for a sediment trap.

---

## ★★★ Research-oriented

**★★★9.** A relative gravimeter must reach 0.01 mGal. (a) Compute the vertical
gravity gradient at the mean radius. (b) Compute the resulting levelling
requirement. (c) Compute the *centrifugal* contribution to the gradient that the
instrument cannot distinguish from elevation, and state whether it is likely to
matter for a survey spanning 50 km in latitude. (d) Write the sensitivity
statement you would put in a survey report.

---

**★★★10.** Slope stability with pore pressure. For a planar slide of thickness
$h$ on a plane inclined at $\theta$, saturated to the surface, the Coulomb
criterion becomes $\tau = c' + (\sigma_n - p)\tan\phi'$.

(a) Derive the critical angle in the drained case ($p = 0$) and in the
saturated case with $\rho_{\text{sat}} = 2000$
$\mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$ and $\rho_w = 1000\ \mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$.
(b) Evaluate both for $\phi' = 30°$ and $c' = 0$.
(c) Explain in terms of forces why saturation lowers the threshold, and why a
rainfall event can change a stable slope into an unstable one without changing
the slope's geometry or its friction angle.
