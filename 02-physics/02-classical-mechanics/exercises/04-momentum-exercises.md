# Exercises — 02-02-04 Momentum, Impulse and Collisions

Attempt these **closed-book** before opening the
[solutions](04-momentum-solutions.md). Difficulty:
★ foundational · ★★ applied · ★★★ research-oriented.

Use $g = 9.82025\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$ and
$1\ \mathrm{kt\ TNT} \equiv 4.184\times10^{12}$ J where needed. State a regime of
validity with every numerical answer in ★★ and ★★★.

---

## ★ Foundational

**★1.** (a) State the impulse–momentum theorem and derive it from
$\mathbf{F} = d\mathbf{p}/dt$. (b) A 200 kg body receives an impulse of 500 N s.
Compute its velocity change. (c) A force $F(t) = F_0(1 - t/\tau)$ for
$0\le t\le\tau$ acts on a body of mass $m$. Compute the impulse and the resulting
velocity change for $F_0 = 10^{4}$ N, $\tau = 0.05$ s, $m = 200$ kg.

---

**★2.** Blocks of 2 kg (moving at +3 m/s) and 4 kg (at rest) collide on a
frictionless line with coefficient of restitution $e = 0.5$.
(a) Compute the total momentum. (b) Compute the final velocities.
(c) Compute the kinetic energy before and after, and the energy lost.
(d) Check the energy loss against $\tfrac12\mu v_{\text{rel}}^{2}(1-e^{2})$.

---

**★3.** Two bodies of 2 kg and 3 kg are 10 m apart and attract each other.
(a) State why their centre of mass does not accelerate. (b) Compute the
centre-of-mass position. (c) If the 2 kg body moves 1 m towards the other, how
far does the 3 kg body move, and why?

---

**★4.** Explain in one or two sentences each: (a) why momentum conservation holds
in an inelastic collision; (b) why energy conservation does not; (c) why an
explosion source has zero net momentum but still radiates.

---

## ★★ Applied

**★★5.** A weight drop for seismic surveying uses a 500 kg mass released from
1.5 m, with an assumed coefficient of restitution $e = 0.3$.
(a) Compute the impact speed and kinetic energy. (b) Compute the impulse
delivered to the ground. (c) Compute the rebound energy and the fraction of the
impact energy retained in the ground. (d) State what fraction of that retained
energy becomes seismic waves, and how it must be determined.

---

**★★6.** A rocket burns 900 kg of its 1000 kg initial mass with an exhaust speed
of 3000 m/s in free space. (a) Compute $\Delta v$. (b) If the burn takes 20 s,
compute the average thrust. (c) Repeat (a) with an extra 1000 kg of payload and
comment on why staged rockets exist.

---

**★★7.** A fault is modelled by the four point forces
$+\mathbf{F}\hat{x}$ at $(0,+a)$, $-\mathbf{F}\hat{x}$ at $(0,-a)$,
$+\mathbf{F}\hat{y}$ at $(+a,0)$ and $-\mathbf{F}\hat{y}$ at $(-a,0)$.

(a) Compute the net force. (b) Compute the net torque about the origin.
(c) What would the net force and torque be for the "same" set of forces with the
sign of the second couple reversed? (d) Which of the two is the double couple,
and why does the distinction matter for what an earthquake does to the Earth?

---

**★★8.** A landslide delivers a force of $10^{9}$ N for 20 s into a lake.
(a) Compute the impulse. (b) If the affected water mass is $10^{12}$ kg, compute
the velocity change. (c) Explain why this source is modelled as a single force
rather than a double couple, and what observable distinguishes it.

---

## ★★★ Research-oriented

**★★★9.** Momentum and seismic moment.

(a) Write the dimensions of linear momentum, of impulse, and of seismic moment,
and identify which two coincide.
(b) For a single-force source of $F = 10^{9}$ N acting for 20 s, compute the
impulse and the momentum change of the Earth.
(c) For a double couple with $M_0 = 10^{18}$ N m, compute the radiated seismic
energy for an assumed stress drop of 3 MPa, and compare it with the Earth's spin
angular momentum in magnitude — explaining why that comparison is meaningless.
(d) State what measurement determines the impulse of a landslide source, and what
measurement determines the seismic moment of a fault source.

---

**★★★10.** A 1000 kg projectile hits the ground at 300 m/s vertically.

(a) Compute its momentum and kinetic energy.
(b) Assume the impact is stopped in 5 ms and compute the average force.
(c) Compare that force with the projectile's weight and comment on the ratio.
(d) The ground's response depends on whether the momentum is transferred as a
single force or as a double couple. Explain which applies here, and how the
resulting seismic radiation pattern differs.
