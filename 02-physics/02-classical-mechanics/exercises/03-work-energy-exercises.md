# Exercises — 02-02-03 Work, Energy, Power and Conservative Forces

Attempt these **closed-book** before opening the
[solutions](03-work-energy-solutions.md). Difficulty:
★ foundational · ★★ applied · ★★★ research-oriented.

Use $g = 9.82025\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$, $GM_\oplus = 3.986\,004\,418\times10^{14}$
m³ s⁻² (defining) and $R = 6\,371$ km. The TNT-equivalent convention is
$1\ \mathrm{kt} \equiv 4.184\times10^{12}$ J. State a regime of validity with
every numerical answer in ★★ and ★★★.

---

## ★ Foundational

**★1.** (a) Compute the work done by a constant 40 N force over 5 m at 0°, 60°
and 90° to the displacement. (b) Compute the work done by a spring of stiffness
$k = 500\ \mathrm{N\,m^{-1}}$ stretched from 0 to 0.20 m, and check it against
$\int kx\,dx$. (c) State which of these forces is conservative and why.

---

**★2.** (a) Derive $W = -\Delta U$ for a conservative force. (b) Compute the
relative error of $mgh$ against the exact Newtonian work for $h = 5$ km.
(c) At what height does the approximation err by 1%?

---

**★3.** (a) Derive the escape speed from a spherical body of mass $M$ and radius
$R$. (b) Evaluate it for the Earth at $R = 6\,371$ km. (c) Compute the specific
energy needed, and state the sign criterion for a bound orbit.

---

**★4.** A pump lifts water at $2\ \mathrm{m^3\,min^{-1}}$ through 50 m.
(a) Compute the mass flow rate. (b) Compute the useful power. (c) If the pump
draws 25 kW, compute the efficiency.

---

## ★★ Applied

**★★5.** A 5 kg block slides 3 m along a surface with $\mu_k = 0.4$.
(a) Compute the work done by friction and by gravity. (b) If it starts at
4 m/s, compute its final speed, and state whether mechanical energy is conserved.

---

**★★6.** A spring launcher with $k = 500\ \mathrm{N\,m^{-1}}$ is compressed by
0.20 m and releases a 0.5 kg mass vertically upward. (a) Compute the stored
energy and the launch speed. (b) Compute the maximum height reached, and say
which approximation limits the answer. (c) A student computes the height with
$mgh$ and with the exact Newtonian form: how much do they differ at that height?

---

**★★7.** A 1 km × 1 km column of crust is raised 1 km.
(a) Compute the work against gravity, using $\rho = 2700\ \mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$.
(b) Compute how long a 1 MW process would take, and a 1 GW process.
(c) Explain why the energy is not the interesting number; the power is.

---

**★★8.** A synthetic source has $\mu = 30$ GPa, fault area
$2\times10^{7}\ \mathrm{m^{2}}$ and slip 0.5 m, with an assumed stress drop of
3 MPa. (a) Compute the seismic moment. (b) Compute the radiated energy using
$E_s = (\Delta\sigma/2\mu)M_0$. (c) Express $E_s$ in kilotonnes of TNT equivalent
and state which inputs are assumed.

---

## ★★★ Research-oriented

**★★★9.** A geothermal doublet produces 10 MW of net electrical power from a
reservoir of $1\ \mathrm{km^{3}}$ at $\Delta T = 200$ K, with
$\rho c = 2.16\times10^{6}\ \mathrm{J\,m^{-3}\,K^{-1}}$.

(a) Compute the thermal energy in place.
(b) Compute the reservoir lifetime at 10 MW and at 100 MW of *thermal* output.
(c) Explain why the "lifetime" is not the energy divided by the power in reality,
and name two mechanisms that shorten it.
(d) State what additional measurement would most change the answer.

---

**★★★10.** (a) Verify that $\mathbf{F} = -kx\,\hat{\mathbf{x}}$ is conservative by
computing the work around a closed rectangular path and showing it vanishes.
(b) Do the same for $\mathbf{F} = -c\,|\mathbf{v}|\mathbf{v}$ and show that the
result does *not* vanish when the speed changes around the loop.
(c) Explain why the curl test $\nabla\times\mathbf{F} = \mathbf{0}$ is a
sufficient but not necessary condition for path independence (hint: think about
domains).
