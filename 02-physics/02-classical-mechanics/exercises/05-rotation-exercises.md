# Exercises — 02-02-05 Rotational Motion, Moment of Inertia and Angular Momentum

Attempt these **closed-book** before opening the
[solutions](05-rotation-solutions.md). Difficulty:
★ foundational · ★★ applied · ★★★ research-oriented.

Use $\omega_\oplus = 7.292\,115\times10^{-5}$ rad s⁻¹ (defining),
$a = 6\,378\,137$ m and $1/f = 298.257\,223\,563$ (defining, WGS 84), and
$g = 9.82025\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$. State a regime of validity with every numerical
answer in ★★ and ★★★.

---

## ★ Foundational

**★1.** Compute the moment of inertia about the symmetry axis for:
(a) a uniform disk of mass 2 kg and radius 0.3 m; (b) a solid sphere of mass
5 kg and radius 0.2 m; (c) a thin spherical shell of mass 5 kg and radius 0.2 m.
(d) Which of (b) and (c) stores more rotational energy at the same $\omega$, and
by what factor?

---

**★2.** A thin rod of mass 3 kg and length 1.2 m rotates about an axis
perpendicular to it. (a) Compute $I$ about the centre. (b) Compute $I$ about one
end, using the parallel-axis theorem, and check against $ML^{2}/3$.
(c) State the geometry assumption in $ML^{2}/12$.

---

**★3.** A torque of 5 N m acts on the disk of ★1(a). (a) Compute the angular
acceleration. (b) Compute the angular velocity after 3 s from rest.
(c) Compute the angular momentum at that time. (d) State the condition for
$\tau = I\alpha$ to be valid.

---

**★4.** (a) Compute the **sidereal** rotation period $2\pi/\omega_\oplus$ in
seconds, and explain why it is not 86 400 s. (b) Using
$\Delta\omega/\omega = -\Delta T/T$ with $T = 86\,400$ s, compute the change in
$\omega$ for a day longer by 1 ms, and state the approximation involved in using
86 400 s rather than the sidereal period.

---

## ★★ Applied

**★★5.** A two-layer Earth model has $\rho_{\text{core}} = 11\,000$,
$\rho_{\text{mantle}} = 4500\ \mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$, core radius 3 480 km and outer
radius 6 371 km. (a) Compute the core mass fraction. (b) Compute $I$ as the sum
of a uniform core and a uniform shell. (c) Compute $I/(MR^{2})$. (d) The measured
value is smaller than yours: state what that implies about the density
distribution and how the measured value is obtained.

---

**★★6.** Using $I = 8.4699\times10^{37}\ \mathrm{kg\,m^{2}}$ and the defining
$\omega_\oplus$: (a) compute the spin angular momentum and the rotational energy;
(b) compute the change in each for a day longer by 1 ms; (c) compute the torque
needed to produce that change in one year; (d) compare that torque with the torque
of a $10^{6}$ N force acting on a $6.371\times10^{6}$ m lever arm.

---

**★★7.** A solid sphere, a solid cylinder and a thin spherical shell of the same
mass and radius are released from rest and roll without slipping down an incline
of height 1 m. (a) Compute the final centre-of-mass speed of each.
(b) Compute the rotational share of the kinetic energy for each.
(c) Which arrives first, and does the answer depend on the mass or the radius?
Justify with the formula.

---

**★★8.** (a) Compute the radius of gyration of a uniform sphere of radius $R$ and
of a thin shell of the same radius, as a fraction of $R$. (b) Interpret the
difference physically. (c) For the Earth, state what quantity the radius of
gyration would give you if you knew $I$ and $M$.

---

## ★★★ Research-oriented

**★★★9.** Interior inference from the moment of inertia. You are given a measured
moment-of-inertia factor $C/(MR^{2}) = 0.33$ (a literature value; treat it as
given) and the mass and radius.

(a) Compute the mean density and compare it with the surface rock density; state
what that comparison alone implies.
(b) Show that a two-layer model with a core of adjustable density can reproduce
any factor between the uniform value and the "mass at the centre" limit; state
the bounds.
(c) Explain why the moment of inertia alone cannot determine the core radius.
(d) Name two further observables that constrain the core radius, and say what
each measures.

---

**★★★10.** Angular momentum exchange between the atmosphere and the solid Earth.

(a) Explain why a change in the atmosphere's axial angular momentum must be
compensated by an opposite change in the solid Earth's, and state the assumption
that makes the compensation exact.
(b) If the atmospheric axial angular momentum changes by
$1\times10^{26}\ \mathrm{kg\,m^{2}\,s^{-1}}$ (a representative seasonal
magnitude), compute the corresponding change in the solid Earth's rotation rate
using $I$ from ★★5.
(c) Convert that to a length-of-day change, and state which measured LOD signal
has this size.
(d) Explain why the resulting LOD signal is invisible in the long-term secular
trend but dominates the seasonal one.
