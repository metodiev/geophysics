# Exercises — 12-01-03 Upper-Mantle Discontinuities at 410 and 660 km

Attempt these before reading the
[solutions](./03-upper-mantle-discontinuities-solutions.md). The lesson is
[03 Upper-Mantle Discontinuities at 410 and 660 km](../03-upper-mantle-discontinuities.md).
All parameters are **SYNTHETIC** teaching values.

Useful relations: $\mathrm{d}z/\mathrm{d}T = \gamma/(\rho g)$,
$\gamma = \Delta S/\Delta V$,
$\Delta t_\text{prec} = 2z q_s(p)$ with $q_s = \sqrt{v_s^{-2}-p^2}$,
$R = (Z_2-Z_1)/(Z_2+Z_1)$ with $Z = \rho v_s$,
$\lvert R(f)\rvert = R_0\lvert\operatorname{sinc}(\pi f \tau)\rvert$,
$g = 9.81\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$.

## ★ Foundational

**★1.** A boundary has $\gamma = +2.5\ \mathrm{MPa\,K^{-1}}$ and sits where
$\rho = 3700\ \mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$. Compute $\mathrm{d}z/\mathrm{d}T$ in
m K$^{-1}$, and the displacement produced by $\Delta T = +150\ \mathrm{K}$. Then
compute the two-way precursor delay for a boundary at 400 km with
$v_s = 4.75\ \mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}$ at $p = 0.050\ \mathrm{s\,km^{-1}}$.

**★2.** Compute the Clapeyron slope from $\Delta S = -6.0\
\mathrm{J\,mol^{-1}\,K^{-1}}$ and $\Delta V = -2.8\times10^{-6}\
\mathrm{m^{3}\,mol^{-1}}$. State whether the transition is exothermic or
endothermic, and whether a cold descending slab will be helped or opposed by the
displacement of this boundary.

**★3.** A boundary has $(\rho, v_s) = (3.30, 4.40)$ above and $(3.42, 4.62)\
\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}$ (with $\rho$ in g cm$^{-3}$) below. Compute $Z_1$, $Z_2$ and
the normal-incidence reflection coefficient $R$, and express it as a percentage
amplitude.

## ★★ Applied

**★★4.** A precursor study reports $\delta(\Delta t) = -1.5\ \mathrm{s}$ for the
410 km boundary and $+2.0\ \mathrm{s}$ for the 660 km boundary, at
$p = 0.055\ \mathrm{s\,km^{-1}}$ with $v_s = 4.70$ and $5.80\
\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}$. Compute (a) the two boundary displacements, (b) the
transition-zone thickness change, (c) the mean-depth change, and (d) the two
temperature anomalies implied by the demonstration's $\mathrm{d}z/\mathrm{d}T$
coefficients. Comment on whether (d) is self-consistent.

**★★5.** A reflection from a boundary is measured at 0.25 Hz with half the
amplitude expected from a sharp interface. (a) Compute the transition width
assuming a linear ramp with $v_s = 4.70\ \mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}$. (b) Compute the
width that would produce the same half-amplitude at 0.10 Hz. (c) For a 12 km ramp,
what fraction of the sharp-boundary amplitude survives at 0.10 Hz?

**★★6.** A 410 km precursor delay changes by $+2.0\ \mathrm{s}$ at
$p = 0.055\ \mathrm{s\,km^{-1}}$. Compute the two possible explanations —
boundary displacement, and a fractional velocity change distributed over the
250 km transition zone — and state whether their signs are consistent with a
single thermal anomaly.

## ★★★ Research-oriented

**★★★7.** Separate the thermal signal from the dynamical-pressure signal. Derive
the sensitivity of (i) the transition-zone *thickness* and (ii) the *mean* depth
of the two boundaries to a temperature change and to a uniform dynamic-pressure
change. Build the 2×2 cross-talk matrix, and say which observable to use for which
parameter and what the residual leakage is.

**★★★8.** Suppose the 660 km transition had a *positive* Clapeyron slope of the
same magnitude. Recompute the transition-zone thickness sensitivity and the
mean-depth sensitivity, and state what would be lost for thermometry. Then
compute what the *difference* of the two boundary depths would measure instead.

**★★★9.** A triplication is produced by a jump from $v_s = 5.80$ to
$6.30\ \mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}$ at 660 km depth. Compute the slowness gap
$\Delta p$ in s rad$^{-1}$ and as a percentage, and explain why the gap — not the
jump itself — is what a travel-time record section measures.
