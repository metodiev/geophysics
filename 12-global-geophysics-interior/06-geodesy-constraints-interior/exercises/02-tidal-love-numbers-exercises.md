# Exercises — 12-06-02 Love Numbers and Theoretical Tides

Attempt these before reading the
[solutions](./02-tidal-love-numbers-solutions.md). The lesson is
[02 Love Numbers and Theoretical Tides](../02-tidal-love-numbers.md). All models
are **SYNTHETIC** teaching values.

## ★ Foundational

**★1.** A homogeneous body has $R = 1.0\times10^{6}$ m and
$\rho = 3000\ \mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$. A degree-2 surface displacement of
$u = 1.0$ m is imposed. Compute the induced degree-2 potential coefficient
$V_{2}(R)$ in J/kg and state its sign and meaning.

**★2.** Derive the fluid-body limit $h_{2} = 5/2$ and $k_{2} = 3/2$ from the
equipotential condition, and compute the gravity-tide factor $\delta$ and the
surface-tilt factor $\tau$ for a fluid body and for a rigid body.

## ★★ Applied

**★★3.** For $\hat\mu = \mu/(\rho g R) = 0.5$, compute $h_{2}$, $k_{2}$,
$k_{2}/h_{2}$, $\delta$ and $\tau$. State whether the body is closer to fluid or
to rigid, and which single number decides that.

**★★4.** A gravimeter measures a gravity-tide factor $\delta = 1.13$. Using the
elastic homogeneous model and the exact ratio $k_{2} = \tfrac35 h_{2}$, compute
$h_{2}$, $\hat\mu$ and the rigidity $\mu$ for
$\rho = 3500\ \mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$, $g = 10\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$ and
$R = 6.0\times10^{6}$ m.

**★★5.** A Maxwell body has $\hat\mu = 0.5$ and
$\tau_{M} = 9.45\times10^{10}$ s. Compute the complex rigidity at a period of
$10^{9}$ hours, then $|h_{2}|$, $|k_{2}|$ and the phase lag of $k_{2}$. State
which regime the observation is in.

**★★6.** Show analytically that the amplification
$|\mathrm{d}\ln\hat\mu/\mathrm{d}\ln h_{2}|$ for
$\hat\mu = (1/9.5)(2.5/h_{2} - 1)$ is $-2.5/(9.5\,h_{2}\hat\mu)$. Evaluate it at
$h_{2} = 0.6$ and compare with the value the demonstration measures.

## ★★★ Research-oriented

**★★★7.** Design an observing programme that separates the *elastic* from the
*anelastic* part of a body's tidal response, using observations in two frequency
bands. State the observables, the precision required, and the residual
ambiguity (how many parameters remain, and which additional measurement would
fix them).

**★★★8.** The lesson derives $k_{2} = \tfrac35 h_{2}$ for a *homogeneous* body
from the degree-2 shell potential. Prove the ratio again, in a form that makes
clear which assumptions entered, and then explain what a measured ratio of
$k_{2}/h_{2} = 0.40$ would tell you about the body. State whether such a
measurement alone determines a profile.
