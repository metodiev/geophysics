# Exercises — 12-05-01 Free Oscillations of the Earth (Normal Modes)

Attempt before the
[solutions](./01-free-oscillations-solutions.md). Lesson:
[01 Free Oscillations of the Earth](../01-free-oscillations.md). All models
**SYNTHETIC**; the structure and periods below belong to invented teaching models.

## ★ Foundational

**★1.** Write the radial equation of motion for a spherically symmetric elastic
medium and verify by substitution that, for constant moduli,
$U \propto j_1(kr)$ is a solution with $k^{2} = \rho\omega^{2}/(\lambda+2\mu)$.

**★2.** For a homogeneous sphere with $R = 6371$ km and
$\alpha = 10.0\ \mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}$, compute the fundamental radial period from
the root $x_0 = 2.60263$, and state the units of every factor.

**★3.** State the two boundary conditions of the radial eigenproblem and explain
in one sentence why the second is a *traction* condition rather than a
displacement condition.

## ★★ Applied

**★★4.** The demonstration's finite-difference errors at $n = 100$, 200 and 400
points are $2.48\times10^{-5}$, $6.58\times10^{-6}$ and $1.69\times10^{-6}$.
Compute the successive error ratios, state the order of the scheme, and predict
the error at $n = 800$ from the trend.

**★★5.** Using $T \propto R/\alpha$, compute the fundamental period of the
demonstration's homogeneous model after the radius is reduced by 3% and the P
speed is increased by 2%, and compare with the unperturbed 32.0431 min.

**★★6.** In the demonstration's layered model the fundamental mode has 17.2% of
its kinetic energy below 660 km. Estimate the fractional period change from a 2%
reduction in $\alpha$ and $\beta$ confined to that region, state your assumption,
and say whether the estimate is an upper or lower bound.

## ★★★ Research-oriented

**★★★7.** A single period constrains the ratio $R/\alpha$. Design a measurement
programme that determines $R$ and $\alpha$ separately, naming at least two
observables and explaining what each one senses.

**★★★8.** The demonstration's model is purely elastic. For the gravest spheroidal
modes of a real planet, self-gravitation contributes as much as elasticity.
Explain qualitatively what term enters the equation of motion, why the mode
period depends on the density distribution as well as the moduli, and which
observable therefore carries density information that a body-wave travel time
does not.

## Notes on answers

An answer that reports a period without stating the model it came from is
incomplete, and an answer that claims convergence order without showing the ratios
is unsupported.
