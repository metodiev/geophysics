# Exercises — 12-04-03 Palaeomagnetic Record and Reversal Frequency

Attempt before the [solutions](./03-palaeomagnetic-record-solutions.md). Lesson:
[03 Palaeomagnetic Record and Reversal Frequency](../03-palaeomagnetic-record.md).
All models **SYNTHETIC**; the chronologies below are generated, not observed.

## ★ Foundational

**★1.** A synthetic chronology has 500 reversals in 200 Myr. Compute the mean
interval and the mean reversal rate, and state the units of each.

**★2.** For an exponential interval distribution with mean $\bar{\Delta t}$,
compute the median. Evaluate it for $\bar{\Delta t} = 0.380$ Myr and compare with
the ratio observed in the demonstration.

**★3.** Compute the probability that a stationary Poisson process with rate
$5\ \mathrm{Myr^{-1}}$ produces an interval of at least 10 Myr, and express it as
a power of ten.

## ★★ Applied

**★★4.** A record's longest interval is 25 Myr and its mean interval is 0.400 Myr.
Compute the probability of that gap under a constant rate equal to the record's
mean, then compute the rate at which the gap would be a 5% fluctuation and
express it as a fraction of the mean rate.

**★★5.** At a rate of $5\ \mathrm{Myr^{-1}}$, intervals are exponential with mean
0.200 Myr. Estimate the fraction of intervals shorter than a 0.5-Myr sampling
resolution, and explain why the resulting count bias is one-sided.

**★★6.** A palaeointensity database is drawn from a lognormal distribution with
median $8\times10^{22}\ \mathrm{A\,m^2}$, and only determinations within a factor
of two of a threshold are retained. Given the demonstration's bias table, state
the approximate bias for a threshold at $0.8\times$ the median and explain why
the bias is not removable by averaging more determinations.

## ★★★ Research-oriented

**★★★7.** Design a stationarity test for a real record of 200 reversals. State
the test statistic, the null, the expected power against a factor-of-two rate
change, and one confound that would inflate the apparent significance.

**★★★8.** The demonstration's bootstrap null uses a *stationary* Poisson process.
Describe what happens to the trend test if the null instead uses a smoothly
varying rate, and explain why that version is more conservative. Then state which
null is appropriate for asking whether the *dynamo regime* changed.

## Notes on answers

A statistical claim must be accompanied by the test's power, and no interval
statistic may be quoted without the sampling resolution that produced the
chronology.
