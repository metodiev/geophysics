# Exercises — 12-07-03 Giant Planet Interiors and Magnetic Fields

Attempt these before reading the
[solutions](./03-giant-planets-solutions.md). The lesson is
[03 Giant Planet Interiors and Magnetic Fields](../03-giant-planets.md). All
models are **SYNTHETIC**; the polytropic constants $K$ are chosen teaching values,
so the masses and radii below are not planetary.

## ★ Foundational

**★1.** Show by substitution that $\theta = \sin\xi/\xi$ solves the Lane-Emden
equation for $n = 1$ with $\theta(0) = 1$ and $\theta'(0) = 0$, and that
$\xi_{1} = \pi$.

**★2.** For $n = 1$, $K = 10^{7}$ and $\rho_{c} = 10^{4}\ \mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$,
compute $\alpha$, $R = \alpha\xi_{1}$ and
$M = 4\pi\rho_{c}\alpha^{3}\omega_{1}$, with $\omega_{1} = \pi$.

**★3.** State the mass–radius exponent $(3-n)/(1-n)$ for $n = 1$, $n = 1.5$ and
$n = 3$, and give the physical meaning of each. Say which of the three is the
giant-planet case and why.

## ★★ Applied

**★★4.** The polytrope with $n = 1.5$ gives $C/MR^{2} = 0.20460$, against
$0.40000$ for a uniform sphere. Compute the ratio and explain, in terms of the
weight $r^{4}/R^{4}$ in the inertia integral, why a compressible body has the
smaller value. State whether a measured $C/MR^{2} = 0.21$ is evidence for a core.

**★★5.** Take $\rho = 10^{3}\ \mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$,
$\Omega = 1.75\times10^{-4}\ \mathrm{s^{-1}}$, $\sigma = 10^{4}\ \mathrm{S\,m^{-1}}$
and a conducting-region radius $R_{c} = 5\times10^{7}$ m. Using $\Lambda = 1$,
compute $B$ and the dipole moment
$m = B R_{c}^{3}(4\pi/3\mu_{0})$. Then compute the magnetic Reynolds number for
$U = 10^{-3}\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$ and $L = 10^{6}$ m, and state whether a dynamo
is plausible.

**★★6.** In the lesson's degeneracy table, the spread profile with $p = 2$ is
matched by a core model with a core mass fraction of $0.008$, while the added
heavy-element mass is $10\%$ of the total. Explain what this says about the
*composition* of the two models, and state precisely which quantity is degenerate.

## ★★★ Research-oriented

**★★★7.** Specify a measurement that would distinguish a body with a distinct
core from one with the same heavy elements spread through its envelope. Use the
lesson's numbers for the difference in $C/MR^{2}$ between the two families, state
the precision needed, and state the assumption that would remain.

**★★★8.** Derive the $n = 1$ result that the radius is independent of the mass,
starting from $\alpha^{2} = (n+1)K\rho_{c}^{1/n-1}/(4\pi G)$ and
$R = \alpha\xi_{1}$. Then state the physical reason a giant planet's radius is a
poor measure of its mass and a good measure of its internal entropy.
