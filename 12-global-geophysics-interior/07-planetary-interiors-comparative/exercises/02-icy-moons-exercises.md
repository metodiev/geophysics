# Exercises — 12-07-02 Icy Moons, Subsurface Oceans and Induction Studies

Attempt these before reading the
[solutions](./02-icy-moons-solutions.md). The lesson is
[02 Icy Moons, Subsurface Oceans and Induction Studies](../02-icy-moons.md). All
models are **SYNTHETIC** teaching values.

## ★ Foundational

**★1.** Compute the skin depth in a layer of conductivity $0.05\ \mathrm{S\,m^{-1}}$
at a period of $5\times10^{4}$ s. Compare it with a 20 km layer and state whether
the layer is electromagnetically thin.

**★2.** Write the radial diffusion equation for a degree-$n$ poloidal magnetic
field inside a spherical conductor, and state the two boundary conditions that
must hold at each shell interface. Explain why the equation has no
first-derivative term.

**★3.** A shell of thickness 5 km has $\sigma = 1\ \mathrm{S\,m^{-1}}$. Compute
its conductance in siemens, and state — using the lesson's table — the amplitude
$|Q|$ and phase of its response at a period of $10^{5}$ s.

## ★★ Applied

**★★4.** Four shells have $(\sigma, d) = (1, 5)$, $(0.5, 10)$, $(0.2, 25)$ and
$(0.1, 50)$ in S/m and km. Compute the conductance of each and show that they are
equal. Explain why the four amplitudes in the lesson differ slightly.

**★★5.** At a period of $10^{4}$ s the response of a 5 km layer is
$|Q| = 0.2724$ with $\mathrm{d}\ln|Q|/\mathrm{d}\ln C = 0.700$ at that operating
point. A measurement gives $|Q| = 0.30$ with a 3% relative error. Compute the
nominal conductance, the admitted range, and say whether the phase would add
independent information.

**★★6.** A shell of negligible thickness has an infinite conductance in the limit
$C\to\infty$ — take it as a perfect conductor. Derive from continuity of the
radial field that the response must tend to $|Q| = n/(n+1)$, and evaluate the
number for $n = 1$.

## ★★★ Research-oriented

**★★★7.** Specify a two-frequency induction experiment intended to separate the
conductivity of a layer from its thickness. State the two frequencies in terms of
the layer's conductance, the precision needed, and the assumption that would
remain even if both measurements were perfect.

**★★★8.** The lesson assumes a global spherical shell. Describe, with a scaling
argument rather than a simulation, how the response of a *partial* (non-global)
conducting region differs from that of a full shell of the same conductance, and
state what that difference does to the inverted conductance. Say what extra
observation would break the ambiguity.
