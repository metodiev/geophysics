# Exercises — 19-02-01 Deriving Finite-Difference Operators via Taylor Expansion

Attempt these before reading the
[solutions](./01-fd-derivation-solutions.md). The lesson is
[01 FD Derivation](../01-fd-derivation.md). All models are **SYNTHETIC**.
Numbers printed by `../code/fd_derivation_demo.py` may be quoted directly.

## ★ Foundational

**★1.** Write the moment equations for a two-point forward first-derivative
stencil and solve for the weights.

**★2.** For the centred three-point second derivative, state the order and the
leading truncation term with its constant.

**★3.** A five-point centred first-derivative stencil has weights
$(1, -8, 0, 8, -1)/12$ on offsets $(-2,-1,0,1,2)$. Verify that the moments
$m = 0, 1, 2, 3$ vanish or equal $1!$ as required, and state the order.

## ★★ Applied

**★★4.** Derive the first-derivative stencil on the offsets $[-1, 0, 0.5]$ and
give its order and leading constant.

**★★5.** A stencil is used on a grid whose right-hand node is at $1.05h$ instead
of $h$, with weights derived for the uniform case. Estimate the resulting
$O(1)$ error as a fraction of $f'(x_0)$ and state whether refinement removes it.

**★★6.** In the demonstration the forward-difference measured error$/h$ is
$-0.2411734$ against the exact $-0.2397128$ at $h = 0.01$. Explain the residual
difference and predict whether it shrinks if $h$ is halved.

## ★★★ Research-oriented

**★★★7.** Design a second-order-accurate first-derivative operator for the first
node of a grid whose first four nodes are at $0$, $0.3h$, $0.9h$ and $2h$. Derive
the weights, verify polynomial exactness, and compare the leading constant with
the uniform three-point stencil's $1/6$.

**★★★8.** A colleague reports a "verified" second-order scheme on a stretched
grid but cannot reproduce the order when the stretching is generated by a
coordinate transform with an error of $10^{-6}$ relative. Analyse whether the
reported order is credible, what the transform error does to the stencil, and
what diagnostic would settle the question.
