# Exercises — 07-06-04 Post-Glacial Rebound: Viscosity and Ice History Inference

Attempt these before reading the
[solutions](./04-post-glacial-rebound-solutions.md). The lesson is
[04 Post-Glacial Rebound](../04-post-glacial-rebound.md). **All models and data
in this module are SYNTHETIC.**

Constants: $R = 6371\ \mathrm{km}$, $\rho_m = 3300\ \mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$,
$\rho_i = 917\ \mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$, $g = 9.81\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$,
$k_l = l/R$, $\tau_l = 2\eta k_l/(\rho_mg)$.

## ★ Foundational

**★1.** Compute $\tau_3$ and $\tau_{30}$ for $\eta = 5\times10^{21}\
\mathrm{Pa\,s}$, giving each in years.

**★2.** Compute the equilibrium (fully compensated) deflection for a $2000\
\mathrm{m}$ ice load and state the ratio used and why it is a ratio.

**★3.** A load vanishes at $t = 0$ with a degree-2 deflection of
$w_0 = 150\ \mathrm{m}$. Compute the deflection at $5\ \mathrm{kyr}$ and
$20\ \mathrm{kyr}$ for $\tau_2 = 6146\ \mathrm{yr}$, and the average uplift rate
in each interval.

## ★★ Applied

**★★4.** Show that doubling the ice scale does not compensate for halving the
viscosity, and quantify the residual mismatch using
$\dot w = -w_0e^{-t/\tau}/\tau$ evaluated at $t = 10\ \mathrm{kyr}$,
$\tau = 6146\ \mathrm{yr}$. State which combination produces the *larger* rate.

**★★5.** The demonstration's fit leaves $5.1\%$ of the $(\log_{10}\eta, s)$ plane
acceptable with one rate, $2.7\%$ with two rates, $0.36\%$ with an RSL curve, and
$0.24\%$ with both plus the second rate. Rank the observable sets by their
constraining power and state the factor by which each addition narrows the
region.

**★★6.** A colleague argues that because satellite gravity measures mass and
levelling measures height, combining them gives an independent handle on the
viscosity. Using the relation $\dot g \propto (\rho_mg/R)\dot w$, explain why
this is not so for a single degree and epoch, and state what *does* make space
gravimetry valuable.

## ★★★ Research-oriented

**★★★7.** Design an experiment that tests whether a *layered* viscosity structure
can be recovered from a network of RSL curves. Specify the layered truth, the
network (how many sites, where, and what degrees they see), the observations and
their noise, the inverse problem, and the degeneracy that remains even with
perfect data.

**★★★8.** The sea-level equation (meltwater redistribution, changing ocean
geometry and the ocean's own load response) is omitted in the demonstration.
Design a synthetic experiment that measures the size of the error this omission
introduces in an inferred viscosity, and state which observable would reveal the
omission most clearly.
