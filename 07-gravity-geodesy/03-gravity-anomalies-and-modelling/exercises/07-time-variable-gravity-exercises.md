# Exercises — 07-03-07 Time-Variable Gravity: Hydrology, Ice and Mass Redistribution

Attempt these before reading the
[solutions](./07-time-variable-gravity-solutions.md). The lesson is
[07 Time-Variable Gravity](../07-time-variable-gravity.md). All models are
**SYNTHETIC**;
$G = 6.67430\times10^{-11}\ \mathrm{m^{3}\,kg^{-1}\,s^{-2}}$,
$\rho_w = 1000\ \mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$, $1\ \mu\mathrm{Gal} = 10^{-8}\
\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$.

## ★ Foundational

**★1.** Convert $5\ \mathrm{cm}$ of water to a gravity change in both the
flat-Earth and spherical-shell models.

**★2.** State which of the two constants applies to a $100\ \mathrm{km}$-wide
basin and which to a change in global ocean mass, and why.

**★3.** Write the conversion from a gravity change to an equivalent water height
in the flat-Earth limit, with units.

## ★★ Applied

**★★4.** A catchment loses $0.3\ \mathrm{m\,yr^{-1}}$ of equivalent water over
$150\times150\ \mathrm{km}$. Compute the annual gravity change and the mass loss.

**★★5.** A satellite product shows a feature with a $40\ \mathrm{km}$ Gaussian
footprint recovered as $0.35\ \mathrm{m}$ EWH after a $40\ \mathrm{km}$ filter.
Using the leakage table, estimate the true EWH and state the direction of the bias
if the correction is omitted.

**★★6.** With $1\ \mu$Gal noise and a ten-year record, use the trend/season table
to state the trend precision you would expect for a
$-0.05\ \mathrm{m\,yr^{-1}}$ signal, and explain why the annual amplitude is
better determined.

## ★★★ Research-oriented

**★★★7.** Design a processing chain that distinguishes a secular trend from a
seasonal cycle in a five-year record with $1.5\ \mu$Gal noise and a possible
annual plus semi-annual signal. State the model, the expected parameter
uncertainties and the residual risk of misattribution.

**★★★8.** A basin-scale mass loss is inferred from a gravity change with a stated
filter and noise level. Enumerate the error terms in the mass estimate, order them
by size for a $300\ \mathrm{km}$ region, and state which one a reviewer should
challenge first.
