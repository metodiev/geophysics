# Exercises — 08-03-01 Magnetic Anomaly Physics and Remanent vs Induced Magnetisation

Attempt these before reading the
[solutions](./01-magnetic-anomaly-basics-solutions.md). The lesson is
[01 Magnetic Anomaly Physics](../01-magnetic-anomaly-basics.md). All models are
**SYNTHETIC** teaching values.

## ★ Foundational

**★1.** Convert an ambient flux density of $55\ \mu$T to a field strength $H_0$
in A m$^{-1}$, using $\mu_0 = 4\pi\times10^{-7}$ H m$^{-1}$. State the unit of
each step.

**★2.** A body of volume $2.0$ km$^3$ has $\chi = 0.020$ in that field. Compute
$M_i$ in A m$^{-1}$ and the magnetic moment $m$ in A m$^2$.

**★3.** With the moment from ★2 and a vertical magnetisation observed on axis
directly above the body at $2.0$ km depth, compute $\Delta T$ using
$\Delta T = (\mu_0/4\pi)(2m/z^{3})$. State the answer in nT.

**★4.** State in one sentence why $\Delta T$ is a projection and not a
magnitude, and name the quantity that is measured by the instrument.

## ★★ Applied

**★★5.** The demonstration measures $\Delta T z^{3} = 248.7$ nT km$^3$ at every
depth for its synthetic body. Use it to compute the anomaly at $5.0$ km depth and
explain in one sentence why the product is constant.

**★★6.** For a body magnetised along the field at inclination $I$, the anomaly
over its centre is proportional to $3\sin^{2}I - 1$. Compute the ratio of the
anomaly at $I = 45^\circ$ to the anomaly at $I = 90^\circ$ for the same moment,
and state the inclination at which the anomaly vanishes.

**★★7.** A target anomaly of $25.0$ nT must be explained at $3.0$ km depth.
Compute the required moment, and the volume required if $M = 2.0$ A m$^{-1}$.

## ★★★ Research-oriented

**★★★8.** The total-field expansion was truncated after the linear term. For
$B_0 = 50\ \mu$T and an anomaly of $500$ nT, compute the size of the quadratic
term as a fraction of the anomaly, and state the anomaly amplitude at which the
truncation would reach 1%.

**★★★9.** Design a numerical experiment that measures the dipole approximation
error of a compact body (a cube of side $a$) as a function of distance in units
of $a$, at fixed magnetisation. State the metric, the distances you would sample,
and the expected shape of the curve. Include what you would use as the exact
reference.

**★★★10.** A survey records a negative anomaly over a body. List three distinct
physical explanations, and for each state one measurement that would discriminate
it from the others.
