# Exercises — 08-03-02 Koenigsberger Ratio and Remanence Dominance

Attempt these before reading the
[solutions](./02-koenigsberger-and-remanence-solutions.md). The lesson is
[02 Koenigsberger Ratio and Remanence Dominance](../02-koenigsberger-and-remanence.md).
All models are **SYNTHETIC**.

## ★ Foundational

**★1.** A rock has $\chi = 0.030$ and $M_r = 2.5$ A m$^{-1}$ in a field of
$H_0 = 40$ A m$^{-1}$. Compute $M_i$ and $Q$.

**★2.** For the rock of ★1, suppose the remanence is parallel to the induced
magnetisation. Compute $\left|\mathbf{M}\right|$ and the angle between
$\mathbf{M}$ and the ambient field.

**★3.** For the same rock, suppose the remanence is perpendicular to the induced
magnetisation. Compute $\left|\mathbf{M}\right|$ and compare it with the
parallel case.

## ★★ Applied

**★★4.** In the demonstration, $M_i = 0.3979$ A m$^{-1}$, $M_r = 0.200$
A m$^{-1}$ and the remanence is at $30^\circ$ inclination, $45^\circ$ declination
while the field is at $60^\circ$ inclination, $0^\circ$ declination. Compute
$\gamma$, the angle between $\mathbf{M}_r$ and $\hat f$, then
$\left|\mathbf{M}\right| = M_i\sqrt{1+Q^{2}+2Q\cos\gamma}$, and compare with the
demonstration's $0.5621$ A m$^{-1}$.

**★★5.** A body with $Q = 10$ gives an anomaly of $90.5$ nT over its centre. An
interpreter assuming induced magnetisation computes $\left|\mathbf{M}\right| =
5.793$ A m$^{-1}$ and an apparent susceptibility $\chi_{\text{app}} = 0.1456$.
The true susceptibility is $0.0126$. Compute the factor by which the
interpretation is wrong, and state the direction of the error.

**★★6.** Using the demonstration's table of anomaly versus the angle $\theta$
between $\mathbf{M}$ and the ambient field (9.06 nT at $\theta = 0$,
$-12.68$ nT at $\theta = 120^\circ$), compute the ratio and explain why the
maximum is not at $\theta = 0$.

## ★★★ Research-oriented

**★★★7.** Design a sampling programme that would determine $Q$ and the
magnetisation direction for a magnetite body in a survey area. State the number
of samples, the orientation procedure, the measurement you would make, and what
you would do if the samples showed two superimposed remanence components.

**★★★8.** Using the law of cosines
$\left|\mathbf{M}\right| = M_i\sqrt{1+Q^{2}+2Q\cos\gamma}$, find the condition
on $Q$ and $\gamma$ for which the *along-field* component of $\mathbf{M}$
vanishes, and interpret the result geologically.
