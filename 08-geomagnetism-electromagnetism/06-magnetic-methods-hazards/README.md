# Module 08-06 — Applied Magnetism in Hazard and Environmental Studies

**Stages:** advanced → research · **Prerequisites:** modules 08-01 and 08-03;
basic statistics.

This module takes the physics of the Earth's field and of rock magnetisation and
turns it to three applied purposes: dating (archaeomagnetic directions against a
secular-variation curve), mapping (susceptibility and magnetic anomalies as
proxies for pollution and for buried objects), and monitoring (field changes above
a volcano or a fault). The three share one honest difficulty, stated in each
lesson in its own units: **the magnetic measurement determines a product of
unknowns, not the unknowns.** A direction determines a set of candidate ages, not
an age. An anomaly determines $\chi(a/d)^3$, not a magnetisation and a depth. A
field change determines a moment change *if* the secular-variation trend is known,
and a slow source change is indistinguishable from a small trend error.

That structure is the module's content. Each lesson derives the forward relation,
then spends most of its length on what the relation does *not* pin down: the
multiple crossings of a secular-variation curve, the depth-to-size ambiguity and
the cube-root detection depth of magnetics, the measured false-alarm rate of a
monitoring threshold, and the site-specific nature of a proxy calibration. All of
these are quantified with synthetic data whose truth is known.

All models and data in this module are **SYNTHETIC**. The secular-variation curve,
the sediments and bodies, the pollutant index, the monitoring record, the noise
and the transients are generated by the demonstrations from a fixed seed. No real
site, sample, monument, volcano, fault or published measurement is described.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this module you will be able to:

1. **Derive** the geocentric axial dipole inclination relation
   $\tan I=2\tan\lambda$ and use it as a consistency check on an inclined
   measurement.
2. **Compute** the admissible age intervals of a measured archaeomagnetic
   direction against a secular-variation curve, and **identify** when the curve
   passes the direction more than once.
3. **Quantify** dating precision as angular uncertainty divided by the local rate of
   change, and **explain** why it is a property of the curve and the epoch.
4. **Quantify** the date error produced by a systematic offset between a local curve
   and a reference curve, including the branch jump it can cause.
5. **Derive** the anomaly of a uniformly magnetised sphere and the
   $\frac{2}{3}\chi H(a/d)^3\mu_0$ scaling, and **demonstrate** the depth-to-size
   ambiguity.
6. **Compute** the maximum detection depth of a magnetic body and **explain** its
   cube-root dependence on magnetisation and on the noise floor.
7. **Distinguish** a proxy correlation from a calibration, and **state** the
   normalisation and fractionation effects that break a
   susceptibility–concentration relationship.
8. **Derive** the field change produced by a moment change and **invert** it into
   the moment change a stated detection implies.
9. **Measure** a monitoring estimator's scatter by Monte Carlo, **set** a detection
   threshold from it, and **report** the detection probability and false-alarm rate.
10. **Separate** an ambiguity of the data (a slow source change versus a trend
    error) from a limitation of the estimator, and **state** the design rule that
    follows.

## Mastery checkpoint

Complete this without notes:

> A SYNTHETIC survey at a site of latitude $45^\circ$ yields (a) an
> archaeomagnetic direction of $D=+6.0^\circ$, $I=62.5^\circ$ with a $1^\circ$
> uncertainty, (b) a $30\ \mathrm{nT}$ magnetic anomaly over a soil of
> $\chi=0.015$ (SI), and (c) a monitoring station showing a $0.4\ \mathrm{nT}$
> change over 60 days. **(a)** Give the admissible age intervals from a curve that
> passes that direction at $1200$ and at $400\ \mathrm{yr\,BP}$, and say which is
> better determined if the local rates of change are $3^\circ$ and $7^\circ$ per
> century. **(b)** Compute $a/d$ for the anomaly in a $48\ \mathrm{A\,m^{-1}}$
> field, and state what else you must know before quoting a depth. **(c)** Set a
> detection threshold for the monitoring change given an estimator scatter of
> $0.05\ \mathrm{nT}$, and state the trend accuracy the claim requires.

## Common pitfalls across this module

1. **Reporting a single answer where the method gives a set.** Age intervals, body
   families and detection thresholds are all sets.
2. **Quoting a precision without its rate of change.** Angular error and age error
   are different units.
3. **Reading a magnetisation or a pollutant from an anomaly.** The anomaly measures
   a product, and the proxy is empirical.
4. **Using a curve, a slope or a threshold from another region or site.** All three
   are site- and instrument-specific.
5. **Quoting a detection without a false-alarm rate.** They come from the same
   threshold.
6. **Confusing the instrument's noise with the experiment's scatter.** Averaging
   thousands of samples makes the second far smaller, but only if the scatter is
   measured.
7. **Attributing a mechanism from magnetics alone.** Thermal, piezomagnetic,
   intrusive and core-field changes look alike in one scalar time series.
