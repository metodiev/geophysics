# Exercises — 08-04-05 MT Interpretation: Conductors, Fluids, Melts and Frontiers

Attempt these before reading the
[solutions](./05-mt-interpretation-solutions.md). The lesson is
[05 MT Interpretation](../05-mt-interpretation.md). All models are **SYNTHETIC**.

## ★ Foundational

**★1.** Compute the bulk conductivity from Archie's law for $\phi = 0.02$,
$m = 2$ and $\sigma_{\text{water}} = 3.0$ S m$^{-1}$, and the corresponding
resistivity.

**★2.** Using the Hashin–Shtrikman bounds of the lesson for a solid of
$10^{-4}$ S m$^{-1}$ and a melt of 3.0 S m$^{-1}$, read off the lower and upper
bounds at a melt fraction of 2% and compute their ratio.

**★3.** State in one sentence why the *connectedness* of a phase matters more than
its fraction.

## ★★ Applied

**★★4.** A 2-D inversion shows a conductor of $0.02$ S m$^{-1}$ extending from 30
to 40 km depth. Compute its conductance and state what the data constrain.

**★★5.** For the conductor of ★4, compute the melt fraction a *connected-melt*
interpretation requires, using the upper-bound relation in the limit of small $f$
(this is most easily done by interpolation in the lesson's bound table).

**★★6.** The detectability table gives, for a conductor at 20 km depth, a maximum
change in $\rho_a$ of 2.251% and in phase of 0.453$^\circ$ for a conductance of
10 S. If your phase precision is $0.2^\circ$, state the smallest detectable
conductance by interpolation.

## ★★★ Research-oriented

**★★★7.** A conductor is 100 km wide, 5 km thick and sits at 25 km depth. Write
the interpretation section of a report: state the conductance, the candidate
mechanisms with the conditions each requires, and the two independent
observations that would discriminate them.

**★★★8.** The mantle conductor of ★4 is detected at periods of $10^4$–$10^5$ s.
Using the $k\delta$ criterion of
[06 Geomagnetic Depth Sounding](../06-geomagnetic-depth-sounding.md), decide
whether a 2000 km source at those periods allows a plane-wave interpretation, and
state what you would do if it does not.
