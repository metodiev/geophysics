# Exercises — 13-02-03 Mantle Plumes, Hotspots and Large Igneous Provinces

Attempt these before reading the
[solutions](./03-mantle-plumes-solutions.md). The lesson is
[03 Mantle Plumes](../03-mantle-plumes.md). All parameters are **SYNTHETIC**;
$g = 9.81\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$,
$\rho_0 = 3300\ \mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$,
$\alpha = 3\times10^{-5}\ \mathrm{K^{-1}}$,
$c_p = 1250\ \mathrm{J\,kg^{-1}\,K^{-1}}$ and
$\kappa = 10^{-6}\ \mathrm{m^{2}\,s^{-1}}$ where stated.

## ★ Foundational

**★1.** Compute the density anomaly of a plume with $\Delta T = 150$ K and give
it as a percentage of $\rho_0$.

**★2.** A conduit of radius 40 km has $\eta_c = 2\times10^{19}\ \mathrm{Pa\,s}$.
Compute the maximum velocity, the mean velocity, the volume flux and the buoyancy
flux for $\Delta T = 200$ K.

**★3.** Using the flux of ★2, compute the heat carried per plume and the number
of such plumes needed to transport 44.4 TW.

## ★★ Applied

**★★4.** For a head of radius 300 km and $\Delta\rho = 19.8\ \mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$,
compute the rigid-sphere Stokes rise speed and the time to rise 2900 km. Compare
with the 250 km head in the lesson and state the scaling with radius.

**★★5.** For a blob of size 300 km and $\Delta\rho = 19.8\ \mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$ in
a mantle of $\eta = 10^{21}\ \mathrm{Pa\,s}$, compute the blob Rayleigh number
(compare it with 657.5) and the diffusive time $L^{2}/\kappa$. Compare the
diffusive time with the rise time from ★★4 and state the conclusion.

**★★6.** Take the lesson's synthetic phase relations but shift the solidus up by
50 K: $T_{\text{sol}} = 1400 + 3z$ K, $T_{\text{liq}} = T_{\text{sol}}+400$ K,
$T_{\text{plume}} = 1600-0.5z$ K. Recompute the melting column depth, the mean
melt fraction and the igneous crustal thickness at a productivity of 0.5. Compare
with the lesson's 75 km, 0.319 and 12.0 km and explain the direction of the
change.

## ★★★ Research-oriented

**★★★7.** A hotspot has a measured buoyancy flux of
$3\times10^{3}\ \mathrm{kg\,s^{-1}}$. Invert it for the volume flux for a conduit
of radius 60 km at three conduit viscosities ($10^{18}$, $10^{19}$,
$10^{20}$ Pa s) and state the excess temperature each would require. Then state
which of the three quantities (flux, excess temperature, conduit radius) the
observation actually constrains.

**★★★8.** Design a test of hotspot fixity. State the data, the model, the
measured quantity, the expected signature of a fixed hotspot versus one moving at
$10\ \mathrm{mm\,yr^{-1}}$, and the confound from absolute plate-motion frames. A
specification that could not be executed is a failed answer.
