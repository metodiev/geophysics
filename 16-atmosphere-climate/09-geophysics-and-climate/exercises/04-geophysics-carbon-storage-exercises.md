# Exercises — 16-09-04 Geophysics for Carbon Capture and Storage Monitoring

Attempt these before reading the
[solutions](./04-geophysics-carbon-storage-solutions.md). The lesson is
[04 Geophysics for Carbon Capture and Storage Monitoring](../04-geophysics-carbon-storage.md).
All models and numbers are **SYNTHETIC** teaching values (with standard constants
as anchors).

## ★ Foundational

**★1.** A sandstone has $\phi = 0.20$, $K_{\text{dry}} = 12\ \mathrm{GPa}$ on a
quartz grain $K_{\min} = 37\ \mathrm{GPa}$, and is brine-saturated with
$K_{\text{fl}} = 2.5\ \mathrm{GPa}$. Compute $K_{\text{sat}}$ by Gassmann
substitution, showing the numerator and denominator.

**★2.** For the same rock, compute the saturated densities with brine
($\rho = 1050\ \mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$) and with CO2
($\rho = 700\ \mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$), given a grain density of
$2650\ \mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$.

**★3.** An interface has impedances $Z_1 = 8.0\times10^{6}$ and
$Z_2 = 7.0\times10^{6}\ \mathrm{kg\,m^{-2}\,s^{-1}}$. Compute the reflection
coefficient and state its sign meaning.

## ★★ Applied

**★★4.** Using the result of ★1 ($K_{\text{sat}} = 16.917\ \mathrm{GPa}$) and
$\mu = 9.0\ \mathrm{GPa}$, $\rho = 2330\ \mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$, compute $v_p$ and
$v_s$. Repeat for the CO2 case of ★2 with $K_{\text{sat}} = 12.114\
\mathrm{GPa}$ and the same $\mu$, and state the direction of each change.

**★★5.** A plume creates a mass deficit of $-5.0\times10^{9}\ \mathrm{kg}$ at
$1500\ \mathrm{m}$ depth. Compute the surface gravity change in microGal using
$G = 6.674\times10^{-11}\ \mathrm{m^{3}\,kg^{-1}\,s^{-2}}$, and state whether a
survey with $5\ \mathrm{\mu Gal}$ noise detects it at $2\sigma$.

**★★6.** A gravity-derived mass estimate assumes a plume depth that is wrong by
$+8\%$. Compute the fractional error in the inferred mass and state the general
rule relating depth error to mass error.

## ★★★ Research-oriented

**★★★7.** Design a measurement, monitoring and verification (MMV) programme that
would distinguish a plume that is spreading laterally at constant mass from one
that is losing mass to a shallow leak. Specify the observables, the metric, the
time sampling, and the pattern of results that would falsify the leak hypothesis.

**★★★8.** Seismic data constrain the elastic contrast and gravity data constrain
the mass. Design a synthetic joint inversion that exploits both, and specify the
(then testable) question of whether saturation and geometry can be separated
where a single method cannot.
