# Exercises — 06-04-05 Magnitude Saturation and Why Moment Magnitude Is Preferred

Attempt these before reading the
[solutions](./05-magnitude-saturation-solutions.md). The lesson is
[05 Magnitude Saturation](../05-magnitude-saturation.md). All models are
**SYNTHETIC** teaching values.

## ★ Foundational

**★1.** A band centre sits at $f_b = 0.5\ \mathrm{Hz}$ and the reference corner
frequency is $f_{c,\text{ref}} = 0.6\ \mathrm{Hz}$ at
$M_0 = 10^{15}\ \mathrm{N\,m}$. Compute the crossing moment and the crossing
$M_w$.

**★2.** State the asymptotic slope of a saturated band magnitude in $M_w$ and in
$\log_{10}M_0$.

**★3.** Convert a magnitude difference of $+0.5$ into a factor in moment.

## ★★ Applied

**★★4.** The demonstration reports $\hat{b} = 1.609$ for the $m_b$ sample with
$M_{\min} = 3.98$. Using $\hat{b} = \log_{10}e/(\bar{M} - M_{\min})$, compute the
sample mean.

**★★5.** A catalogue converts magnitudes with
$M_w^{\text{est}} = 1.261\,m_b - 0.935$. Compute the reported $M_w$ for a true
$M_w = 8.0$ event (use the printed $m_b = 6.10$) and the factor by which the
moment is understated.

**★★6.** Explain why the $M_s$ crossing ($M_w = 6.09$) is larger than the $m_b$
crossing ($M_w = 3.49$) using the corner-frequency relation only.

## ★★★ Research-oriented

**★★★7.** A regional catalogue reports only $m_b$ over a range corresponding to
$M_w$ from 3 to 7. Specify the analysis that would recover the b-value of the
seismicity rather than the b-value of the scale: list the data you need, the
functional form you would fit, and the bias you would expect if a single linear
$m_b \to M_w$ term were used instead.

**★★★8.** The model's $M_s$ curve saturates later than observed for real great
earthquakes. Explain the missing physical effect, write down what a finite-fault
model adds to the source spectrum, and predict the direction in which the real
$M_s$ curve departs from the synthetic one.
