# Exercises — 06-07-04 Autocorrelation and Body-Wave Microseismics

Attempt these before reading the
[solutions](./04-autocorrelation-solutions.md). The lesson is
[04 Autocorrelation and Body-Wave Microseismics](../04-autocorrelation.md). All
models are **SYNTHETIC** teaching values.

## ★ Foundational

**★1.** Two media meet at an interface with
$Z_1 = 1.50\times10^{7}\ \mathrm{rayl}$ and
$Z_2 = 1.90\times10^{7}\ \mathrm{rayl}$. Compute $R$ and $T = 1+R$, and state
the units of both.

**★2.** A layer is $3.0\ \mathrm{km}$ thick with $v_p = 6.00\
\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}$. Compute the two-way time to its base.

**★3.** The demonstration's first interface has a two-way time of
$1.7241\ \mathrm{s}$ and the layer above it has $v_p = 5.80\ \mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}$.
Convert the time to a depth and verify the model's $5.0\ \mathrm{km}$.

## ★★ Applied

**★★4.** The single-reflector calibration gives
$A_{\text{peak}}/A(0) = 0.077497$ for $R = +0.069279$. Compute the ratio and
state what the excess represents.

**★★5.** Using the lesson's tuning-curve table, compute the ratio of the response
amplitude at $\Delta = 0.15\ \mathrm{s}$ to that at $\Delta = 0.05\ \mathrm{s}$,
and explain the sign of the effect for two reflectors of opposite polarity.

**★★6.** From the convergence table ($7.456\times10^{-3}$ at $N=1$,
$1.135\times10^{-3}$ at $N=64$), compute the ratio and the implied exponent, and
compare with the fitted $-0.454$.

## ★★★ Research-oriented

**★★★7.** A borehole sensor at $1\ \mathrm{km}$ depth is used instead of a
surface sensor. State how the zero-lag reference changes, what the
autocorrelation then measures, and design one test that would reveal whether
the reference is adequate.

**★★★8.** The three-reflector model recovers amplitude ratios that are
$-13.9\%$ and $-26.2\%$ away from the model values, and the lesson attributes
this to the difference peaks at $1.270\ \mathrm{s}$ and $1.690\ \mathrm{s}$.
Specify a processing step that would reduce the error, predict its effect on the
$A_3/A_1$ ratio, and state the cost.
