# Exercises — 06-07-05 Monitoring Velocity Changes with Coda-Wave Interferometry

Attempt these before reading the
[solutions](./05-velocity-change-monitoring-solutions.md). The lesson is
[05 Monitoring Velocity Changes with Coda-Wave Interferometry](../05-velocity-change-monitoring.md).
All models are **SYNTHETIC** teaching values.

## ★ Foundational

**★1.** A medium speeds up by $0.05\%$. Compute the time shift of an arrival at
lapse time $t = 4\ \mathrm{s}$ and at $t = 24\ \mathrm{s}$, with sign and units.

**★2.** A coda arrival at $t = 15.0\ \mathrm{s}$ advances by
$\delta t = -12.0\ \mathrm{ms}$. Compute $\delta v/v$ in ppm and state the
direction of the change.

**★3.** A study claims $\delta v/v$ to a precision of $19\ \mathrm{ppm}$ using a
window of characteristic lapse time $17\ \mathrm{s}$. Compute the timing
stability the clocks must provide, in seconds and in milliseconds.

## ★★ Applied

**★★4.** The demonstration's single-window precision is $51\ \mathrm{ppm}$ at
$N=1$. Assuming independent windows and the $\sqrt{N}$ law, compute how many
windows are needed to reach $10\ \mathrm{ppm}$, and compare with the measured
value at $N=16$.

**★★5.** Using the shallow-only table (window 2-10 s gives
$+1546\ \mathrm{ppm}$, 10-18 s gives $-23\ \mathrm{ppm}$), state what a
depth-localised change must look like, and give the observation that would
distinguish it from a source-distribution change between epochs.

**★★6.** The measured scatter is $349\ \mathrm{ppm}$ in the $0.5$-$1.0\
\mathrm{Hz}$ band and $19\ \mathrm{ppm}$ in the $4$-$8\ \mathrm{Hz}$ band.
Compute the ratio of centre frequencies, the ratio of scatters, and the implied
power-law exponent; compare with the $1/f$ prediction of the scaling argument.

## ★★★ Research-oriented

**★★★7.** Design a monitoring experiment whose purpose is to detect a
$100\ \mathrm{ppm}$ velocity change at a known depth range, given that the
source field is suspected to change seasonally. Specify the observations, the
processing, the decision rule and the falsification test. State the residual
risk you cannot eliminate.

**★★★8.** The lesson lists four ways a $\delta v/v$ measurement is wrong. For
each, give one *quantitative* diagnostic that would be computed from data
already collected by a monitoring network, and state the threshold at which you
would call the measurement into question.
