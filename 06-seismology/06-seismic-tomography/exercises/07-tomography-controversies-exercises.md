## ★ Foundational

1. Two models of the same region have rms values of $2.60\times10^{-2}$ and
   $2.21\times10^{-2}$ in $v/v_0$ and a mutual difference of
   $1.42\times10^{-2}$. Compute the difference as a fraction of each model's rms
   and of the true anomaly's rms ($3.12\times10^{-2}$).
2. The same data inverted with two dampings correlate at $+0.416$. Compute
   $1-\rho$ and state in one sentence what it means for the two images.
3. The vote map shows mean sign agreement of 0.966 over 30 models. Compute the
   number of models that disagree with the majority in a typical cell.

## ★★ Applied

4. Using the coverage split (worst quarter correlation 0.050, best quarter
   0.447), decide which of two claims is supported: "the models disagree about
   structure beneath the badly covered region" or "the models disagree because
   the badly covered region is not resolved".
5. Combining the two data sets raised the mutual correlation from 0.717 to 0.934
   while the error against the truth stayed at $1.37\times10^{-2}$. State what a
   reader of the joint model gains and what they do not gain.
6. A paper reports a 1.2 per cent anomaly; a second paper reports 0.6 per cent
   for the same cell. Both used the same data. Enumerate at least three
   explanations consistent with the measurements in this lesson, in each case
   naming the number you would ask for.

## ★★★ Research-oriented

7. Design a full comparison protocol between two global models of the same
   region: list the quantities you would compute, the order in which you would
   compute them, and the decision rule at each step.
8. The vote map is built from five damping values and six noise realisations. A
   reviewer objects that the ensemble is arbitrary. Defend or attack the
   construction quantitatively, using the measured spread of amplitudes (a factor
   of two) and of mutual correlations (0.4-0.9).
