# Exercises — 06-13-02 Glacial and Cryoseismology: Icequakes and Calving

Attempt these before reading the
[solutions](./02-glacial-seismology-solutions.md). The lesson is
[02 Glacial and Cryoseismology](../02-glacial-seismology.md). All models are
**SYNTHETIC**; use $E$, $\nu$, $\rho$ values as stated.

## ★ Foundational

**★1.** For $\rho = 920\ \mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$, $E = 9.0\ \mathrm{GPa}$,
$\nu = 0.33$, compute $G$, $K$, $V_s$ and $V_p$ with units, and report
$V_p/V_s$.

**★2.** For the ice of ★1, compute the tangent sensitivity
$\mathrm{d}V_s/\mathrm{d}T = -\gamma_T V_s/2$ with
$\gamma_T = 3.0\times10^{-3}\ \mathrm{K^{-1}}$, and the fractional velocity change
over a $30\ \mathrm{K}$ warming.

**★3.** State the two limiting values of the fundamental Love-mode phase velocity
for a slow layer over a fast half-space, with the physical reason for each, and
say which limit a $0.2\ \mathrm{Hz}$ measurement on a $1000\ \mathrm{m}$ thick
sheet is closest to.

## ★★ Applied

**★★4.** For a layer with $\beta_1 = 1800\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$,
$\rho_1 = 917\ \mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$ over a half-space with
$\beta_2 = 2800\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$, $\rho_2 = 2600\ \mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$,
compute the modulus ratio $\mu_2/\mu_1$, the admissible range of $c$, and the
wavelengths (in units of $H$) at $1\ \mathrm{Hz}$ if $c = 2000\
\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$ and $H = 800\ \mathrm{m}$.

**★★5.** A dispersion curve is inverted for $(H, \beta_1)$ with the substrate
fixed. The accepted family is: $H = 950$ to $1050\ \mathrm{m}$ at
$\beta_1 = 1900\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$, and no accepted model for
$\beta_1 = 1805$ or $1995\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$. **(a)** State what this says about
the trade-off. **(b)** The substrate velocity is then allowed to vary by $\pm 5\%$;
explain in two sentences why the accepted family must widen, and what assumption
about the substrate was doing the work before.

**★★6.** A vertical S-wave path through a $1200\ \mathrm{m}$ ice column gives a
travel time of $t = 0.6280\ \mathrm{s}$. Compute the implied mean $V_s$. Then
compute the $V_s$ that would explain a $10\ \mathrm{ms}$ shorter time with the
thickness fixed, and state whether that is a plausible temperature effect given
$\mathrm{d}V_s/\mathrm{d}T \approx -2.9\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}\,K^{-1}}$.

## ★★★ Research-oriented

**★★★7.** Design a field experiment to measure both the thickness and the shear
velocity of a grounded ice sheet, with a target accuracy of $\pm 10\ \mathrm{m}$
in thickness and $\pm 1\%$ in shear velocity. Specify the sensor geometry, the
band, the sources (active and passive), the independent constraint you will use,
and the synthetic test you will run before the survey. State which of the two
targets is harder and why.

**★★★8.** The lesson argues that a repeating source turns monitoring into a
differential problem. Construct the quantitative argument: suppose the same
source radiates twice with an identical waveform but the ice column between
source and receiver has warmed by $\Delta T$. Using the numbers of §2 of the
lesson, compute the differential travel time for $\Delta T = 5\ \mathrm{K}$ over
a $900\ \mathrm{m}$ path, and state the timing precision needed to detect a
$1\ \mathrm{K}$ change. Then state the assumption about the source that the whole
argument depends on, and one measurement that would test it.
