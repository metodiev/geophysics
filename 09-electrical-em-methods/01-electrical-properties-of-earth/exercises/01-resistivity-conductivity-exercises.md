# Exercises — 09-01-01 Resistivity and Conductivity: Definitions and Ranges

Attempt these before reading the
[solutions](./01-resistivity-conductivity-solutions.md). The lesson is
[01 Resistivity and Conductivity](../01-resistivity-conductivity.md). All models
are **SYNTHETIC** teaching values.

## ★ Foundational

**★1.** A rock has $\sigma = 2.5\times10^{-3}\ \mathrm{S\,m^{-1}}$. Compute
$\rho$ in $\Omega\,\mathrm{m}$ and place it in the teaching catalogue of the
lesson.

**★2.** A core plug is 5.0 cm long and 2.0 cm in diameter and measures
$2.4\ \mathrm{k}\Omega$. Compute $\rho$ and $\sigma$, showing the units of each
factor.

**★3.** Write the potential of a point current source on a half-space and
evaluate it at $r = 2.5$ m for $\rho = 250\ \Omega\,\mathrm{m}$ and
$I = 0.25\ \mathrm{A}$.

## ★★ Applied

**★★4.** A cylindrical sample measures $R = 1.0\ \mathrm{k}\Omega$ with
$L = 0.08$ m and $r = 0.015$ m. Then the caliper is found to read 0.1 mm too
large on the radius. Compute the correct $\rho$ and the size of the error made
with the wrong radius.

**★★5.** A two-electrode measurement uses hemispherical electrodes of radius
0.03 m on a $50\ \Omega\,\mathrm{m}$ half-space. The contact resistance is
$250\ \Omega$ per electrode. Compute the measured resistance, the apparent
resistivity, and the factor by which it is wrong. Then compute the error if the
electrodes were 0.15 m instead, and state the trend.

**★★6.** A voltmeter with input impedance $10^{7}\ \Omega$ is used across
potential electrodes with a combined contact resistance of $600\ \Omega$ and a
signal of $0.5$ V. Compute the fractional error introduced by the potential
circuit and compare it with the geometric factor error from a 1 cm electrode
mislocation (0.25%).

## ★★★ Research-oriented

**★★★7.** Derive the resistance between two hemispherical electrodes of radii
$a_1$ and $a_2$ separated by a distance $d \gg a$ on a half-space, stating where
the superposition approximation fails. Then compute the error of that
approximation for $d = 10 a$.

**★★★8.** A laboratory wants to measure $\rho$ to $1\%$. The plug radius is
known to $0.1\%$ and the length to $0.2\%$, and the resistance is measured to
$0.5\%$. State whether the target is achievable, and what single improvement
would help most. Express the whole budget as a quadrature sum and discuss
whether that combination rule is justified.
