# Exercises — 09-01-05 Temperature, Pressure and Frequency Dependence

Attempt these before reading the
[solutions](./05-temperature-and-pressure-effects-solutions.md). The lesson is
[05 Temperature, Pressure and Frequency Dependence](../05-temperature-and-pressure-effects.md).
All models are **SYNTHETIC** teaching values; $k_B$ and $\varepsilon_0$ are exact
SI-defined constants.

## ★ Foundational

**★1.** Compute $\alpha = E_a/(k_BT^{2})$ at $T = 298.15$ K for
$E_a = 0.15\ \mathrm{eV}$.

**★2.** A sample has $\sigma = 0.05\ \mathrm{S\,m^{-1}}$ at $25\ ^\circ\mathrm{C}$
and $E_a = 0.25\ \mathrm{eV}$. Compute $\sigma$ at $60\ ^\circ\mathrm{C}$ and the
value the linear approximation would give.

**★3.** Compute $\phi(z)$ at 500 m for
$\phi(z) = 0.35\exp(-z/1200\ \mathrm{m})$ and then $F = \phi^{-2}$.

## ★★ Applied

**★★4.** For the model of ★3 with $\sigma_w = 0.15\ \mathrm{S\,m^{-1}}$, compute
the conductivity and resistivity at 0, 500 and 1500 m, and the effective
pressure at each depth with $\rho_b = 2.2\times10^{3}$ and
$\rho_w = 1.15\times10^{3}\ \mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$.

**★★5.** A permafrost survey uses the unfrozen-water model of the lesson
($w = [1+(T_f-T)/1.0]^{-1}$ for $T<0$). Compute the ratio $\sigma(0)/\sigma(-3)$
and compare it with the ratio $\sigma(25)/\sigma(0)$ from the Arrhenius law with
$E_a = 0.20\ \mathrm{eV}$. State which effect is larger over which interval.

**★★6.** Compute the charge-relaxation frequency for $\sigma = 0.02\
\mathrm{S\,m^{-1}}$ and $\varepsilon_r = 12$, and decide whether a 1 kHz
resistivity survey is in the DC regime.

## ★★★ Research-oriented

**★★★7.** A time-lapse survey shows a 15% resistivity decrease in a shallow
aquifer. Build a quantitative argument that separates (i) a $5\ \mathrm{K}$
temperature rise, (ii) a 10% porosity increase and (iii) a factor-1.3 decrease in
$\rho_w$. State which extra measurement would settle it and how the residual
would be reported.

**★★★8.** Design a laboratory experiment that measures the temperature
dependence of both the DC conductivity and the IP relaxation frequency of one
sample, and specify how you would test the claim that they share a single
activation energy.
