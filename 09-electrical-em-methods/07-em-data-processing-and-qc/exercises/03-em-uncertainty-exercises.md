# Exercises — 09-07-03 Quantifying Uncertainty in EM Interpretation

Attempt these before reading the
[solutions](./03-em-uncertainty-solutions.md). The lesson is
[03 Quantifying Uncertainty in EM Interpretation](../03-em-uncertainty.md).
All models are **SYNTHETIC** teaching values.

## ★ Foundational

**★1.** Write down the linearised estimator and its covariance, and state the two
assumptions the covariance needs. For a two-parameter problem, give the units of
$J_{ij}$, $W_{ii}$, $C_{jj}$ and $\sigma_{p_j}$ when the data are apparent
resistivities in $\Omega\,\mathrm{m}$ and the parameters are $(\rho_2, H)$.

**★2.** Using only two datapoints — the 43.29 m and 100 m Wenner spacings of the
lesson with 3% relative errors — compute the $2\times2$ covariance for $(\rho_2,H)$
and the correlation coefficient. State whether the interval on $H$ from two
datapoints is usable.

**★3.** The lesson measures a coverage of 0.945 for $\rho_2$ from 200 realisations
with a nominal interval of 0.95. Compute the binomial standard error and state
whether the difference is significant.

## ★★ Applied

**★4.** The lesson's systematic-error table gives a $\rho_1$ bias of $+0.90\sigma$
at $g = 0$ and $+4.16\sigma$ at $g = 5\%$. Compute the gain error at which the
bias reaches one standard error, and state whether such a gain error would be
visible in the residual of the fit.

**★5.** The lesson reports a Monte Carlo skewness of $+0.386$ for $\rho_2$ and
$+0.189$ for $H$, and an empirical $\rho_2$–$H$ correlation of $+0.7405$ against a
linearised $+0.6920$. Say which of the two statements — the interval or the
correlation — is better approximated by the linear theory, and what you would
report to a reader.

**★6.** A sounding restricted to $a\le20$ m admits $\rho_2$ values from 186.8 to
1500 $\Omega\,\mathrm{m}$ at 95%, over which the best-fit $H$ slides from 6.496 to
8.773 m. Compute the ratio of the extremes for both parameters and state how you
would report the result in one sentence.

## ★★★ Research-oriented

**★★★7.** Design a synthetic study that estimates the *model* error incurred by
inverting a two-layer model for data generated by a three-layer earth. Specify
the forward model, the inversion, the statistic and the decision rule, and state
what the study cannot tell you. A specification that could not be executed is a
failed answer.

**★★★8.** The lesson's covariance is calibrated (coverage 0.945–0.970) because
the data were generated by the same two-layer model that is inverted. Design a
study that measures the calibration of the *whole uncertainty budget* — noise plus
systematic plus model error — and state the coverage you would require before
publishing a quoted interval.
