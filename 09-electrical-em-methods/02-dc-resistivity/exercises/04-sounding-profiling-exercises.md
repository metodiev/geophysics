# Exercises — 09-02-04 Vertical Electrical Sounding vs Profiling

Attempt these before reading the
[solutions](./04-sounding-profiling-solutions.md). The lesson is
[04 Vertical Electrical Sounding vs Profiling](../04-sounding-profiling.md).
All models are **SYNTHETIC** teaching values.

## ★ Foundational

**★1.** State, in one sentence each, what a sounding resolves and what a profile
resolves, and name the quantity that limits each.

**★2.** From the four-layer aquifer sounding of the lesson, state the spacing
you would use to (a) detect the aquifer, (b) estimate its resistivity, and
(c) test for the bedrock.

**★3.** Compute the 2% noise level on $\rho_a$ at $a = 20$ m in the lesson's
aquifer model, given $\rho_a = 108.2\ \Omega\,\mathrm{m}$.

## ★★ Applied

**★★4.** The lesson's thickness-resolution table gives $\rho_a = 113.32$ and
$115.68\ \Omega\,\mathrm{m}$ for 2 m and 4 m aquifers at $a = 12$ m. Compute the
percentage difference and decide whether a 5% voltage error would resolve the
two.

**★★5.** The footprint of a Wenner array with $a = 5$ m has a 10–90% width of
18.2 m. Estimate the width for $a = 11.3$ m, and the lateral resolution it
implies at the corresponding DOI.

**★★6.** From the dipping-contact table, compute the ratio of the drop in
$\rho_a$ at $x = 100$ m between $a = 2$ m and $a = 10$ m. Explain the direction
of the difference using the depth sensitivity of the two arrays.

## ★★★ Research-oriented

**★★★7.** The locally 1-D profiling approximation ignores lateral current
channelling. For a 10:1 conductivity contrast, argue (with numbers from the
lesson and from [02](02-electrode-arrays-solutions.md)) whether the edge position
or the amplitude is the more trustworthy output of the approximation.

**★★★8.** Design a three-spacing traverse that distinguishes a dipping contact
from a lateral change in layer thickness, and specify the quantitative
discriminator you would compute from the three profiles.
