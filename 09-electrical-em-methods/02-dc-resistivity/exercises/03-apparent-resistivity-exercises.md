# Exercises — 09-02-03 Apparent Resistivity and Geometric Factors

Attempt these before reading the
[solutions](./03-apparent-resistivity-solutions.md). The lesson is
[03 Apparent Resistivity and Geometric Factors](../03-apparent-resistivity.md).
All models are **SYNTHETIC** teaching values.

## ★ Foundational

**★1.** A two-layer model has $\rho_1 = 150\ \Omega\,\mathrm{m}$,
$h_1 = 8$ m, $\rho_2 = 30\ \Omega\,\mathrm{m}$. State the limits of the Wenner
sounding at very small and very large spacing.

**★2.** Compute $\Delta V$ for a Wenner array with $a = 30$ m reading
$\rho_a = 45\ \Omega\,\mathrm{m}$ at $I = 0.2\ \mathrm{A}$.

**★3.** Compute $T$ and $S$ for a layer with $\rho = 400\
\Omega\,\mathrm{m}$ and $h = 3$ m, with units.

## ★★ Applied

**★★4.** A thin resistive layer is known to have $T = 1200\
\Omega\,\mathrm{m^{2}}$. Compute the thickness and resistivity of two members of
its equivalence family, and state the range admitted if the curve is measured to
2%.

**★★5.** From the lesson's two-layer table, estimate the spacing at which the
conductive case ($\rho_2 = 10\ \Omega\,\mathrm{m}$) is halfway between 100 and
10 $\Omega\,\mathrm{m}$ on a log scale, and compare it with $h_1$.

**★★6.** A sounding gives $\rho_a = 100.27\ \Omega\,\mathrm{m}$ at $a = 2$ m and
$111.67$ at $a = 8$ m for an unknown two-layer model. Using the geometric-mean
rule of thumb, estimate $\rho_2$; then explain why the estimate is unreliable by
computing the true value (1000 $\Omega\,\mathrm{m}$) and the spacing at which the
geometric mean actually occurs (about 75 m).

## ★★★ Research-oriented

**★★★7.** Derive the asymptotic behaviour of a Wenner sounding over a two-layer
model for $a\ll h$ to first order in $(a/h)^{2}$ or explain why the coefficient
cannot be obtained without more work, then use the lesson's table to estimate
the exponent numerically from the $a = 0.5,1,2$ rows.

**★★★8.** Design a field programme that determines the transverse resistance of
a thin resistive layer to 5% and its depth to 1 m, given 2% voltage noise, an
arbitrary current, and the equivalence relations of the lesson. State explicitly
what remains unresolved after the programme.
