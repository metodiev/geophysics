# Exercises — 09-02-08 ERT Applications: Aquifers, Contamination, Landslides, Archaeology

Attempt these before reading the
[solutions](./08-ert-applications-solutions.md). The lesson is
[08 ERT Applications](../08-ert-applications.md). All models are **SYNTHETIC**
teaching values.

## ★ Foundational

**★1.** From the lesson's archetype table, state the apparent-resistivity
minimum of the saline-intrusion model and the spacing at which it occurs.

**★2.** Compute the factor by which the aquifer thickness is uncertain, using
the admitted range 4.0–13.0 m about a true 10.0 m.

**★3.** A wall of $600\ \Omega\,\mathrm{m}$ in $60\ \Omega\,\mathrm{m}$ soil
gives $\rho_a = 87.64\ \Omega\,\mathrm{m}$ at $a = 3$ m. Compute the percentage
anomaly and the sign for a *conductive* pit of $15\ \Omega\,\mathrm{m}$ in the
same soil, given the corresponding reading would be about $52\
\Omega\,\mathrm{m}$.

## ★★ Applied

**★★4.** The lesson's monitoring test fails to resolve the depth of a 20%
change at any depth, with $\chi^{2} < 1$ for every pair of tested depths.
Compute the maximum depth separation that would be needed for
$\chi^{2} = 1$ if the patterns decayed as $1/d$ and the patterns at 5 m and 18 m
differ by $3.81\%$ and $2.12\%$ respectively.

**★★5.** A landslide survey must resolve a 1 m slip surface at 12 m depth. Using
the footprint ratio from [05](05-ert-tomography-solutions.md) (width ≈ 2.5$a$
and DOI ≈ 1.79$a$ for $n = 6$), compute the spacing needed for the depth and the
resulting footprint, and state the conclusion.

**★★6.** An archaeological survey has a 4×4 m grid of electrodes at 1 m
spacing. Compute the number of measurements for a full dipole-dipole scan with
$n = 1..3$ along one line, and discuss whether the survey can detect a 0.5 m wide
wall at 1 m depth.

## ★★★ Research-oriented

**★★★7.** Design a monitoring programme for a leaking landfill liner at 6 m
depth, with a required detection limit of a $10\%$ change in a 1 m layer.
Specify the array, spacing, current, stacking, and the noise budget; then state
the single most likely cause of a false positive and how you would test for it.

**★★★8.** Two interpretations of a $-40\%$ anomaly are offered in the lesson (a
3 m water-table rise, or a factor-3 salinity change in a 6 m layer). Construct a
third explanation involving temperature change, quantify it with the numbers of
[09-01-05](../../01-electrical-properties-of-earth/05-temperature-and-pressure-effects.md),
and state the measurement that would separate all three.
