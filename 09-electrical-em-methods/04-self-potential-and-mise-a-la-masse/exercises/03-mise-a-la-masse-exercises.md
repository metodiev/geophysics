# Exercises — 09-04-03 Mise-a-la-Masse and Induced Polarisation Mapping

Attempt these before the [solutions](./03-mise-a-la-masse-solutions.md). Lesson:
[03 Mise-a-la-Masse](../03-mise-a-la-masse.md). All models **SYNTHETIC**.

## ★ Foundational

**★1.** A current electrode of $1.0$ A is at $60$ m depth in a half-space of
$50\ \Omega$ m. Compute the surface potential directly above it and at $100$ m
horizontal offset, using $V = \rho I/(2\pi\sqrt{x^{2}+z^{2}})$.

**★2.** Show that the apparent resistivity
$\rho_a = 2\pi\sqrt{x^{2}+z^{2}}\,V/I$ equals the true resistivity at both stations
of ★1.

**★3.** For the conducting-sphere coefficient
$A = a^{3}E_0(\sigma_1-\sigma_0)/(\sigma_1+2\sigma_0)$, state the values of $A$ in
the limits $\sigma_1 = \sigma_0$ and $\sigma_1 \to \infty$, and what each means
physically.

## ★★ Applied

**★★4.** Derive $A = a^{3}E_0(k-1)/(k+2)$ from the two boundary conditions at
$r = a$, showing each equation and the elimination of the interior coefficient.

**★★5.** Using the lesson's saturation table, explain with numbers why doubling
the conductivity contrast of a well-conducting body changes its anomaly by less
than $3\%$, while the surface array's anomaly continues to grow with contrast.

**★★6.** The lesson's anomaly-versus-contrast table gives $2.011\%$, $2.602\%$ and
$2.673\%$ at contrasts $10$, $100$ and $1000$ for a body at $4.63$ radii. Compute
the fractional change between consecutive entries and state the trend.

## ★★★ Research-oriented

**★★★7.** Design a field procedure that separates a body at $4.63$ radii with
contrast $1.12$ from one at $11.49$ radii with contrast $3.71$, both giving a
$0.10\%$ anomaly. State the electrode geometry, the stations and the statistic.

**★★★8.** The lesson measures the modelled anomaly growing to $7987\%$ as the
source approaches the centre of a body with contrast $100$. Explain why the
number grows, what physical regime replaces the model there, and what measurement
a survey should make instead in that geometry.
