# Exercises — 09-05-04 EM for Geothermal Systems and Permafrost

Attempt these before the
[solutions](./04-em-geothermal-permafrost-solutions.md). Lesson:
[04 EM for Geothermal Systems and Permafrost](../04-em-geothermal-permafrost.md).
All models **SYNTHETIC**.

## ★ Foundational

**★1.** Compute $\sigma(T)$ at $60$ °C for $\sigma_{25} = 0.03$ S/m and
$E_a = 20$ kJ/mol, with $R = 8.314462618$ J/(mol K).

**★2.** From the lesson's frozen-layer table, state the frequency at which a
$5$ m layer produces the largest response change, and the change itself.

**★3.** State in one sentence why an EM survey cannot distinguish heating from
thawing without independent information.

## ★★ Applied

**★★4.** Derive
$\frac{1}{\sigma}\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma}{\mathrm{d}T} = \frac{E_a}{RT^{2}}$ from
the Arrhenius model, and evaluate it at $50$ and $250$ °C for
$E_a = 15$ kJ/mol.

**★★5.** Using the frozen-layer table and a $1\%$ response threshold, estimate
the thinnest detectable layer at $100$ Hz, and state why that differs from the
thickness *resolution*.

**★★6.** Using the clay-cap table, explain with numbers why a thick cap cannot be
separated from a thicker one, and what that implies for an inversion that reports
a cap thickness.

## ★★★ Research-oriented

**★★★7.** A repeat survey resolves a $2\%$ response change in a section at
$100$ °C. Convert it into a temperature change and give the range implied by a
factor-two uncertainty in $E_a$.

**★★★8.** Design a monitoring programme to resolve a $1$ K change at $20$ °C in
a section of $0.05$ S/m. State the band, the repeat interval, the statistic and
the assumption you accept.
