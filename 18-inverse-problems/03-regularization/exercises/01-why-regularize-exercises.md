# Exercises — 18-03-01 Why Regularisation Is Physically and Statistically Justified

Attempt these before reading the
[solutions](./01-why-regularize-solutions.md). The lesson is
[01 Why Regularisation Is Physically and Statistically Justified](../01-why-regularize.md).
All models are **SYNTHETIC** teaching values; the kernel is the exact 2-D
line-mass gravity kernel with $\sigma_1 = 2.8276\times10^{-7}\ \mathrm{mGal}$ per
$(\mathrm{kg\,m^{-1}})$ and $\kappa_2 = 1.163\times10^{6}$.
## ★ Foundational

**★1.** For the synthetic profile the peak anomaly is $221.27\ \mathrm{mGal}$
and the noise is stated as $0.50\%$ of it. Compute the noise rms in mGal and the
single-coefficient noise level $\sqrt{\mathbb E[(u_i^{\mathsf T}e)^2]}$ for
orthonormal $u_i$, and explain why the two numbers are equal.

**★2.** Using $\kappa_2 = 1.163\times10^{6}$, the noise from ★1,
$\|d\| = 969.5\ \mathrm{mGal}$ and $n = 64$, evaluate both worst-case bounds
$\kappa_2\sigma_e/\|d\|$ and $\kappa_2\|e\|/\|d\|$ with
$\|e\| = \sigma_e\sqrt n$, and state in one sentence what neither bound claims.

**★3.** The fitted decay is $\sigma_i \sim i^{-q}$ with $q = 3.24$ for a
$0.6\ \mathrm{km}$ target and $q = 1.64$ for a $0.3\ \mathrm{km}$ target at the
same station spacing. Compute $\sigma_{32}/\sigma_1$ for both, and say which
target is better resolved.

## ★★ Applied

**★★4.** The demonstration measures a mean relative model error of
$2.231\times10^{3}$ over 200 realisations and an exact-data error of
$1.316\times10^{-10}$. Compute the ratio of the two and explain, in terms of the
singular-value spectrum, why the ratio is so large.

**★★5.** The Picard table shows 3 of 64 exact coefficients above ten times the
noise level, with the highest at $i = 5$; the truncated-SVD optimum is rank 7
with relative error $7.363\times10^{-3}$. Explain why the two indices differ, and
compute what the error would be if the rank were chosen as 5 by mistake (read it
from the demonstration's rank table for $k = 4$ and $k = 8$ and interpolate).

**★★6.** A colleague proposes to "beat the conditioning" by refining the grid to
256 cells with the same 64 stations. Predict the sign and approximate size of the
effect on $\kappa_2$ and on the unregularised error, and state whether the
regularised error would change.

## ★★★ Research-oriented

**★★★7.** Design a synthetic experiment that measures how many independent model
coefficients the data support as a function of (i) station spacing and (ii)
target depth, holding the product of the two fixed. State the statistic you will
report, the number of realisations you need for a stated precision, and what
result would falsify the claim that resolution is set by depth.

**★★★8.** The second-kind control uses $A = cI + K$ with $c$ the maximum
absolute row sum of $K$, giving $\kappa_2(A) = 1.944$ and a noisy error of
$1.76\times10^{-2}$ against $1.42\times10^{3}$ for the first-kind operator.
Derive the scaling of the second-kind error with $c$ (keep $c$ symbolic), and
predict numerically what happens when $c$ is reduced by a factor of 100 while the
data are still generated by $A$ itself. What does the answer say about "making a
problem well-posed by adding a constant to the diagonal"?
