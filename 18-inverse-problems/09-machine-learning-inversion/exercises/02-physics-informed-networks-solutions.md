## ★1 — The loss

$$\Phi(\theta) = \frac{1}{N_c}\sum_{i=1}^{N_c}
\left(u_\theta''(z_i) + f(z_i)\right)^2
+ \beta\left(u_\theta(0)^2 + u_\theta(1)^2\right),$$

with collocation points $\{z_i\}$, boundary terms at $z=0,1$, and weight
$\beta = 100$ in the demonstration. The first sum is the PDE residual
($-u''-f = 0$ rearranged), the second the Dirichlet condition.

---

## ★2 — Second derivative

With $a_j = W_{1j}z + b_{1j}$ and $h_j = \tanh a_j$:

$$\frac{\mathrm{d}h_j}{\mathrm{d}z} = W_{1j}\left(1-h_j^2\right), \qquad
\frac{\mathrm{d}^2h_j}{\mathrm{d}z^2}
= W_{1j}^2\left(-2h_j\left(1-h_j^2\right)\right).$$

Since $u = \sum_j W_{2j}h_j + b_2$,

$$\frac{\mathrm{d}^2u}{\mathrm{d}z^2}
= \sum_{j=1}^{16} W_{2j}\left[-2h_j\left(1-h_j^2\right)W_{1j}^2\right] .$$

No automatic differentiation is required, and the expression is exact for the
network, so the residual is limited only by the representation error of the
network and by round-off.

## ★4 — Two numbers at once

The data-only network has 65 parameters and 5 constraints, so it can interpolate
the five noisy points to machine precision (rms $2.36\times10^{-6}$: it is fitting
the *noise*, not the signal) while wandering arbitrarily between them.

A practitioner who has only the training points would see the spectacular data
rms and conclude the fit is excellent. The L2 error of 0.0626 and the physics
residual of 4.220 are visible only because the exact solution and the equation
are known — i.e. only in a synthetic test. On real data, the missing diagnostic is
the equation: a data-only network has no way to know it is not a solution.

## ★5 — Amplitude ratio for a scaled source

The problem is linear: if $u$ solves $-u'' = f$ with $u(0)=u(1)=0$, then
$\alpha u$ solves $-(\alpha u)'' = \alpha f$ with the same homogeneous boundary
conditions. With $\alpha = 1.20$ the solution of the mis-specified problem is
exactly $1.20\sin(\pi z)$, so the ratio of amplitudes is exactly 1.200 — the
measured value.

General rule: **for a linear equation, a multiplicative error in the source term
(or in a coefficient) maps one-to-one onto a multiplicative error in the
solution**, with no detectable internal signature. Nonlinear equations can
produce more complex biases, but the lesson stands: the residual will not reveal
an error that the equation itself carries.

---

## ★6 — Residual larger than the error

The residual measures $u_\theta'' + f$ pointwise; the error measures
$u_\theta - u_{\text{exact}}$. For a residual of rms $r$ the solution error scales
as $r$ divided by the problem's conditioning (roughly $1/\pi^2$ here for the
lowest mode), so an error of $2.4\times10^{-5}$ is consistent with a residual of
$4.3\times10^{-3}$: the operator amplifies/attenuates the residual by the inverse
of the differential operator's scale.

Consequence: the physics residual is an **unconverted** quantity. It cannot be
read as an error estimate without the conditioning of the operator, and in
problems whose low-order modes are weakly constrained the residual can look
excellent while the solution error is large (and vice versa). Report the residual
*and* an error estimate obtained by another route.

## ★7 — Detecting a mis-specified source term

Held-out data: measurements of $u$ at points **not** used in training, acquired
with a method independent of $f$ (or a repeat measurement at a perturbed location).

Diagnostic: the standardised residual of the PINN solution against the held-out
data,

$$Z = \frac{1}{\sqrt{m}}\left\|\frac{\mathbf{u}^{\text{obs}} -
u_\theta(\mathbf{z}^{\text{obs}})}{\sigma_{\text{obs}}}\right\| .$$

Decision rule: for a correct source term and correct noise, $Z \approx 1$. If the
source term is mis-specified, the solution is biased and $Z$ grows with the number
of held-out points; a $Z$ far above 1 (or a consistent, non-random sign pattern in
the residuals) is evidence of mis-specification. Note that the check requires
observations that were not generated by the assumed equation — synthetic
self-consistency cannot detect it.

## ★8 — PINN versus PDE-constrained least squares

| aspect | PINN | PDE-constrained least squares |
|---|---|---|
| information used | PDE residual + boundary/observation terms | PDE (or its discretisation) + observation term + prior |
| cost per new data set | retraining (no warm start guarantee) | one linearised solve per iteration, warm-startable |
| guarantees | approximation-theoretic only; no error bounds in general | for linear problems: convexity, resolution and posterior covariance available |
| preferable when | the geometry is complex, the unknown is a field, and mesh generation is the bottleneck | the problem is smooth/rectangular, the operator is linear, and uncertainty quantification is required |

Neither is unconditionally better; the second offers the uncertainty machinery
this folder insists on, the first offers flexibility of geometry and
differentiability of the whole pipeline.
