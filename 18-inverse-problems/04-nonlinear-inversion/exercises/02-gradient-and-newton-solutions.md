# Solutions — Gradient, Gauss-Newton and Full Newton Methods

**1.1** $\kappa = 100/1 = 100$, so the rate is $1 - 2/101 = 0.980198$. The demo measured 0.989999 for its own quadratic (whose $\kappa$ was 100 after scaling by the step), i.e. the same order of magnitude; both numbers say that each iteration removes only about 2% of the error.

**1.2** $J^{\mathsf T}J$ is symmetric positive **semi**definite for any $J$, because $x^{\mathsf T}J^{\mathsf T}Jx = \lVert Jx\rVert^2 \ge 0$. The true Hessian $\nabla^2 f$ of a nonlinear least-squares objective contains the residual-weighted curvature term $\sum_i r_i\nabla^2 r_i$ and can be indefinite, as the lesson measured at $(0.2,2.0)$.

**1.3** Starting from an error $e_0$ and needing $e_n \le 10^{-6}e_0$: $0.98020^{n} \le 10^{-6}$ gives $n \ge 6\ln 10 / \ln(1/0.98020) = 13.8155/0.019998 = 690.8$. The measured 1287 is within a factor of two of that bound and on the same scale; the difference is the constant in front of the rate and the fact that the bound is asymptotic.

**2.1** $\Delta = -(J^{\mathsf T}J + \lambda I)^{-1}J^{\mathsf T}r$. As $\lambda \to \infty$, $(J^{\mathsf T}J + \lambda I)^{-1} \to \lambda^{-1}I$, so the step becomes $\Delta \to -\lambda^{-1}J^{\mathsf T}r$, i.e. a short step in the steepest-descent direction (since $\nabla f = -J^{\mathsf T}r$). LM therefore interpolates between Gauss–Newton at $\lambda = 0$ and steepest descent at large $\lambda$.

**2.2** $348/5 = 69.6$. The second-order method needed about 70 times fewer iterations: the second-order information removes the conditioning penalty that the first-order method pays, exactly as the linear analysis predicts.

**2.3** $H = J^{\mathsf T}J + \sum_i r_i\nabla^2 r_i$. The first term is always PSD; the second can be negative where the residuals are large and the forward operator has the wrong curvature. At $(0.2, 2.0)$ the negative term exceeded the positive one in one eigendirection, giving $\lambda_{\min}(H) = -0.17826$, about 73 times larger in magnitude than the positive $\lambda_{\min}(J^{\mathsf T}J) = 2.457\times10^{-3}$. A Gauss–Newton step is therefore still a descent direction there while a full Newton step is not, which is why the lesson's full-Newton run needed damping.

**3.1** Use $\lambda_k$ chosen so that the step satisfies $\lVert\Delta\rVert \le \Delta_k$: solve $(J^{\mathsf T}J + \lambda I)\Delta = -J^{\mathsf T}r$ for a sequence of $\lambda$ until the constraint is met, then accept the step and update $\Delta_k$ by the measured ratio of actual to predicted reduction. Both schemes reach the same minimum on the demo's smooth objective; the trust-region version takes more evaluations per iteration but fewer iterations when the objective is far from quadratic, which is the trade the measurement exposes.

**3.2** The failure set is $\{u : \text{some column of } J(u) \text{ is numerically zero}\}$, i.e. where the data are insensitive to a parameter. For the Gaussian-well objective those are the points far from every well, where all sensitivities vanish exponentially; the demo's $(1000,1000)$ is such a point. Detect them by computing the column norms of $J$ and the smallest singular value of $J$ (or the diagonal of $J^{\mathsf T}J$) and refusing to step when $\sigma_{\min}/\sigma_{\max}$ falls below a declared threshold.

**3.3** For: it is cheap, PSD by construction, and equals the exact Hessian when the residuals are small, which is the asymptotic regime near a good fit. Against: it omits the residual-curvature term, so it can be optimistic where the fit is poor, and it cannot detect a saddle because it is PSD. Two measured numbers frame the choice: $\lambda_{\min}(J^{\mathsf T}J) = 2.457\times10^{-3}$ against $\lambda_{\min}(H) = -0.17826$ at the same point — a sign difference that changes the conclusion from "minimum" to "not a minimum" — and the fact that the GN Hessian is PSD while the true Hessian is not. The defensible practice is to report $J^{\mathsf T}J$ as a covariance proxy only where the residuals are small, and to check the true Hessian's smallest eigenvalue when the classification matters.
