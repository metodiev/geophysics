# Project: Joint Gravity-Seismic Inversion

## Learning objectives

By the end of this project you will be able to:

1. **Build** two kernels for one model — a gravity kernel linear in density and a
   travel-time kernel linearised through a petrophysical relation — and **state**
   the unit of each.
2. **Invert** the two datasets separately and jointly with a regularisation
   parameter chosen by the discrepancy principle, and **report** the misfit,
   uncertainty, model error and resolution trace of each.
3. **Measure** what the second dataset adds: the change in the resolution trace
   and in the mean model error.
4. **Demonstrate** that a *rescaled* petrophysical link is undetectable by the
   data while a *sign-reversed* link is detected, and **explain** why.
5. **Diagnose** the four failure modes of a joint inversion: an unstated link, a
   wrong link absorbed into the model, an over-weighted dataset, and coupled noise
   between the datasets.
6. **Report** a joint inversion with both kernels, both weightings, the link, and
   the resolution of each dataset alone and together.
7. **Judge** whether a joint inversion is worth its cost, from the measured
   resolution gain.

The model and both datasets are **SYNTHETIC**, generated by
[`code/joint_inversion_demo.py`](./code/joint_inversion_demo.py). No field survey,
no seismic line and no borehole is used.

## 1. The question

*Does adding a second dataset actually resolve anything, and how would you know if
the link between them were wrong?*

Joint inversion is often justified by appeal to "complementary sensitivities",
which is a claim about the *kernels* and can be measured. The demonstration does
so: gravity alone resolves the equivalent of $19.23$ of $32$ parameters with a mean
uncertainty of $14.56\ \mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$, and the joint inversion resolves
$19.65$ with $14.23\ \mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$ — a gain of $1.02$ in uncertainty for a
24-path seismic dataset. The second question is sharper, and the answer is
uncomfortable: **a petrophysical link that is wrong by a factor of two changes the
model by $268\ \mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$ while leaving the misfit unchanged** at
$0.746$ per datum.

## 2. The model and the data

**Synthetic model (SYNTHETIC).** $8$ blocks of $6.25\ \mathrm{km}$ width and
$4$ depth layers with boundaries at 0, 1, 3, 6 and 10 km — $32$ density
parameters — with $+250\ \mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$ in blocks 2–4 of layer 1 and
$-200\ \mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$ in blocks 4–5 of layer 3.

**Gravity (SYNTHETIC).** 20 stations at 2.5 km spacing, $\sigma = 0.05\
\mathrm{mGal}$; data from $-13.833$ to $+7.470\ \mathrm{mGal}$.

**Travel times (SYNTHETIC).** 24 paths with varied source–receiver geometry,
$\sigma = 0.05\ \mathrm{s}$; residuals from $-1.129$ to $+0.064\ \mathrm{s}$.

**The link.** $v = 4500 + 0.40(\rho - 2500)\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$, so
$\partial t/\partial\rho_i = -\ell_i\beta/v_i^{2}$ with $\ell_i$ the path length in
block $i$.

**Provenance.** SYNTHETIC, with the link chosen exactly so that the effect of
changing it can be measured.

## 3. The project specification

**Steps.**

1. Build the gravity kernel by quadrature and the travel-time kernel from the
   link; report each kernel's condition number and the unit of each datum per unit
   density.
2. Invert each dataset alone and both together, choosing the regularisation
   parameter by the discrepancy principle ($\chi^{2}/n \le 1$).
3. For each inversion report the misfit per datum, the mean parameter uncertainty,
   the mean absolute model error and the trace of the resolution matrix.
4. Recompute the joint inversion with the link doubled and with its sign reversed,
   and report the model change and the misfit for each.
5. Write the report: the kernels, the weights, the link, the three resolution
   statements, and the link-sensitivity test.

**Deliverable.** The kernel table, the three inversions' statistics, the
layer-by-layer model error, and the link test.

**Success criterion.** Both datasets are used with stated weights and noise models;
the link is stated and tested; the resolution is reported for each dataset alone
and jointly; the *undetectability* of a rescaled link is stated.

## 4. The two kernels

$$A^{g}_{ji} = \frac{\partial g_j}{\partial\rho_i}
\qquad (\mathrm{mGal\ per\ kg\,m^{-3}}),
\qquad
A^{t}_{pi} = -\frac{\ell_{pi}\,\beta}{v_i^{2}}
\qquad (\mathrm{s\ per\ kg\,m^{-3}}).$$

The gravity kernel is **smooth and long-wavelength**; the travel-time kernel is
**sparse** (a path crosses only the blocks it passes through) and
**depth-weighted** by $\ell/v^{2}$. Those are complementary sensitivities in the
precise sense that the row spaces differ.

Condition numbers: gravity $72$, seismic $2.3\times10^{51}$ (effectively singular
with 24 paths and 32 parameters), joint $4.2\times10^{6}$. The seismic-alone
problem is under-determined by construction, which is exactly why the joint
problem is the interesting one.

## 5. What the joint inversion buys

| dataset | $\chi^{2}$ per datum | mean 1σ (kg m⁻³) | mean |error| | $\operatorname{tr}R$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| gravity only | $0.9617$ | $14.56$ | $22.36$ | $19.23$ |
| seismic only | $0.8767$ | $56.51$ | $29.08$ | $5.04$ |
| joint | $0.7697$ | $14.23$ | $21.09$ | $19.65$ |

Layer by layer, mean absolute model error:

| layer | gravity only | joint |
|---|---|---|
| 1 (0–1 km) | $13.13$ | $10.91$ |
| 4 (6–10 km) | $19.66$ | $22.23$ |

Three honest observations, none of which is the textbook "joint inversion
improves everything".

1. **The joint gain is small**: $14.23$ against $14.56\ \mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$, a
   factor of $1.02$, and the resolution trace rises from $19.23$ to $19.65$ of
   $32$. With 24 paths at $0.05\ \mathrm{s}$ the travel times are only weakly
   sensitive to density (a $250\ \mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$ anomaly changes $v$ by
   $2\%$, hence a path time by $\sim\!0.18\ \mathrm{s}$), so complementary
   sensitivities do **not** imply a large gain. A joint inversion must be justified
   by the measured gain, not by the argument that the kernels differ.
2. **The deep layer got worse** ($19.66 \to 22.23$). The joint objective reallocates
   the misfit across the model, and with a regularised misfit the improvement in
   one direction can cost another. Reporting only the mean would hide this.
3. **Seismic alone is nearly useless** for this parameterisation: $5.04$ of $32$
   resolved, and a mean uncertainty of $56.51\ \mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$ comparable to
   the anomaly itself. That is not a defect; it is the honest statement that
   travel times constrain $v$, and $v$ maps to density only through an assumed
   link.

## 6. The link: what is detectable and what is not

| link used | mean model change (kg m⁻³) | $\chi^{2}$ per datum |
|---|---|---|
| correct ($\beta = 0.40$) | $0.00$ | $0.7697$ |
| doubled ($\beta = 0.80$) | $268.0$ | $0.7462$ |
| sign reversed ($\beta = -0.40$) | $40.0$ | $6262.7$ |

**A rescaled link is invisible.** Doubling $\beta$ changes the model by
$268\ \mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$ — more than the anomaly itself — at an essentially
unchanged misfit ($0.746$ against $0.770$), because the inversion simply halves the
density it puts in each block: the product $\beta\,\rho$ is what enters the travel
time, so only the product is determined. This is the **central subtlety** of
petrophysically coupled inversion: the data constrain the product of the density
and the link's slope, and an error in one is exactly compensated by the other.

**A sign-reversed link is detected immediately**, with a misfit of $6263$ per
datum, because no rescaling of a positive-definite density model can reproduce a
travel time that moves the wrong way. So the data *can* falsify a link that is
inconsistent with the physics, and can never confirm one that is merely mis-scaled.

The practical consequences:

1. **Calibrate the link independently** — from well logs, from laboratory
   measurements, or by inverting for $\beta$ alongside the model, which requires
   the link to be *non-linear* or the density to be bounded, otherwise $\beta$ and
   $\rho$ remain degenerate.
2. **Prefer structural coupling** (cross-gradient or a mutual-information-style
   constraint), which asserts only that boundaries coincide and does not introduce
   a scaling degeneracy.
3. **Report the link and the test** of §6 in every joint-inversion paper; a
   rescaled link that has never been tested is a hidden assumption with the
   authority of a measurement.

## 7. Assumptions and regime of validity

1. **A linearised travel-time kernel.** Valid for small density perturbations at
   fixed ray paths; ray bending requires an iterative update of $\ell_i$.
2. **A single, correct functional form for the link.** A linear $v(\rho)$ is a
   local approximation; the true relation is non-linear, which is what makes
   joint inversion able to constrain the link at all when the density range is
   wide.
3. **Independent noise between datasets.** Shared structural error (a wrong
   geometry, a common datum) correlates them and invalidates the joint weighting.
4. **Correct relative weights.** The joint solution depends on the weight ratio
   $\sigma_g/\sigma_t$; it must come from the measured noise, and its effect must be
   reported.
5. **One model parameterisation for both.** Different grids require interpolation
   operators, which introduce their own smoothing.

## The honest summary

Two datasets, one model, and a measured answer to "is it worth it". Gravity alone
resolves $19.23$ of $32$ parameters with a mean uncertainty of
$14.56\ \mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$; adding 24 travel-time paths at $0.05\ \mathrm{s}$
raises that to $19.65$ and reduces the mean error from $22.36$ to $21.09\
\mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$ — a gain of about $2\%$, at the cost of a petrophysical link
whose doubling changes the model by $268\ \mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$ at an unchanged
misfit. The transferable result is that **a joint inversion's value is a measured
number, and its link is an assumption that the data can falsify only when it is
qualitatively wrong**; both facts belong in the report, and neither is visible in a
merged density section.

1. Write the two kernels and state the unit of each.
2. State why a rescaled petrophysical link is not detectable from the data.
3. The joint resolution trace is $19.65$ against $19.23$ for gravity alone. State

4. Name the four joint-inversion pitfalls of §7 and the test for each.

## Common pitfalls

1. **Assuming complementary kernels imply a large gain.** Here it is $2\%$.
2. **Assuming a link because it is linear and plausible.** A scaled link is
   invisible.
3. **Weighting the datasets by taste.** The relative weight controls the answer.
4. **Reporting a mean uncertainty and hiding a layer that got worse.** The
   deep layer's error rose.
5. **Treating a joint model as more reliable than either single-physics model.**
   Its reliability is exactly the measured resolution gain.

Joint inversion is standard in exploration and lithospheric imaging, and it is the
subject of the doctoral project

their own regularisation. In professional practice the resolution gain and the link
test are the two tables a reviewer asks for.
