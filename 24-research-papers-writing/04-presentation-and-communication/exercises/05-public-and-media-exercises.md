# Exercises — 24-04-05 Public Engagement, Media and Policy Communication

Attempt these before reading the
[solutions](./05-public-and-media-solutions.md). The lesson is
[05 Public Engagement, Media and Policy Communication](../05-public-and-media.md).
All numbers are **SYNTHETIC** teaching values.

## ★ Foundational

**★1.** A $7200$-word paper becomes a $240$-word release and a $14$-word headline.
Compute the retention of each relative to the paper and to the release.

**★2.** Compute the number of words a $30$-word uncertainty sentence becomes at
headline density, given the release and headline sizes of ★1.

**★3.** State the two operational definitions of spin used in the lesson, and give a
one-sentence example of each.

## ★★ Applied

**★★4.** Six qualifiers are carried through four retelling steps with a per-step loss
of $30\%$. Compute the expected number surviving, the survival probability of one
specific qualifier, and the probability that at least one survives.

**★★5.** A number survives each step with probability $0.75$. Compute its survival
after $3$ steps and the joint probability that both the number and at least one of
five qualifiers (per-step loss $0.40$) survive.

**★★6.** A correction reaches $15\%$ of an audience of $40000$. Compute the number
reached, the number retaining the original claim, and state the implication for
pre-publication checking.

## ★★★ Research-oriented

**★★★7.** Design a study measuring whether putting the qualifier in the same clause
as the number reduces the distortion introduced by retelling. State the manipulation,
the outcome, the retelling protocol, the sample size for a $10$-percentage-point
difference from a $50\%$ distortion base, and the confound of the reteller's writing
skill.

**★★★8.** Specify an audit of press releases in your field against their papers for
the two spin edits. State the corpus, the coding protocol, the agreement statistic,
and the two ways the audit could overstate the problem.
