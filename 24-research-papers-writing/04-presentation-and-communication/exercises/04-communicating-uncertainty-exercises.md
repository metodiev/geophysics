# Exercises — 24-04-04 Communicating Uncertainty to Non-Specialists

Attempt these before reading the
[solutions](./04-communicating-uncertainty-solutions.md). The lesson is
[04 Communicating Uncertainty to Non-Specialists](../04-communicating-uncertainty.md).
All numbers are **SYNTHETIC** teaching values.

## ★ Foundational

**★1.** A quantity is estimated at $4.0$ with a $90\%$ interval $[3.0, 5.0]$. Compute
the interval width in units and as a percentage of the estimate, and state whether
the action threshold of $3.2$ lies inside the interval.

**★2.** Using a normal approximation with the standard error implied by the interval
of ★1, compute the $95\%$ and $99\%$ intervals.

**★3.** Two likelihood phrases span $[66\%, 100\%]$ and $[90\%, 100\%]$. Compute
each width and their ratio.

## ★★ Applied

**★★4.** Losses are $L_{\text{wrong}} = 15$ and $L_{\text{missed}} = 5$. Compute
$p^{*}$, the posterior probability of being above the threshold required to act, and
state whether you would act if $P(\text{above}) = 0.78$.

**★★5.** For the interval of ★1, compute $P(\text{above})$ at thresholds $3.0$,
$3.5$ and $4.0$ using the normal approximation with the implied standard error, and
state how the recommendation changes with losses $(15, 5)$.

**★★6.** A framing experiment reports misinterpretation rates of $12\%$ and $27\%$.
Compute the ratio and the number of additional misreadings in an audience of $500$.

## ★★★ Research-oriented

**★★★7.** Design a study measuring whether a stated loss ratio changes
decision-makers' agreement with a recommendation. State the manipulation, the
outcome, the sample size for a $15$-point difference on a $100$-point agreement
scale with a standard deviation of $20$ points, and the confound that decision-makers
hold their own loss ratios.

**★★★8.** Specify a study estimating the loss ratios that stakeholders actually hold
for a geophysical hazard decision. State the elicitation method, the sample, how you
would check consistency, and why the estimate matters for how uncertainty is
communicated.
