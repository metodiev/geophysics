# Exercises — 24-04-01 Conference Talks: Structure, Pacing, Visuals

Attempt these before reading the
[solutions](./01-conference-talks-solutions.md). The lesson is
[01 Conference Talks](../01-conference-talks.md). All numbers are **SYNTHETIC**
teaching values.

## ★ Foundational

**★1.** You speak at $140$ words per minute and have a $15$-minute slot with
$3$ minutes reserved for questions. Compute your spoken word budget.

**★2.** Allocate the budget of ★1 across motivation ($15\%$), method ($30\%$),
results ($40\%$) and conclusion ($15\%$), in words and in minutes.

**★3.** At $1.5$ minutes per slide, compute the slide budget for a $10$-minute talk
and the spoken words per slide at $130$ words per minute.

## ★★ Applied

**★★4.** A talk delivers ideas at $10$ seconds each for $12$ minutes. Compute the
number of ideas delivered and the number retained under an audience capacity of
$4$ ideas. Then compute the number of ideas that would give full retention.

**★★5.** You discover in rehearsal that the talk runs $90$ seconds over. Compute the
words and slides to cut at $130$ words per minute and $1.5$ minutes per slide, and
state which of the four ideas you would drop.

**★★6.** A deck has $14$ slides with an average of $45$ visible words each. Compute
the visible-word total and the ratio to a $12$-minute spoken budget of $1560$
words, and state what the slide density tells you.

## ★★★ Research-oriented

**★★★7.** Design a study measuring whether the visible-word ceiling changes audience
recall of the primary result. State the manipulation, the outcome, the sample size
for a $12$-percentage-point difference on a $100$-point recall score, and the
confound that the number of slides introduces.

**★★★8.** Specify a study that measures whether a rehearsed overrun plan reduces the
proportion of talks that run late. State the unit, the outcome, the design, and the
selection bias in who volunteers for the rehearsal condition.
