# Exercises — 24-06-01 Publishing Data and Code Alongside Papers

Attempt these before reading the
[solutions](./01-data-publication-solutions.md). The lesson is
[01 Publishing Data and Code](../01-data-publication.md). All numbers are
**SYNTHETIC** teaching values.

## ★ Foundational

**★1.** A deposit reports $9$ of the $12$ metadata fields. Compute its completeness
and the modelled reuse probability $0.05 + 0.70 \times \text{completeness}$.

**★2.** Two deposits hold $20$ files each: one all in preservation-friendly formats,
the other with $5$ files in a proprietary format. Compute the byte share at risk if
each proprietary file is $500\ \mathrm{MB}$ and each friendly file is $50\
\mathrm{MB}$.

**★3.** State the two metadata fields whose absence makes a deposit unusable in
practice, and the specific problem each absence creates.

## ★★ Applied

**★★4.** Mean completeness is $64.1\%$ with a standard deviation of $12.4$ points
across $120$ deposits. Compute the coefficient of variation, and state what fraction
of deposits lies within one standard deviation of the mean under a normal
approximation.

**★★5.** Adding the weakest field raises each deposit's completeness by $8.3$
percentage points. Compute the new completeness, the new reuse probability, and the
percentage increase in reuse for a deposit at the mean.

**★★6.** A corpus has $81.0\%$ of deposits and $84.5\%$ of bytes in
preservation-friendly formats. Compute the share of bytes and of deposits in the
other category, and explain what the difference implies about file sizes.

## ★★★ Research-oriented

**★★★7.** Design a study measuring whether metadata completeness predicts actual
reuse (downloads, citations of the dataset, or documented re-analysis). State the
unit, the outcome, the follow-up window, the sample size for detecting a $0.2$ change
in a reuse indicator with a standard deviation of $0.5$, and the selection bias in
deposits that document themselves well.

**★★★8.** Specify a study of format longevity: how you would measure the fraction of
deposits from a decade ago that are still readable, the sampling, the outcome
definition, and why the result is biased upward by the repositories that still exist.
