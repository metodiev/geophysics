# Exercises — 24-06-04 Citation Practice and Attribution of Data and Software

Attempt these before reading the
[solutions](./04-citation-and-attribution-solutions.md). The lesson is
[04 Citation Practice and Attribution](../04-citation-and-attribution.md). All
numbers are **SYNTHETIC** teaching values.

## ★ Foundational

**★1.** A corpus has $800$ papers, a mean of $25.8$ citations and a median of $12$.
Compute the total citation count under the mean and the mean/median ratio.

**★2.** Six per cent of the papers hold $49.1\%$ of the citations. Compute the share
held by the other $94\%$, and the ratio of the top group's share to its proportional
share.

**★3.** State the difference between citing a dataset and acknowledging it, and which
one enters a citation index.

## ★★ Applied

**★★4.** A fitted complementary cumulative distribution has slope $-0.713$ over the
range $1$ to $661$ citations. Compute the implied exponent $\alpha = -\text{slope} + 1$
and state two reasons the value is not a universal constant.

**★★5.** Of $600$ papers that use a dataset, $252$ cite it formally; of $760$ that use
software, $266$ cite it. Compute both citation rates and both attribution gaps, and
state which gap is larger proportionally.

**★★6.** A paper with $25.8$ citations on average uses three cited software packages
and two uncited ones. Compute the fraction of the paper's dependency-credit that is
invisible to a citation index, and state what the index would report instead.

## ★★★ Research-oriented

**★★★7.** Design a study measuring whether a journal's software-citation requirement
increases the number of software citations per paper. State the unit, the outcome, the
design, the sample size for a difference of $0.5$ software citations per paper with a
standard deviation of $1.2$, and the confound that software citation norms are changing
everywhere.

**★★★8.** Specify an analysis of the citation penalty for software relative to method
papers. State the matched comparison, the outcome, the sampling, and the two reasons
any measured penalty is partly an artefact of how software papers are written.
