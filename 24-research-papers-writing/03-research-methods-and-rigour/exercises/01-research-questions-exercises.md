# Exercises — 24-03-01 Formulating Answerable Research Questions

Attempt these before reading the
[solutions](./01-research-questions-solutions.md). The lesson is
[01 Formulating Answerable Research Questions](../01-research-questions.md). All
rates are **SYNTHETIC** teaching values.

## ★ Foundational

**★1.** A question states the measurable quantity and the comparison but not the
scope or the mechanism. Count the required elements present, and compute the number
of candidate designs under the model $2^{k}$ for $k$ missing elements.

**★2.** Two raters agree on $76$ of $100$ questions. Rater A says "answerable" for
$45$ questions, rater B for $38$. Compute $\mathbb{P}_o$, $\mathbb{P}_e$ and
$\kappa$.

**★3.** A question is missing three of the four elements. Compute the number of
candidate designs and the expected false-start effort at $14$ days per design
resolved.

## ★★ Applied

**★★4.** The demonstration reports a mean of $3.7$ candidate designs per question
and a maximum of $16$. Reconstruct the distribution of missing elements implied by
those numbers, given $400$ questions with $54$ fully specified and $7$ missing all
four.

**★★5.** With $\kappa = 0.4485$ measured between two raters, compute the agreement
rate that would be required to reach $\kappa = 0.70$ at the same chance agreement
$\mathbb{P}_e = 0.5648$.

**★★6.** A reviewer argues that a question stating the mechanism is more scientific
than one stating a comparison. Using the demonstration's element rates, compute
what fraction of questions would be **disqualified** by requiring the mechanism
element, and state why that requirement is the wrong test.

## ★★★ Research-oriented

**★★★7.** Design a study that measures whether a written specificity checklist
raises the proportion of proposals whose primary question states quantity,
comparison and scope. State the unit, the design, the sample size for a $20$
percentage-point improvement from a base of $27\%$ at $80\%$ power, and how you
would keep the coding blind.

**★★★8.** Specify a study that estimates the cost of ambiguity in real projects
rather than in a model: the unit, the outcome, how ambiguity is coded, and the
confound that ambitious questions are both vaguer and more likely to be funded.
