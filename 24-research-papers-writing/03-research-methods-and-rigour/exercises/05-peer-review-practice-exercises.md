# Exercises — 24-03-05 Peer Review: How to Do It Well and Receive It Well

Attempt these before reading the
[solutions](./05-peer-review-practice-solutions.md). The lesson is
[05 Peer Review Practice](../05-peer-review-practice.md). All panel numbers are
**SYNTHETIC** teaching values.

## ★ Foundational

**★1.** Four reviewers score one proposal $4.5$, $3.0$, $5.0$ and $3.5$. Compute
the mean, the standard deviation and the range, and state what the range says about
a single review.

**★2.** A panel's mean square between proposals is $2.856$ and within is $0.420$,
with $k = 4$ reviewers. Compute the intraclass correlation.

**★3.** Write the first two sentences of a review summary for a paper you know, then
state whether you could write the paper's claim in one sentence. If not, name what
stopped you.

## ★★ Applied

**★★4.** With an ICC of $0.592$, compute the reliability of the mean of $k = 2, 4,
8$ reviewers using the Spearman-Brown relation, and state the diminishing return.

**★★5.** A panel uses a mean of four reviewer scores with a funding fraction of
$25\%$. Dropping one reviewer shifts the mean by $+0.156$ points but flips no funded
proposal. Explain both facts with one mechanism.

**★★6.** Reviewer-normalised scores change the decile of $6$ of $40$ proposals and
leave the reviewer means exactly zero. State what normalisation removed and what it
left, and give an example of a disagreement it cannot address.

## ★★★ Research-oriented

**★★★7.** Design a study measuring whether structured review reports (summary,
major, minor, recommendation) reduce the number of revision rounds. State the unit,
the design, the sample size for a difference of $0.7$ rounds with a standard
deviation of $1.0$ round, and the confound that expert reviewers both write
structured reports and give clearer first-round feedback.

**★★★8.** Specify a study estimating the ICC of a journal's scores across proposals
and reviewers. State the model, the handling of missing reviews, the minimum number
of proposals for a useful estimate, and why ICC alone does not justify using scores
for decisions.
