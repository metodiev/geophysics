# Exercises — 24-02-04 Writing Results and Discussion Without Overclaiming

Attempt these before reading the
[solutions](./04-writing-results-discussion-solutions.md). The lesson is
[04 Writing Results and Discussion](../04-writing-results-discussion.md). All
numbers are **SYNTHETIC**.

## ★ Foundational

**★1.** A two-group comparison with $n = 200$ per group reports $d = 0.20$ with a
standard error of $0.098$. Compute the $95\%$ confidence interval for $d$ and state
what the interval does and does not say.

**★2.** Compute the family-wise error rate for $20$ independent null tests at
$\alpha = 0.05$ exactly (not by simulation) and compare it with the demonstration's
simulated $63.6\%$.

**★3.** Compute the Bonferroni threshold for $20$ tests at $\alpha = 0.05$ and the
expected number of significant results per experiment before any correction.

## ★★ Applied

**★★4.** Using the normal approximation, compute the power of a two-group test at
$n = 200$ per group for a true effect of $d = 0.20$ and $\alpha = 0.05$. Compare
with the demonstration's simulated $49.9\%$.

**★★5.** For the $n = 20$ row of the winner's-curse table (declared significant
studies have mean $d = 0.752$ against a true $0.20$), compute the expected
shortfall of an independent replication as a percentage of the published value.

**★★6.** Compute the number of independent null tests at which the family-wise error
rate first exceeds $50\%$. State the implication for a paper reporting a single
significant result from an unspecified number of tests.

## ★★★ Research-oriented

**★★★7.** Design an experiment measuring whether reporting an interval changes how
readers interpret a marginally significant result. State the manipulation, the
outcome, the sample size for a $15$-point difference on a $100$-point scale with a
between-subject standard deviation of $22$ points, and the confound that statistical
training introduces.

**★★★8.** Specify a study of the winner's curse in your subfield: define the unit,
the comparison (published effects versus pre-registered or registered-report
effects), the sampling, and the selection bias you cannot remove.
