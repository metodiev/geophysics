# Exercises — 24-02-07 Self-Editing, Revision and Response to Reviewers

Attempt these before reading the
[solutions](./07-editing-and-revision-solutions.md). The lesson is
[07 Self-Editing and Revision](../07-editing-and-revision.md). All numbers are
**SYNTHETIC** teaching values.

## ★ Foundational

**★1.** A draft of $6200$ words is cut by $12\%$, then $8\%$, then $5\%$ of the
remaining words. Compute the word count after each pass and the total reduction as a
percentage of the draft.

**★2.** Each pass takes $3\ \mathrm{h}$. Compute the words removed per hour for each
pass using the counts of ★1.

**★3.** A draft of $4000$ tokens and its final version have a sequence similarity of
$0.930$. Compute the fraction of the sequence changed and the length change if the
final has $3880$ tokens.

## ★★ Applied

**★★4.** A response letter addresses $7$ of $12$ reviewer points. Compute the
coverage, the response length at $180$ words per point, and the expected number of
points re-raised next round at a re-raise probability of $0.60$.

**★★5.** Continue cutting at $5\%$ per pass from $4769$ words (after three passes).
Compute how many further passes are needed to reach $70\%$ of the original draft.

**★★6.** A revision replaces $20\%$ of words and yields a similarity of $0.7767$.
Treating the relation between replacement rate and similarity as approximately
linear, estimate the replacement rate that would give a similarity of $0.500$, and
state the reason your estimate is only approximate.

## ★★★ Research-oriented

**★★★7.** Design an experiment testing whether a point-by-point response letter
reduces the number of review rounds. State the unit of randomisation, the outcome,
the sample size implication for a difference of $0.8$ rounds with a standard
deviation of $1.1$ rounds, and the confound that heavier revisions also need more
rounds.

**★★★8.** Specify an analysis of abstract drift: comparing the abstract of the
submitted version of a paper with the published version. State the sampling, the
unit, the coding of drift, the statistic, and what a measurable rate of drift would
justify procedurally.
