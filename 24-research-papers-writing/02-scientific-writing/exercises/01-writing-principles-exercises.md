# Exercises — 24-02-01 Principles of Clear Scientific Writing

Attempt these before reading the
[solutions](./01-writing-principles-solutions.md). The lesson is
[01 Principles of Clear Scientific Writing](../01-writing-principles.md). All
style numbers are **SYNTHETIC** teaching values.

## ★ Foundational

**★1.** Ten consecutive sentences have lengths $12, 18, 22, 27, 35, 48, 61, 9,
31, 15$ words. Compute the mean, the median, the fraction of sentences longer than
$30$ words, and the fraction of all words that live in those sentences.

**★2.** Using the Flesch reading-ease index, compute the score for a sentence of
$40$ words at $1.6$ syllables per word and for one of $20$ words at the same
syllable rate. State the score difference attributable to the $20$ words.

**★3.** A paragraph of $240$ words contains $12$ sentences, $2$ passives and $4$
nominalisations. Compute passives per $100$ sentences and nominalisations per $100$
words, and say whether each exceeds the demonstration's measured targets of
$26$ per $100$ sentences and $5.7$ per $100$ words.

## ★★ Applied

**★★4.** The demonstration's passages score $51.8$ on average at a syllable rate of
$1.545$. Holding syllables per word fixed, compute the average number of words that
must be removed from every sentence to raise the mean score to $60.0$.

**★★5.** The demonstration splits every sentence longer than $30$ words into two
halves, taking $500$ sentences to $619$ and the mean length from $24.0$ to $19.4$
words with a total of $12005$ words. Verify both the sentence count and the mean
length from the total, and state the number of sentences longer than $30$ words
that remain.

**★★6.** A journal counts words and your abstract is $297$ words in $6$ sentences,
against a limit of $250$. Compute the excess, the mean sentence length, and how
many sentences you would have to delete at the mean length. Then state which of the
six content sentences of the lesson you would delete and why.

## ★★★ Research-oriented

**★★★7.** Design an experiment that tests whether given-before-new ordering
improves comprehension of a technical paragraph. State the manipulation, the
outcome measure, the control paragraph, the sample size justification for a
$10$-point difference on a $100$-point comprehension scale with a within-subject
standard deviation of $18$ points, and the confound that prior familiarity
introduces.

**★★★8.** Specify a corpus study of passive voice in abstracts of your subfield:
the sampling frame, the coding unit, the coding protocol, the agreement statistic
with its target, and how you would separate journal house style from field
practice. Include how many abstracts a $\pm 0.05$ precision target requires for an
expected passive rate of $0.25$.
