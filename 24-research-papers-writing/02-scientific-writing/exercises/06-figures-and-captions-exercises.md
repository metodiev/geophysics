# Exercises — 24-02-06 Figures, Captions and Self-Contained Visual Arguments

Attempt these before reading the
[solutions](./06-figures-and-captions-solutions.md). The lesson is
[06 Figures and Captions](../06-figures-and-captions.md). All rates and ink values
are **SYNTHETIC** teaching values.

## ★ Foundational

**★1.** A caption names the variable, states the unit and identifies the panels but
gives no sample size. Compute its completeness score and state whether the figure
passes the separated-figure test.

**★2.** A chart uses $1.10$ units of total ink of which $0.40$ encodes data. Compute
the data-ink ratio and compare it with the demonstration's bar chart ($0.62$) and dot
plot ($0.82$).

**★3.** A figure has four series distinguished by colour only. State how many series
remain distinguishable in greyscale and name the remedy.

## ★★ Applied

**★★4.** The demonstration's element rates are $0.888$ (variable), $0.675$ (unit),
$0.729$ (panels) and $0.500$ (sample or statistic). Compute the independence
prediction of the standalone rate, compare it with the measured $22.1\%$, and state
the sign and likely cause of the difference.

**★★5.** A colour scale has $12$ levels of which $6$ are reliably distinguishable.
Compute the fraction of the scale that carries distinct information, and the number
of levels to remove to make every level distinguishable.

**★★6.** Replacing five pie charts (total ink $1.20$, data ink $0.35$ each) with five
dot plots (total ink $0.55$, data ink $0.45$ each) changes the ink. Compute the
total ink before and after, the saving in absolute and percentage terms, and the
change in data-ink ratio.

## ★★★ Research-oriented

**★★★7.** Design a reader study measuring whether a caption containing the
comparison sentence improves a reader's ability to state what a figure shows. State
the design, the outcome, the sample size for a $15$-point difference on a
$100$-point scale with a between-subject standard deviation of $23$, and the
blinding.

**★★★8.** Specify an accessibility audit of the figures in a journal's recent
volume: the sampling, the coding protocol for colour and greyscale dependence, the
agreement statistic, and the metric you would report to an editor.
