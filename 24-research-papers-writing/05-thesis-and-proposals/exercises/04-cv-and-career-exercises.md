# Exercises — 24-05-04 Academic CVs, Research Statements and Career Paths

Attempt these before reading the
[solutions](./04-cv-and-career-solutions.md). The lesson is
[04 Academic CVs, Research Statements and Career Paths](../04-cv-and-career.md). All
numbers are **SYNTHETIC** teaching values.

## ★ Foundational

**★1.** A record has $60$ papers, $2030$ citations, a mean of $33.8$ and a median of
$16$. Compute the mean/median ratio and state what it indicates.

**★2.** Six papers hold $35.3\%$ of the citations. Compute the share held by the other
$54$ papers, and the ratio of the top-six share to their proportional share
($10\%$).

**★3.** Compute a Gini coefficient interpretation: a Gini of $0.562$ means what, in
terms of two randomly chosen papers from the record?

## ★★ Applied

**★★4.** A record has $h = 24$. Compute the new $h$ when (a) the most-cited paper
gains $100$ citations, and (b) each paper is duplicated. Explain the difference from
the definition of $h$.

**★★5.** Metrics grow with career length: $h = 13$ at $10$ years and $30$ at $25$
years for the same publication rate. Compute the ratio of $h$ values and the ratio of
years, and state what the comparison shows.

**★★6.** Two rubrics weight criteria differently, and $7$ of $12$ candidates change
rank between them. Compute the fraction changed, and state whether a high rank
correlation between the rubrics would contradict that figure.

## ★★★ Research-oriented

**★★★7.** Design a study measuring whether reporting a full citation distribution
(rather than a single index) changes reviewers' judgements of candidates. State the
unit, the outcome, the sample size for a $12$-point difference on a $100$-point
assessment scale with a standard deviation of $22$, and the confound of the reader's
own metric familiarity.

**★★★8.** Specify an analysis of the relationship between career-stage-normalised
metrics and later career outcomes. State the outcome, the predictor, the population,
and the two reasons the analysis may be uninformative.
