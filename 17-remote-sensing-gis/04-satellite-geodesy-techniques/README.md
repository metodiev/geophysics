# Module 17-04 — Satellite Geodesy Techniques

**Stages:** advanced → doctoral · **Prerequisites:** module 17-01; the wave
vocabulary of folder 02; module 07-04 for reference frames; basic linear algebra.

This module is about the techniques that turn satellites into geodetic
instruments. It is deliberately not a tour of products: each lesson works out
what the *observation* is, where its signal enters, and which error term limits
it — and then measures that limit in a synthetic experiment.

The chain is built around three questions. **Where am I?** Lesson 01 derives the
pseudorange observation and the least-squares solution, and shows that the
geometry (through the DOP) matters as much as the electronics; lesson 02 shows
what differencing removes and what it leaves, and why the atmosphere is the
limiting error in the vertical. **What is the Earth doing?** Lesson 03 treats
satellite gravimetry as an observation system — orbits, sampling, attenuation and
aliasing — leaving the inversion to module 07-05, and lesson 04 covers the
time-and-length metrology of laser ranging, DORIS and VLBI, which define rather
than merely measure the reference frame.

Two themes recur and they are the point of the module. First, **every technique
here measures a difference**, so common-mode errors cancel and differential ones
do not; the whole error budget follows from that. Second, **non-uniqueness is
geometric**: a constant range bias and a global scale are the same parameter to
within the fractional spread of station radii, a common delay is indistinguishable
from a clock error, and a shallow mass is invisible from orbit. In each case the
lesson measures the correlation that quantifies what the data can separate.

All models and data in this module are **SYNTHETIC**. The constellation geometry,
orbit radius, clocks, noise, total electron content, network, weather, time
series, mass anomalies, error spectrum, stations, baselines, source directions and
series are teaching values generated by the demonstrations. The only external
numbers are stated interface and physical constants: the speed of light, standard
gravity, the Earth radius and rotation rate, the gravitational parameter and $J_2$,
the two civil L-band carrier frequencies, the first-order ionospheric coefficient
$40.308\ \mathrm{m^{3}\,s^{-2}}$, the Newtonian constant, and the standard M2 tidal
period. No real constellation, satellite, station, product, baseline or gravity
field is used anywhere in the module. The inversion of gravity observations into
a spherical-harmonic model is the subject of module 07-05 and is not attempted
here.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this module you will be able to:

1. **Derive** the pseudorange observation equation and the least-squares position
   solution, and **explain** how the DOP converts range noise into position
   scatter.
2. **Compute** the dual-frequency ionosphere-free combination, **verify** that the
   ionospheric term cancels identically, and **quantify** the noise it costs.
3. **Derive** single and double differences and **state** which error terms cancel
   in each, **measuring** the residual that remains.
4. **Integrate** a refractivity profile for the tropospheric delay, **compare** the
   plane-parallel and curved-Earth mapping functions, and **convert** a zenith
   delay error into a height error.
5. **Quantify** the benefit and the failure mode of integer ambiguity resolution,
   and **explain** how a small wrong-fix rate can degrade a solution.
6. **Estimate** a velocity from a position time series and **measure** the bias
   from omitting a seasonal term and the inflation caused by coloured noise.
7. **Compute** orbital geometry, repeat cycles and the aliasing of a periodic mass
   signal, and **state** which depths a satellite gravity mission observes.
8. **Quantify** the non-gravitational acceleration budget and **explain** why the
   accelerometer and orbit determination are part of the measurement.
9. **Convert** a laser round-trip time, a Doppler shift and an interferometric
   delay into ranges and angles, and **measure** the degeneracy between a range
   bias and a global scale.

## Suggested path

**If you are new to satellite geodesy:** lessons 01 and 02 in order, running both
demonstrations. Lesson 02 §3.3 (height error) is the practical payoff.

**If you need orbits for another technique:** 03 §3.1–3.3 (geometry, precession,
repeat cycles) and 04 §3.1 (ranging). Those three sections are the shared
infrastructure of every satellite observation in this curriculum.

**If you work with reference frames:** 04 in full, then module
[07-04](../../07-gravity-geodesy/04-geodesy-reference-frames/README.md). Section
3.3's bias/scale degeneracy is the single most useful thing in the module for
frame work.

**If you are preparing a research project:** 02 §3.5–3.6 (velocity,
noise colour and the seasonal model), 03 §3.4–3.7 (attenuation, drag, aliasing and
the crossover degree), and the ★★★ exercises throughout.

## How to run the demos

Use the project virtual environment, which provides NumPy, SciPy, Matplotlib and
pandas:

```bash
cd 17-remote-sensing-gis/04-satellite-geodesy-techniques/code
/Users/miroslav/Documents/workspace/geophysics/.venv/bin/python gnss_principles_demo.py
/Users/miroslav/Documents/workspace/geophysics/.venv/bin/python gnss_processing_demo.py
/Users/miroslav/Documents/workspace/geophysics/.venv/bin/python satellite_gravimetry_observation_demo.py
/Users/miroslav/Documents/workspace/geophysics/.venv/bin/python satellite_laser_ranging_demo.py
```

Every demo prints labelled results with units, uses a fixed random seed
(`np.random.default_rng(20261014)`), requires no network access and exits 0. Each
verifies at least one of its claims numerically — a Gauss-Newton solution against
a known receiver, the cancellation of the ionospheric term, the amplification of
a zenith delay into a height error, the correlation that makes a scale and a bias
inseparable — so the tables in the lessons are measured output rather than copied
numbers.

## Common pitfalls across this module

1. **Treating a pseudorange as a range.** It carries the clock and the atmosphere.
2. **Estimating a common delay** instead of removing it: it hides in the clock.
3. **Quoting a position without its DOP.** The same noise gives an 82-fold worse
   solution in a clustered constellation.
4. **Believing differencing removes the atmosphere.** Only the common part goes.
5. **Fixing ambiguities without validation.** A 1% wrong-fix rate can make the
   fixed solution worse than the float one.
6. **Using a white-noise error bar on a correlated time series.** The measured
   inflation is a factor of 7.3 in scatter.
7. **Quoting gravity coefficients above the crossover degree as data.**
8. **Estimating a global scale from radial observations**, or any parameter whose
   signature duplicates the error under study.
9. **Forgetting that a frame is a choice**, and that a datum error shifts every
   station without leaving a residual.
