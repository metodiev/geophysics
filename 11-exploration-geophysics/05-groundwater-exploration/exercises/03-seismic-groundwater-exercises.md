# Exercises — 11-05-03 Seismic Refraction and Surface Waves for Aquifers

Attempt these before reading the
[solutions](./03-seismic-groundwater-solutions.md). The lesson is
[03 Seismic Refraction and Surface Waves for Aquifers](../03-seismic-groundwater.md).
All models are **SYNTHETIC** teaching values.

## ★ Foundational

**★1.** A two-layer refraction survey gives $v_1 = 1000\
\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$, $v_2 = 3000\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$ and a crossover at
$x_c = 40\ \mathrm{m}$. Compute the refractor depth.

**★2.** For the same survey, compute the critical angle, $\cos\theta_c$ and the
intercept time $t_i$, then recover the depth from $t_i$ and compare.

**★3.** A dispersion curve gives $v_R = 300\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$ at
$f = 5, 15, 45\ \mathrm{Hz}$. Compute the wavelength and the sampling depth
$\lambda/3$ at each frequency, and state what each frequency can resolve.

## ★★ Applied

**★★4.** Using the lesson's sensitivity relation, compute the depth error from a
$+10\%$ error in $v_1$ for a velocity contrast of (a) $1.5$ and (b) $3.0$. State
which survey you would trust and why.

**★★5.** A water table at $8\ \mathrm{m}$ produces a velocity change from
$1200$ to $1800\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$. Compute the critical angle and the
crossover distance, and explain why this head wave is harder to pick than the
one in ★1.

**★★6.** A $3\ \mathrm{m}$ layer sits at $12\ \mathrm{m}$ depth with a $40\%$
velocity contrast to its surroundings, and the phase velocity at that depth is
$250\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$. Using the $\lambda/3$ sampling rule, compute the
highest frequency needed for the sampling window to be no wider than the layer,
and the frequency at which the window is centred on the layer. State whether a
practical acquisition could reach them.

## ★★★ Research-oriented

**★★★7.** Design a combined refraction and MASW survey to (a) locate a water
table at about $15\ \mathrm{m}$ and (b) map porosity variations between
$5$ and $25\ \mathrm{m}$. Specify the array, the source, the offset range, the
frequency band, and the numerical test you would use to confirm that the water
table is saturation and not a lithological boundary.

**★★★8.** A refraction line gives first arrivals with a $800\
\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$ branch and a $2400\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$ branch, and an
interpreter fits two layers, obtaining a refractor depth of $20\ \mathrm{m}$. A
later borehole logs $800\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$ to $10\ \mathrm{m}$, $1600\
\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$ to $26\ \mathrm{m}$, and $2400\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$ below.
Using the ray geometry, show that a two-layer fit to those same picks gives
$\approx 20\ \mathrm{m}$, and state the bias and one observation that would have
revealed the intermediate layer.
