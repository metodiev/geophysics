# Exercises — 11-04-01 Geophysical Signatures of Geothermal Reservoirs

Attempt these before reading the
[solutions](./01-geothermal-signatures-solutions.md). The lesson is
[01 Geophysical Signatures of Geothermal Reservoirs](../01-geothermal-signatures.md).
All models are **SYNTHETIC** teaching values.

## ★ Foundational

**★1.** With $T_0 = 15$ °C, $k = 2.8\ \mathrm{W\,m^{-1}\,K^{-1}}$,
$q_m = 70\ \mathrm{mW\,m^{-2}}$ and $A = 1.2\ \mathrm{\mu W\,m^{-3}}$, compute
the temperature at 2.5 km (both terms separately) and the surface heat flow for a
10 km thick producing layer.

**★2.** With the same $T_0$ and $k$, compute the heat flow needed for 180 °C at
2.5 km in the conductive, production-free limit.

**★3.** Compute the heat in place of a $1.5\times1.5$ km, 400 m thick reservoir
at 220 °C with a 50 °C reference and $\phi = 0.05$, and the thermal power if all
of it were produced in 25 years.

## ★★ Applied

**★★4.** For $H = 250$ m, $\Delta T = 120$ K,
$k_{\text{therm}} = 2.2\ \mathrm{W\,m^{-1}\,K^{-1}}$,
$\beta = 5\times10^{-4}\ \mathrm{K^{-1}}$,
$\nu = 3\times10^{-7}\ \mathrm{m^{2}\,s^{-1}}$ and
$\rho_w c_w = 4.18\times10^{6}\ \mathrm{J\,m^{-3}\,K^{-1}}$, compute the
permeability required for convection (in m² and darcy) and state the two
assumptions that most affect the answer.

**★★5.** Using the temperature relation of the lesson, compute $\rho_w$ and the
clay-cap resistivity ($\phi = 0.25$, $a = 1$, $m = 2$) at 150 °C, and state the
factor by which temperature alone has reduced the resistivity relative to 25 °C.

**★★6.** A low-density body has $\Delta\rho = -250\ \mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$,
radius 400 m and centre depth 1200 m. Compute the maximum gravity anomaly and
the half-width, then state the depth you would infer from the half-width and the
assumption it carries.

## ★★★ Research-oriented

**★★★7.** A heat-flow survey finds a 60 mW/m² anomaly with a 5 km width.
Enumerate the source strength/depth combinations consistent with the *amplitude*
at 1, 2 and 4 km depth, and state precisely what additional measurement would
resolve them. Compute the amplitude of the additional measurement you propose.

**★★★8.** Design a two-stage screening programme for a frontier geothermal
area: stage 1 must cost one measurement per 100 km² and reject unlikely areas;
stage 2 must test convection potential. State the measurements, the quantities
computed, and the thresholds, and state what the programme cannot rule out.
