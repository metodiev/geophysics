# Exercises — 11-04-02 Magnetotellurics for Geothermal Resource Assessment

Attempt these before reading the
[solutions](./02-mt-for-geothermal-solutions.md). The lesson is
[02 Magnetotellurics for Geothermal Resource Assessment](../02-mt-for-geothermal.md).
All models are **SYNTHETIC** teaching values.

## ★ Foundational

**★1.** Compute the skin depth at 0.05 Hz in a 15 $\Omega$ m rock, showing the
constant and the units.

**★2.** A station measures $|Z| = 0.020\ \Omega$ at 0.5 Hz with a phase of 45°.
Compute the apparent resistivity and state what the 45° phase implies about the
section.

**★3.** A conductive layer is 300 m thick with resistivity
$5\ \Omega$ m. Compute its conductance in siemens and construct two other
(thickness, resistivity) pairs with the same conductance.

## ★★ Applied

**★★4.** A layer of $20\ \Omega$ m is 600 m thick over a $200\ \Omega$ m
half-space. Compute the period at which the skin depth in the layer equals its
thickness, and state what the apparent-resistivity curve and the phase do on
either side of it.

**★★5.** Using the lesson's conductance-equivalence table, state what the data
constrain and what they do not above 10 s period, and explain why the
short-period data behave differently.

**★★6.** A static shift of 2.0 is present. Compute the error in the interpreted
apparent resistivity and in the interpreted cap conductance, and state two ways
of removing the shift, with one sentence each on their limitations.

## ★★★ Research-oriented

**★★★7.** Design the period band for a survey whose target is a cap at 1.5 km
under a 25 $\Omega$ m overburden and a reservoir at 2.5 km. Justify the shortest
and longest useful periods with skin-depth numbers, and state the effect of the
MT dead band on your plan.

**★★★8.** Design an experiment that tests whether two candidate models (a thin
very conductive cap and a thick moderately conductive one) are separable with a
stated noise level. Specify the metric, the bands, the alternative measurement
you would propose if they are not separable, and the experiment's failure mode.
