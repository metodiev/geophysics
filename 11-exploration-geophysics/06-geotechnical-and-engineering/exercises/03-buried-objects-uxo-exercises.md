# Exercises — 11-06-03 Buried Objects, UXO Detection and Clearance

Attempt these before reading the
[solutions](./03-buried-objects-uxo-solutions.md). The lesson is
[03 Buried Objects, UXO Detection and Clearance](../03-buried-objects-uxo.md).
All models are **SYNTHETIC** teaching values.

## ★ Foundational

**★1.** Compute the magnetic moment of a uniformly magnetised sphere of radius
$0.15\ \mathrm{m}$ with $M = 800\ \mathrm{A\,m^{-1}}$.

**★2.** For the object of ★1, compute the vertical field directly above at
$0.5$, $1.0$ and $2.0\ \mathrm{m}$ depth, in nT, using
$B_z = 2\times10^{-7}m/z^{3}$.

**★3.** A GPR hyperbola has an apex time of $12.0\ \mathrm{ns}$ and a velocity
of $0.10\ \mathrm{m\,ns^{-1}}$. Compute the target depth, and the two-way time at
an offset of $0.5\ \mathrm{m}$.

## ★★ Applied

**★★4.** With noise $1.0\ \mathrm{nT}$ and threshold $2.0\ \mathrm{nT}$, compute
the 50% and 90% POD depths for the object of ★1.

**★★5.** A clearance survey has $\mathrm{POD} = 0.97$ at the specified depth.
Compute the probability that a site with 30 objects is fully cleared, and the
expected number of missed objects.

**★★6.** A hyperbola is picked over an aperture of $\pm0.6\ \mathrm{m}$ for a
target whose true depth is $1.0\ \mathrm{m}$ and true velocity
$0.12\ \mathrm{m\,ns^{-1}}$. Using the lesson's aperture result, state the
approximate admitted ranges of $v$ and $z$, and explain why a wide aperture is
preferable when the site allows it.

## ★★★ Research-oriented

**★★★7.** Design a clearance survey for a $20\ \mathrm{ha}$ site: specify the
instrument and its noise, the line spacing, the target size and depth you are
certifying, and the clearance depth with its POD. Then compute the expected
number of digs and the residual miss probability for a stated object density, and
state the two largest uncertainties in the calculation.

**★★★8.** The lesson assumes detections are independent. Describe two physical
processes that correlate misses, show qualitatively how each degrades
$\mathrm{POD}^{n}$, and propose a survey design change that reduces the
correlation.
