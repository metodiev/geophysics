# Exercises — 14-01-05 Groundwater Chemistry and Salinity

Attempt these before reading the
[solutions](./05-groundwater-chemistry-solutions.md). The lesson is
[05 Groundwater Chemistry and Salinity](../05-groundwater-chemistry.md). All
analyses are **SYNTHETIC**.

Equivalent weights (mg meq$^{-1}$): Ca 20.04, Mg 12.15, Na 22.99, K 39.10,
HCO$_3$ 61.02, SO$_4$ 48.03, Cl 35.45. Standard seawater: Cl
$19{,}350\ \mathrm{mg\,L^{-1}}$, TDS $35{,}000\ \mathrm{mg\,L^{-1}}$. Carbonate
constants at $25\ ^\circ\mathrm{C}$: $K_{a2} = 10^{-10.33}$,
$K_{sp}(\text{calcite}) = 10^{-8.48}$. Debye–Hückel: $A = 0.509$, $B = 0.328$ per
Å. Synthetic calibration $\sigma_w = 0.15 \times \mathrm{TDS}$ (g L$^{-1}$) S m$^{-1}$.

## ★ Foundational

**★1.** Convert to meq L$^{-1}$: Ca $80\ \mathrm{mg\,L^{-1}}$,
SO$_4$ $150\ \mathrm{mg\,L^{-1}}$, Na $200\ \mathrm{mg\,L^{-1}}$. Show the factor
of two for sulfate explicitly.

**★2.** A synthetic water has Ca $55$ and Mg $18\ \mathrm{mg\,L^{-1}}$. Compute the
hardness as CaCO$_3$ (a) with the 2.497/4.118 factors and (b) via equivalents and
the $50.04\ \mathrm{mg\,L^{-1}}$ per meq conversion, and state why the two agree.

**★3.** A synthetic coastal sample has $\mathrm{Cl} = 1500\
\mathrm{mg\,L^{-1}}$; the fresh end member has Cl $30\ \mathrm{mg\,L^{-1}}$ and
TDS $400\ \mathrm{mg\,L^{-1}}$. Compute the seawater fraction and the TDS expected
on a conservative mixing line.

## ★★ Applied

**★★4.** For a synthetic analysis Ca 88, Mg 24, Na 120, K 6, HCO$_3$ 260,
SO$_4$ 70, Cl $200\ \mathrm{mg\,L^{-1}}$, compute the meq L$^{-1}$ of each ion and
the charge balance error. Then recompute it assuming SO$_4$ was misreported as
$700\ \mathrm{mg\,L^{-1}}$, and state which of the two analyses you would accept.

**★★5.** For a synthetic water with Ca $80\ \mathrm{mg\,L^{-1}}$, alkalinity
$3.0\ \mathrm{meq\,L^{-1}}$, pH 7.5 and ionic strength
$0.010\ \mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}$, compute $[\mathrm{HCO_3^-}]$,
$[\mathrm{CO_3^{2-}}]$, the activity coefficients, the ion activity product and the
calcite saturation index. State whether calcite can dissolve in this water.

**★★6.** A synthetic clay with exchange capacity $1.5\ \mathrm{meq\,L^{-1}}$
takes up Ca and releases Na. Compute the Ca and Na concentrations before and after
exchange for a mixture at $f_{\text{sw}} = 0.02$ (seawater end members Ca 400, Na
$10\,800\ \mathrm{mg\,L^{-1}}$; fresh end member Ca 42, Na
$45\ \mathrm{mg\,L^{-1}}$), and report both percentage changes.

## ★★★ Research-oriented

**★★★7.** Design a coastal monitoring programme to detect the *onset* of seawater
intrusion, using chloride sampling plus time-lapse ERT. Using the synthetic
calibration and Archie with $\phi = 0.30$, $m = 2$, show the arithmetic for the
bulk-resistivity change produced by a seawater fraction of 0.001, 0.01 and 0.05,
compare those with a stated ERT repeatability of 3%, and specify the sampling
schedule, the electrode configuration change (if any) and the static
heterogeneity that could be mistaken for intrusion. State the detection limit in
mg L$^{-1}$ of chloride and one reason it will be optimistic in the field.

**★★★8.** A bulk resistivity of $60\ \Omega$ m is measured in a sand. Using Archie
with $a = 1$, $n = 2$, $S_w = 1$ and the synthetic $\sigma_w$ calibration, invert
for TDS at $\phi = 0.20, 0.25, 0.30, 0.35$ and 0.40, and also at $\phi = 0.30$ for
$m = 1.8, 2.0, 2.2$. Report the full range of TDS consistent with the measurement
and state the single measurement that would reduce it most.
