# Exercises — 14-04-05 Permafrost Monitoring with Geophysics

Attempt before the [solutions](./05-permafrost-monitoring-solutions.md). Lesson:
[05 Permafrost Monitoring with Geophysics](../05-permafrost-monitoring.md).
All models **SYNTHETIC**. Use $L_f = 334\times10^{3}\ \mathrm{J\,kg^{-1}}$,
$\rho_w = 1000\ \mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$ and $k_\text{ice} = 9.0\ \mathrm{GPa}$.

## ★ Foundational

**★1.** Derive $X = \sqrt{2k\Delta T t/L_v}$ from the energy balance and state the
definition of $L_v$. Then compute $X$ for $\Delta T = 8\ \mathrm{K}$, $90$ days,
$k = 1.5\ \mathrm{W\,m^{-1}\,K^{-1}}$ and $\theta = 0.35$.

**★2.** Using $\phi = 0.30$, $m = n = 2$, $a = 1$ and $\rho_w = 25\
\Omega\,\mathrm{m}$, compute the bulk resistivity at unfrozen fractions of
$1.00$ and $0.10$, and the ratio.

**★3.** State the degree-day form of the Stefan solution and verify it for the
case of ★1 by converting the forcing to degree-days.

## ★★ Applied

**★★4.** By what factor does the thaw depth change if the water content doubles
from $0.35$ to $0.70$ (all else fixed)? Explain why this makes a measured
thaw-depth increase a poor indicator of warming on its own.

**★★5.** A seasonal cycle has $\Delta T = 8\ \mathrm{K}$ over $150$ days with
$k = 1.5\ \mathrm{W\,m^{-1}\,K^{-1}}$ and $\theta = 0.35$, and a fixed sensor
sits at $1.5\ \mathrm{m}$. **(a)** Compute the maximum seasonal thaw depth.
**(b)** Compute the day on which the front crosses the sensor. **(c)** Compute
the rate $\mathrm{d}X/\mathrm{d}t$ at the crossing and hence the depth resolution
of a weekly survey.

**★★6.** Using the lesson's velocity table, state why Gassmann's water-to-ice
substitution predicts only $802\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$ of velocity shift, and give
the range of the illustrative cementation bracket. State which measurement
constrains the missing term.

## ★★★ Research-oriented

**★★★7.** Design a permafrost monitoring programme that must detect a change in
the *unfrozen water content* at a fixed depth, in a setting where the pore-water
salinity is changing. **(a)** State what the survey can and cannot separate.
**(b)** Propose the measurement combination that breaks the ambiguity.
**(c)** State the repeatability requirement and derive it from a stated target
resolution in unfrozen water fraction.

**★★★8.** The Stefan solution assumes a single moving front with constant
properties. **(a)** Identify the two physical effects that most limit it in a
coarse, permeable substrate and explain the direction of the error each causes.
**(b)** Explain why the thermal conductivity of frozen ground differs from that
of thawed ground and how that changes the effective $k$ as the season
progresses. **(c)** State what field measurement would detect the failure of the
Stefan assumption without a temperature model.
