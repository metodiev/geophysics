# Module 15-04 — Seafloor Mapping and Habitat Characterisation

**Stages:** doctoral → research · **Prerequisites:** module 15-02; basic
statistics and rock physics.

This module turns a mapped seafloor into a usable product: a habitat map, a
resource estimate, or an assessment of what a survey can and cannot see. Its
organising idea is that **the map is a model with a resolution, a classification
and an uncertainty**, and that each of the three is chosen rather than measured.

Three honest difficulties run through it. A habitat map's accuracy is a property
of its validation design, not of the map, and the naive sample-count formulas that
design a validation are optimistic by factors that are measured here. A gas
hydrate saturation inferred from a velocity anomaly depends on the rock-physics
model by a factor of two, and the velocity constrains a curve in
(porosity, saturation) rather than a point. And a deep-sea mineral deposit is
almost always too small to detect with the potential-field methods a ship carries,
so the geophysics maps the setting and the resource estimate carries a
geostatistical uncertainty that no amount of algebra removes.

All models and data in this module are **SYNTHETIC**. The seabed parameters,
abundance fields, phase boundaries, rock-physics models and resource geometries
are teaching models with stated model constants; no real survey, seabed, field,
basin, deposit or licence area is referenced, and no number is quoted from a
published measurement. The classification and kriging results are properties of
the synthetic data sets generated by the demonstrations.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this module you will be able to:

1. **Compute** a seabed reflection coefficient and its level in decibels, and
   **state** whether a seabed is in the coherent or the diffuse scattering regime.
2. **Measure** the angular response of backscatter and **explain** why the shape
   of the curve, not a single value, identifies a seabed type.
3. **Quantify** the resolution dependence of a terrain attribute and **state** its
   consequence for a threshold-based habitat class.
4. **Compute** a confusion matrix, recall, precision, accuracy and kappa, and
   **explain** why accuracy alone misleads when class priors are unequal.
5. **State** the two biases that make a reported map accuracy optimistic, and name
   the validation design that removes each.
6. **Derive** the gas yield of structure-I hydrate and **compute** the base of the
   hydrate stability zone from a phase-boundary model and a geotherm.
7. **Invert** a velocity for hydrate saturation under two rock-physics models and
   **quantify** the model dependence.
8. **Produce** the (porosity, saturation) trade-off curve at fixed velocity.
9. **Compute** the gravity and magnetic signature of a model deposit and **say**
   whether a survey can detect it.
10. **Compute** the electromagnetic depth of investigation in seawater and
    **explain** why deep-towed systems are required.
11. **Fit** a variogram, **perform** ordinary kriging, **validate** the kriging
    variance, and **demonstrate** why a denser grid cannot improve an area-mean
    estimate beyond the correlation length.
12. **Produce** a resource estimate with an uncertainty interval and **rank** the
    parameters that dominate it.

## How to run the demos

Use the project virtual environment, which provides NumPy, SciPy, Matplotlib,
pandas, ObsPy and SymPy. Each demonstration prints labelled results with units,
uses a fixed random seed (`np.random.default_rng(20261014)`), requires no network
access, and exits 0.

```bash
cd 15-oceanography-marine/04-seafloor-mapping-and-habitats/code
/Users/miroslav/Documents/workspace/geophysics/.venv/bin/python habitat_mapping_demo.py
/Users/miroslav/Documents/workspace/geophysics/.venv/bin/python gas_hydrates_demo.py
/Users/miroslav/Documents/workspace/geophysics/.venv/bin/python marine_minerals_demo.py
```

Every number quoted in a lesson is either printed by one of these programs or is a
standard constant stated with its units.

## Mastery checkpoint

Complete this without notes:

> A SYNTHETIC survey of a deep-water area returns: a multibeam backscatter curve
> that rises 0.14 dB/deg between 20° and 60° grazing; a slope map whose maximum
> slope on a 200 m grid is 6.72°; a validation matrix in which the rarest of three
> habitats is found with a recall of 0.64; a velocity anomaly of 2300 m/s in a
> sand of 0.35 porosity; and a nodule abundance field whose variogram fits a range
> of 1656 m. **(a)** State the seabed's scattering regime with the roughness
> parameter, and compute what the same seabed would look like at 300 kHz.
> **(b)** Compute the true maximum slope if the mound is Gaussian with $A=50$ m and
> $s=200$ m, and give the resolution bias. **(c)** Compute overall accuracy, kappa
> and the majority-class baseline that the map's headline number must be compared
> with. **(d)** Compute the hydrate saturation under both rock-physics models and
> state which one a $v_p/v_s$ of 4.11 supports. **(e)** Compute the tonnage over
> the area for a mean abundance of $14\ \mathrm{kg\,m^{-2}}$ and state the largest
> single contribution to its uncertainty. **(f)** Label each answer as
> **measured**, **modelled** or **chosen**.

## Common pitfalls across this module

1. **Confusing grazing angle with incidence from the normal.** Every angular
   statement inverts.
2. **Using a coherent-reflection model on a rough seabed.** At 100 kHz the model
   has left its regime and the honest answer is "not applicable".
3. **Reporting overall accuracy with unequal priors.** The majority-class baseline
   can exceed 50% with no skill at all.
4. **Validating with randomly chosen ground truth.** Spatial autocorrelation makes
   the number optimistic; block the validation.
5. **Quoting a gas yield or a saturation without its model constants.** Both are
   model outputs with stated inputs.
6. **Reading a BSR as a geothermometer.** It measures a product of the phase
   boundary, the seafloor temperature and the gradient.
7. **Reporting a deposit-scale potential-field anomaly as detectable.** The
   numbers here are hundredths of a mGal and fractions of a nT.
8. **Using $\sigma/\sqrt n$ for a spatially correlated resource variable.** It
   understated the error by up to 8.5× in this module's measurement.
