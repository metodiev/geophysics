# Exercises — 05-04-04 Velocity–Porosity–Clay Models

Difficulty: ★ · ★★ · ★★★.

---

## ★ Foundational

**★1.** Write the Wyllie time-average and Raymer–Hunt–Gardner relations and
check the dimensions of each.

**★2.** Derive the time average from a travel-time sum and state its geometric
assumption.

**★3.** List the three ways clay changes the two-component picture.

---

## ★★ Applied

**★★4.** With $V_\text{ma} = 4500$ m/s and $V_\text{fl} = 1500$ m/s, compute
$V_p$ from both relations at $\phi = 0.10$ and $\phi = 0.35$, and state where
they diverge most.

**★★5.** Estimate $V_p$ at $\phi = 0.20$ with $V_\text{ma} = 4500$ and again with
$V_\text{ma} = 4300$, using Wyllie, and report the change. Compare with the
Wyllie–Raymer difference at that porosity.

**★★6.** An observed $V_p$ is 2600 m/s at $\phi = 0.30$. Compute the percentage
misfit against both relations (using $V_\text{ma} = 4500$, $V_\text{fl} = 1500$)
and state the likely cause.

**★★7.** Explain why inverting a velocity–porosity relation to obtain porosity,
and then reporting that porosity as a measurement, is a category error.

---

## ★★★ Research-oriented

**★★★8.** Build a three-phase (quartz–clay–water) velocity model as a
volume-weighted average of end members and compare its predictions with the
two-phase relations on a synthetic shaly-sand set. Report the porosity and clay
content at which the two-phase models exceed the three-phase prediction by more
than 5%, and state the calibration you would require before using the three-phase
model on real data.

**★★★9.** Uncertainty propagation. Propagate stated uncertainties in
$V_\text{ma}$ (±4%), $V_\text{fl}$ (±3%) and $\phi$ (±0.02) through the Wyllie
relation at $\phi = 0.15$ and $\phi = 0.30$. Report the resulting $V_p$
uncertainty at each porosity, identify the dominant term, and state whether an
observed 3% velocity anomaly is distinguishable from the input uncertainty.
