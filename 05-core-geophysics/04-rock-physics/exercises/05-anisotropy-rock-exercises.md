# Exercises — 05-04-05 Anisotropy in Rocks: Causes and Magnitudes

Difficulty: ★ · ★★ · ★★★.

---

## ★ Foundational

**★1.** Name the three causes of elastic anisotropy and give an example of each.

**★2.** Write the Thomsen parameters in terms of the VTI stiffness constants and
state the unit of each.

**★3.** Write the axial velocity relations
$V_p(90°)/V_p(0)-1 = \sqrt{1+2\varepsilon}-1$ and the shear analogue, and say
what they check.

---

## ★★ Applied

**★★4.** With $C_{33} = 55$ GPa, $C_{11} = 66$ GPa, $\rho = 2500$ kg/m³,
compute $V_p(0)$ and $V_p(90°)$ and verify the $\varepsilon$ relation.

**★★5.** With $C_{44} = 20$ GPa and $C_{66} = 23$ GPa, compute $\gamma$ and the
SH anisotropy.

**★★6.** Solve the Christoffel matrix at $\theta = 45°$ for the stated VTI
constants and report the three phase velocities. State which is which.

**★★7.** Explain why $\delta$, not $\varepsilon$, controls near-vertical
moveout and depth ties.

---

## ★★★ Research-oriented

**★★★8.** Backus averaging. For a stack of thin isotropic layers with stated
moduli and thickness fractions, compute the effective VTI constants via the
Backus averages, then compute the Thomsen parameters. Report the anisotropy as a
function of the layer contrast, and state the wavelength condition under which
the result is valid. Compare with the anisotropy you would need to explain a
stated depth tie error.

**★★★9.** Solve the inverse problem: given measured axial velocities and an
assumed $\delta$ (or an assumed $\varepsilon/\delta$ ratio), recover the five
stiffness constants. Report which combinations are well determined and which are
not, and explain why $\delta$ is the hardest to constrain from vertical and
horizontal velocities alone.
