# Exercises — 05-01-01 The Central Idea: Property Contrasts Produce Signals

Attempt these **closed-book** before opening the
[solutions](./01-physical-property-contrast-solutions.md). Difficulty:
★ foundational · ★★ applied · ★★★ research-oriented.

**Use $G = 6.67430\times10^{-11}\ \mathrm{m^3\,kg^{-1}\,s^{-2}}$ and
$1\ \mathrm{mGal} = 10^{-5}\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$. Show the units at every step.**

---

## ★ Foundational

**★1.** Explain in two or three sentences why a uniform, infinite half-space of
any density produces no detectable gravity anomaly.

**★2.** A body has density contrast $\Delta\rho = +400$ kg/m³ with respect to
its host. State the anomaly's sign, and give the relative contrast for a host
density of 2600 kg/m³.

**★3.** Convert: (a) 12.4 mGal to m/s²; (b) 850 nT to T; (c) 2.67 g/cm³ to
kg/m³; (d) 4500 m/s to km/s. State which conversions are exact by definition.

**★4.** From $\Phi = -Gm/r$, derive $g_z = -\partial\Phi/\partial z$ for a mass
at depth $d$ directly below the observer, and check the dimensions of the
result.

---

## ★★ Applied

**★★5.** A sphere of radius 40 m has a density contrast of 350 kg/m³. Compute
its excess mass, and its peak anomaly for centre depths of 100 m, 200 m and
400 m. Express the answers in mGal and state which depths are detectable against
0.01 mGal repeatability and a 0.05 mGal model uncertainty.

**★★6.** A dyke can be approximated as an infinite 2-D line mass with line
density $\lambda$ (kg/m) at depth $d$. Using
$g_z = 2G\lambda d/(x^2+d^2)$, derive the peak anomaly and show that it decays
as $d^{-1}$ rather than $d^{-2}$. Explain the difference from the sphere case
and what it implies for the depth reach of a survey over a tabular body.

**★★7.** A body has volume $2\times10^{6}$ m³ and magnetisation 3 A/m. Compute
its dipole moment. Evaluate the total-field anomaly directly above it at 200 m
depth for ambient inclinations of 0°, 45° and 90°, and state which geometry
gives no signal.

**★★8.** Two rock units have $\rho_1v_1 = 8.0\times10^{6}$ and
$\rho_2v_2 = 9.6\times10^{6}$ kg m⁻² s⁻¹. Compute $R$. Then find a second pair
of densities and velocities with the same $R$ and explain why the seismic data
cannot distinguish them.

---

## ★★★ Research-oriented

**★★★9.** Design a detectability study for a target of your choice: state the
contrast with a source or as an explicit synthetic value, compute the anomaly
with the correct geometry (3-D or 2-D), state every noise contribution
separately, and give a CNR. Then identify which single assumption, if wrong by
a factor of two, would reverse your conclusion. Report all numbers with units
and state explicitly which are measured, which are derived, and which are
assumed.

**★★★10.** The information-content argument in the lesson claims that
information falls as roughly $d^{-3}$ for a compact source. Construct a
numerical experiment that measures the exponent: place a compact body at a
range of depths, add fixed-amplitude noise, and quantify how well the source
mass can be recovered (for example, the width of the posterior or the condition
number of a two-parameter fit). Report the measured exponent, the method, and
the limits of your conclusion. Does the exponent depend on the source symmetry?
Justify your answer with the scaling of the kernel.
