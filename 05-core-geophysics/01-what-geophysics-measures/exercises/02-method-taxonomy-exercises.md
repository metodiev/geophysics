# Exercises — 05-01-02 Taxonomy of Methods

Attempt closed-book. Difficulty: ★ · ★★ · ★★★.

---

## ★ Foundational

**★1.** Classify each method on the four axes (energy source, field type,
measurement domain, target property): (a) gravity, (b) magnetics,
(c) magnetotellurics, (d) reflection seismics, (e) GPR, (f) DC resistivity.

**★2.** State the Nyquist condition linking receiver spacing $\Delta x$ to the
shortest resolvable lateral wavelength. Convert it to a maximum wavenumber.

**★3.** A source has a 30 Hz dominant frequency but a 5–90 Hz bandwidth. State
which number controls resolution and why.

---

## ★★ Applied

**★★4.** A shallow reflection survey uses a source with usable bandwidth
5–80 Hz in material of velocity 1800 m/s. Compute the vertical resolution
limit. Then compute it for a 5–160 Hz source, and state what must be true about
attenuation for the second source to actually help.

**★★5.** A profile is 800 m long with 4 m receiver spacing. State the shortest
and longest recoverable lateral wavelengths and the ratio between them. If the
target is 12 m wide, is the survey adequate?

**★★6.** Explain, with a physical argument and no equations, why
magnetotellurics can reach 100 km depth while a controlled-source EM system
reaches at most a few hundred metres — from the *source* viewpoint, then from
the *physics* viewpoint. Which one really decides?

**★★7.** An array is 200 m across and records microseismic events at 2 km
depth. Estimate the achievable location accuracy in the horizontal plane from
the aperture alone, and compare it with a reflection survey over the same area.

---

## ★★★ Research-oriented

**★★★8.** Choose a real exploration problem type (a tabular orebody, a
saline aquifer, a shallow fault, a buried landfill). For each, propose the
method family and the geometry, and justify both with a computation: a
contrast-to-noise ratio for the method and a resolution calculation for the
geometry. State every input as measured, derived, or assumed. Then state the
single design change that would most improve the outcome and its approximate
cost.

**★★★9.** Design, on paper, a survey whose aperture and spacing are chosen so
that the recoverable wavelength band matches a specified target spectrum.
Derive the band, state the receiver count, and explain what happens to the
image if you halve the receiver count but keep the aperture (aliasing) and if
you halve the aperture but keep the count (loss of long wavelengths). Quantify
both effects.
