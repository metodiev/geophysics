# Exercises — 05-03-03 Superposition and Building Complex Models

Difficulty: ★ · ★★ · ★★★.

---

## ★ Foundational

**★1.** State the superposition principle and write it in matrix form.

**★2.** For each of gravity, magnetics, DC resistivity and seismic amplitude,
state what the method is linear in, if anything.

**★3.** Explain why "linearity of the governing equation" and "linearity in the
model parameter" are different statements, using resistivity as the example.

---

## ★★ Applied

**★★4.** Two prisms of density contrast 300 kg/m³, each 100 m wide, lie side by
side forming a 200 m wide body at 150 m depth. Explain why the anomaly of the
pair is not simply twice the anomaly of one prism, and sketch (in words) how the
shape differs.

**★★5.** A regional field is modelled by 12 large prisms. Its predicted anomaly
is 8.4 mGal with an uncertainty of 0.9 mGal; the observed anomaly is 9.0 mGal
with an uncertainty of 0.2 mGal. Compute the uncertainty of the residual and
state whether the subtraction was worthwhile.

**★★6.** For a sphere with susceptibility 0.5 in a 50 000 nT field, compute
$\chi_\text{eff}$ and the factor by which a linear (non-demagnetised)
calculation overestimates the magnetisation.

---

## ★★★ Research-oriented

**★★★7.** Build a stepped density model from 10 prisms, compute its anomaly by
summation, and verify it against a direct numerical quadrature of the same
geometry. Report the measured agreement and state which is the reference.
Then displace one prism by 5 m and report the change in the anomaly: this is the
*sensitivity* of the data to one model parameter, i.e. one column of
$\mathbf{G}$.

**★★★8.** Investigate the failure of superposition. For a synthetic
susceptibility of 0.2, 0.5 and 1.0, compute the anomaly (i) with demagnetisation
and (ii) without, and report the ratio. Then state the susceptibility at which
the linear approximation is wrong by more than 10%, and explain why the
threshold depends on body shape.
