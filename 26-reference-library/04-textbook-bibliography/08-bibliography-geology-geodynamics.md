# Annotated Bibliography: Geology, Geodynamics and Tectonics

The geological literature that gives geophysical measurements their meaning,
plus the geodynamics and tectonics texts that connect the measurements to
process. Geophysical results are usually reported as physical quantities; they
become a contribution to Earth science only when related to rock, structure and
time.

## How to use this page

Grouped as: general physical geology, structural geology, petrology and
sedimentology, tectonics and geodynamics, and hydrogeology. The "not for" column
matters particularly here, because geology books are rarely written to answer a
geophysical question.

**Honesty statement about citations.** Every work listed is real. Editions and
years are indicative; verify before citing. Where a widely used textbook is
revised frequently, the edition is stated as "editions vary" rather than guessed.

*Provenance:* bibliographic reference only. No copyrighted text is reproduced.

## General physical geology

| Work | Level | Use it for | Not for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Grotzinger & Jordan**, *Understanding Earth* (Freeman/Macmillan; editions vary) | beginner | The standard introductory physical geology course text; useful as a coherent first picture | Quantitative treatment |
| **Tarbuck & Lutgens**, *Earth: An Introduction to Physical Geology* (Pearson; editions vary) | beginner | An alternative introductory survey, with strong descriptive material | Geophysics |
| **Marshak**, *Earth: Portrait of a Planet* (Norton; editions vary) | beginner | Well-illustrated introduction, good on plate-tectonic context | Research depth |
| **Kehew**, *Geology for Engineers and Environmental Scientists* (Prentice Hall; 3rd ed. 2006) | intermediate | Geology framed for engineering and environmental applications, which is closer to how exploration problems are posed | Tectonics |
| **Press & Siever** (early editions) and its successor texts | beginner | Historical standards still useful for the physical intuition | Modern terminology |

## Structural geology and tectonics

| Work | Level | Use it for | Not for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Twiss & Moores**, *Structural Geology* (Freeman; 2nd ed. 2007) | advanced | The quantitative structural geology reference: stress, strain, fractures, faults, folds, with the mathematics developed | Seismology |
| **Fossen**, *Structural Geology* (Cambridge University Press; 2nd ed. 2016) | intermediate–advanced | The modern, well-illustrated textbook, strong on the link between structures and their tectonic settings | Advanced rock mechanics |
| **Davis, Reynolds & Kluth**, *Structural Geology of Rocks and Regions* (Wiley; 3rd ed. 2011) | advanced | Field-based description and interpretation, with a strong regional-geology orientation | Continuum mechanics |
| **Ramsay & Huber**, *The Techniques of Modern Structural Geology* (Academic Press; three volumes, 1983–2000) | research | Classic techniques, especially strain analysis from deformed markers | Modern numerical modelling |
| **Kearey, Klepeis & Vine**, *Global Tectonics* (Wiley-Blackwell; 3rd ed. 2009) | intermediate–advanced | The best single tectonic synthesis: plates, margins, orogens, and the evidence for each | Quantitative geodynamics |
| **Cox & Hart**, *Plate Tectonics: How It Works* (Blackwell; 1986) | intermediate | The quantitative geometry of plate motion: Euler poles, velocity triangles, magnetic anomalies | Modern data |
| **Condie**, *Earth as an Evolving Planetary System* (Academic Press; 3rd ed. 2016) | advanced | The long-term evolution of the Earth, useful for framing geophysical observations in time | Method detail |

| Work | Level | Use it for | Not for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Best**, *Igneous and Metamorphic Petrology* (Wiley; 3rd ed. 2013) | advanced | Petrology with a physical-chemistry orientation; useful for tying seismic velocity to mineralogy | Geophysics |
| **Winter**, *Principles of Igneous and Metamorphic Petrology* (Pearson; editions vary) | intermediate–advanced | A widely used teaching text for the same material | Research-level thermobarometry |
| **Bucher & Grapes**, *Petrogenesis of Metamorphic Rocks* (Springer; 8th ed. 2011) | research | Metamorphic facies, reactions and pressure–temperature paths | Geodynamics |
| **Boggs**, *Principles of Sedimentology and Stratigraphy* (Pearson; editions vary) | intermediate | Facies, depositional systems and stratigraphic architecture — the framework for interpreting seismic and well data | Sequence-stratigraphic methods in detail |
| **Nichols**, *Sedimentology and Stratigraphy* (Wiley-Blackwell; 2nd ed. 2009) | intermediate | A complementary, tightly written treatment with good process description | Basin modelling |
| **Selley & Sonnenberg**, *Elements of Petroleum Geology* (Academic Press; 3rd ed. 2014) | advanced | The petroleum system as a geological object: source, reservoir, seal, trap | Seismic processing |

## Geodynamics, mantle and deep Earth

| Work | Level | Use it for | Not for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Turcotte & Schubert**, *Geodynamics* (Cambridge University Press; 3rd ed. 2014) | advanced | Heat transfer, convection, flexure, and the quantitative treatment of plate driving forces | Observational seismology |
| **Schubert, Turcotte & Olson**, *Mantle Convection in the Earth and Planets* (Cambridge University Press; 2001) | research | The comprehensive mantle convection reference: scaling, instabilities, planetary comparisons | A first course |
| **Anderson**, *Theory of the Earth* (Blackwell; 1989, made freely available by the author) | research | A coherent physical account of the Earth's interior, written as a consistent argument rather than a survey | Contemporary data |
| **Davies**, *Dynamic Earth: Plates, Plumes and Mantle Convection* (Cambridge University Press; 1999) | research | The dynamics of the mantle and its surface expression, with a clear authorial viewpoint | A neutral synthesis |
| **Karato**, *Deformation of Earth Materials* (Cambridge University Press; 2008) | research | Creep mechanisms and their seismic signature | Geodynamics at plate scale |
| **Ranalli**, *Rheology of the Earth* (Chapman & Hall; 2nd ed. 1995) | advanced | Rheological layering and lithospheric strength | Mantle convection |
| **Anderson & Anderson**, *Geomorphology: The Mechanics and Chemistry of Landscapes* (Cambridge University Press; 2010) | advanced | Surface processes and their coupling to tectonics and climate | Subsurface methods |

## Hydrogeology and porous media

| Work | Level | Use it for | Not for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Freeze & Cherry**, *Groundwater* (Prentice-Hall; 1979) | graduate | The classic comprehensive hydrogeology text; still the reference for flow, transport and aquifer characterisation | Modern numerical practice |
| **Fetter**, *Applied Hydrogeology* (Prentice Hall; 4th ed. 2001, later reissues) | intermediate–advanced | A practical, exercises-rich introduction to aquifer testing, well hydraulics and water chemistry | Research geophysics |
| **Hiscock & Bense**, *Hydrogeology: Principles and Practice* (Wiley-Blackwell; 2nd ed. 2014) | intermediate | A modern teaching text with a European emphasis and good groundwater-flow examples | Contaminant-transport theory |
| **Bear**, *Dynamics of Fluids in Porous Media* (American Elsevier; 1972, reprinted by Dover) | research | The rigorous development of Darcy's law, permeability, dispersion and continuum averaging | Field practice |
| **de Marsily**, *Quantitative Hydrogeology* (Academic Press; 1986) | advanced | Groundwater flow and transport with the mathematics made explicit, including inverse problems | Modern software |
| **Domenico & Schwartz**, *Physical and Chemical Hydrogeology* (Wiley; 2nd ed. 1998) | advanced | The physical and chemical halves of the subject in one volume | Tectonics |

## Which one to read, by need

| If you need to… | Start with |
|---|---|
| Place a geophysical result in a geological story | Grotzinger & Jordan, then Kearey et al. |
| Interpret a seismic or gravity map in structural terms | Fossen or Twiss & Moores |
| Justify a mantle dynamics scaling | Turcotte & Schubert |
| Understand a bulk modulus or density in mineralogical terms | Best or Winter |
| Interpret a resistivity or EM survey in an aquifer | Freeze & Cherry, then Kirsch (in module 26-04-04) |

## How to cite these works

Give the edition, because geological terminology and stratigraphic frameworks are
revised between editions — a "Permian" boundary or a facies name can change
meaning, and a page reference is only meaningful with the edition.

## Assumptions and regime of validity

- Geological maps and units are interpretive products with stated authors and
  dates; cite the map sheet, its scale, its edition and its compiler.
- Textbook "typical" rock properties are ranges; the variation within one
  formation commonly exceeds the variation between formations.
- Copyrighted works are cited only.
