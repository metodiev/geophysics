# Annotated Bibliography: Physics, Elasticity and Continuum Mechanics

The physics behind the equations the rest of the curriculum applies: classical
mechanics, the mechanics of continuous media, elasticity, fluid dynamics, and
rock mechanics as a physical rather than an engineering subject.

## How to use this page

Grouped by role: general geophysics texts first (they set the context), then
continuum mechanics and elasticity, then fluids and thermodynamics, then rock
mechanics. The "not for" column matters as much as the recommendation: most
over-application of a textbook happens because it was read for something it does
not cover.

**Honesty statement about citations.** These are real works; no DOI, URL, author
or title is invented. Editions and years are indicative and must be verified in
a library catalogue before citing. Where the edition is uncertain, the entry says
so.

*Provenance:* bibliographic reference only. No copyrighted text is reproduced.

## General geophysics and Earth physics

| Work | Level | Use it for | Not for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Fowler**, *The Solid Earth: An Introduction to Global Geophysics* (Cambridge University Press; 2nd ed. 2005) | advanced | A single coherent account of the physics of the solid Earth: seismology, gravity, heat, geomagnetism and mantle convection at a consistent level | Detailed exploration methods |
| **Lowrie**, *Fundamentals of Geophysics* (Cambridge University Press; 2nd ed. 2007, later editions exist) | intermediate | A broad, well-illustrated undergraduate survey; good for orientation before a specialist text | Research-level derivations |
| **Stacey & Davis**, *Physics of the Earth* (Cambridge University Press; 4th ed. 2008) | advanced | Quantitative treatment of Earth structure and dynamics: elasticity, thermal state, core, geomagnetism | Field methods |
| **Sleep & Fujita**, *Principles of Geophysics* (Blackwell; 1997) | advanced | Physics-first treatment of the solid Earth, useful for its emphasis on conservation laws and scaling | Modern computational practice |
| **Turcotte & Schubert**, *Geodynamics* (Cambridge University Press; 3rd ed. 2014) | advanced | The standard source for heat transfer, mantle convection, lithospheric flexure and plate driving forces, with worked problems | Seismological data analysis |

## Continuum mechanics and elasticity

| Work | Level | Use it for | Not for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Malvern**, *Introduction to the Mechanics of a Continuous Medium* (Prentice-Hall; 1969) | graduate | The rigorous derivation of stress, strain, conservation laws and constitutive theory in a single consistent notation | Numerical methods |
| **Fung**, *A First Course in Continuum Mechanics* (Cambridge University Press; 3rd ed. 1994, later editions exist) | advanced | A clear, physically motivated introduction to tensors, stress, strain and the field equations | Advanced constitutive modelling |
| **Landau & Lifshitz**, *Theory of Elasticity* (Course of Theoretical Physics, vol. 7; Pergamon, various printings) | research | The concise canonical treatment: equilibrium, plates and shells, dislocations, thermal stresses | A first course — it is extremely terse |
| **Sokolnikoff**, *Mathematical Theory of Elasticity* (McGraw-Hill, later Dover reprint) | graduate | Classical elasticity with full derivations, including the theorem-based solution methods | Anisotropy and rheology of real rocks |
| **Jaeger, Cook & Zimmerman**, *Fundamentals of Rock Mechanics* (Blackwell/Wiley; 4th ed. 2007) | advanced | Rock mechanics with a physics orientation: stress, failure criteria, poroelasticity, laboratory behaviour | Seismic wave propagation |

## Fluids, thermodynamics and heat

| Work | Level | Use it for | Not for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Landau & Lifshitz**, *Fluid Mechanics* (Course of Theoretical Physics, vol. 6; Pergamon, various printings) | research | The concise classical treatment of ideal and viscous fluids, turbulence, boundary layers and shocks | An introductory text |
| **Batchelor**, *An Introduction to Fluid Dynamics* (Cambridge University Press, various printings) | graduate | A rigorous, elegant account with careful attention to vorticity and to the physical meaning of approximations | Geophysical applications, which it barely mentions |
| **Carslaw & Jaeger**, *Conduction of Heat in Solids* (Oxford University Press; 2nd ed. 1959) | graduate | The reference for analytic solutions of the heat equation, used directly in geothermics and in thermal inertia work | Convective heat transfer |
| **Turcotte & Schubert** (above) | advanced | Boundary-layer and convection scaling applied to the mantle | Laboratory fluid mechanics |

## Rock physics and rheology

| Work | Level | Use it for | Not for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Karato**, *Deformation of Earth Materials* (Cambridge University Press; 2008) | research | The modern physical treatment of creep mechanisms, point defects, anisotropy and their seismic signatures | Elastic wave theory |
| **Ranalli**, *Rheology of the Earth* (Chapman & Hall; 2nd ed. 1995) | advanced | Rheological models of the lithosphere and mantle, with the geodynamic applications | Laboratory data compilations published since the 1990s |
| **Mavko, Mukerji & Dvorkin**, *The Rock Physics Handbook* (Cambridge University Press; editions vary) | advanced | A working reference for velocity–porosity relations, effective-medium models and fluid substitution | Derivations from first principles |
| **Schön**, *Physical Properties of Rocks: Fundamentals and Principles of Petrophysics* (Elsevier; 2nd ed. 2015) | advanced | Property measurements and their petrophysical interpretation, useful for tying rock physics to logs | Wave-theory derivation |

## Which one to read, by need

| If you need to… | Start with |
|---|---|
| Place a physical result in the Earth | Fowler or Turcotte & Schubert |
| Get stress and strain defined properly | Malvern, or Fung for a gentler route |
| Look up a classical elasticity result | Landau & Lifshitz, then Sokolnikoff |
| Justify a heat-flow or convection scaling | Turcotte & Schubert; Carslaw & Jaeger for analytic solutions |
| Understand why rock creeps the way it does | Karato; Ranalli for the geodynamic consequence |

## How to cite these works

Cite the edition you used, because the notation tradition differs between books
and editions: the Lamé parameters, the sign convention for stress and the
definition of the strain tensor all vary. A formula copied across books without
checking the convention is the classic source of a factor-of-two error.

## Assumptions and regime of validity

- Levels and "use it for" notes are this repository's opinion, not claims of
  authority.
- Where a book is old (Malvern 1969, Carslaw & Jaeger 1959), its classical
  content is still current, but its numerical and computational practice is not.
- Copyrighted works are cited only. Verify edition, year and availability.
