# Annotated Bibliography: Potential Fields, EM and Electrical Methods

Gravity, magnetics and the electromagnetic and electrical methods: a
literature that is unusually split between theory monographs, SEG volumes of
applied practice, and national-agency documentation of the data themselves. All
three kinds of source are needed, for different reasons.

## How to use this page

Entries are grouped by method. The "use it for" column distinguishes *theory*
(what the physics permits), *practice* (what a survey actually delivers) and
*data documentation* (what a specific compilation contains). Confusing these
three is the commonest citation error in this subject.

**Honesty statement about citations.** Every work listed is real and
attributable. Journal volume and page numbers are omitted rather than guessed —
verify them before citing. Editions of books are indicative.

*Provenance:* bibliographic reference only. No copyrighted text is reproduced.

## Gravity and potential theory

| Work | Level | Use it for | Not for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Blakely**, *Potential Theory in Gravity and Magnetic Applications* (Cambridge University Press; 1995) | advanced | The single best bridge between potential theory and applied practice: Fourier methods, continuation, derivatives, inversion | Modern satellite gravity |
| **Hinze, von Frese & Saad**, *Gravity and Magnetic Exploration* (Cambridge University Press; 2013) | advanced | The modern applied reference: acquisition, reduction, interpretation, with attention to airborne and satellite work | Electrical or EM methods |
| **Heiskanen & Moritz**, *Physical Geodesy* (Freeman; 1967) | graduate | The classical derivation of the gravity field, the geoid and normal gravity | Modern satellite-derived models |
| **Hofmann-Wellenhof & Moritz**, *Physical Geodesy* (Springer; 2nd ed. 2006) | graduate | The current standard text, including the boundary-value formulation and modern applications | Practical surveying technique |
| **Nettleton**, *Gravity and Magnetics in Oil Prospecting* (McGraw-Hill) | applied | Historical field practice and interpretation heuristics that are still quoted | Modern data reduction; the edition and year must be verified before citing |
| **Grant & West**, *Interpretation Theory in Applied Geophysics* (McGraw-Hill; 1965) | applied | Classic anomaly-shape interpretation, with a level of detail rarely repeated | Contemporary acquisition practice |

## Magnetics and geomagnetism

| Work | Level | Use it for | Not for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Blakely** (above) | advanced | Magnetic forward modelling and inversion in the same framework as gravity | Geodynamo theory |
| **Merrill, McElhinny & McFadden**, *The Magnetic Field of the Earth: Paleomagnetism, the Core and the Deep Mantle* (Academic Press; 1996) | research | The comprehensive account of the geomagnetic field, palaeomagnetism and the core | Exploration magnetics processing |
| **Tauxe**, *Essentials of Paleomagnetism* (University of California Press; 2010) | intermediate | A modern, freely available textbook treatment of palaeomagnetic methods and statistics | Active-source magnetics |
| **Campbell**, *Introduction to Geomagnetic Fields* (Cambridge University Press; 2nd ed. 2003) | advanced | The physics of the quiet and disturbed field, useful background for magnetic surveys and MT | Survey processing |
| **IGRF and WMM model documentation** (IAGA and NOAA respectively; updated on a five-year cadence) | reference | The coefficients and conventions for the reference field that every magnetic anomaly is measured against | Anomaly interpretation |
| **Blakely** (above) | advanced | Reduction to the pole, upward continuation and derivative filters | Low-latitude instability, which requires extra care beyond the book |

## Electromagnetics: theory and practice

| Work | Level | Use it for | Not for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Nabighian (ed.)**, *Electromagnetic Methods in Applied Geophysics* (SEG; two volumes, 1988 and 1991) | advanced | The standard applied reference: theory, forward modelling, field systems and case histories, in one coherent set | Modern 3-D inversion practice, which has moved on |
| **Ward & Hohmann**, the EM theory chapter in Nabighian's first volume | advanced | The canonical derivation of EM forward responses used in MT and FDEM | Software practice |
| **Zhdanov**, *Geophysical Electromagnetic Theory and Methods* (Elsevier; 2009) | research | A modern unified treatment including inversion, with the algebra worked out | A first introduction |
| **Simpson & Bahr**, *Practical Magnetotellurics* (Cambridge University Press; 2005) | advanced | The practical MT reference: processing, distortion, dimensionality, interpretation | Full theory of 3-D modelling |
| **Chave & Jones (eds)**, *The Magnetotelluric Method: Theory and Practice* (Cambridge University Press; 2012) | research | The comprehensive modern MT volume: theory, inversion, and honest treatment of uncertainty | Beginner-level reading |
| **Kaufman & Keller**, *The Magnetotelluric Sounding Method* (Elsevier; 1981) | graduate | The classical development of the 1-D sounding problem and its asymptotics | 3-D concepts |
| **Telford, Geldart & Sheriff**, *Applied Geophysics* (Cambridge University Press; 2nd ed. 1990) | intermediate | Cross-method comparison and the elementary theory of electrical and EM methods | Research detail |

## Electrical and near-surface methods

| Work | Level | Use it for | Not for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Reynolds**, *An Introduction to Applied and Environmental Geophysics* (Wiley; 2nd ed. 2011) | intermediate | The best modern cross-method applied text, strong on near-surface electrical, EM and GPR methods | Deep-crustal work |
| **Milsom & Eriksen**, *Field Geophysics* (Wiley; 4th ed. 2011) | beginner–intermediate | Field procedure, instrument handling, and what measurements to make in what order | Theory |
| **Parasnis**, *Principles of Applied Geophysics* (Chapman & Hall; 5th ed. 1997) | intermediate | A compact classical treatment of gravity, magnetics, electrical and seismic methods | Modern computational practice |
| **Everett**, *Near-Surface Applied Geophysics* (Cambridge University Press; 2013) | advanced | The modern near-surface reference: ERT, GPR, EMI, seismic refraction and their joint interpretation | Deep exploration |
| **Sharma**, *Environmental and Engineering Geophysics* (Cambridge University Press; 1997) | intermediate | Application-driven survey design for engineering and environmental problems | Advanced inversion |
| **Butler (ed.)**, *Near-Surface Geophysics* (SEG; 2005) | advanced | A multi-author applied reference covering the whole near-surface toolbox | A single coherent pedagogy |
| **Kirsch (ed.)**, *Groundwater Geophysics: A Tool for Hydrogeology* (Springer; 2nd ed. 2009) | advanced | Methods as hydrogeological tools, with attention to what each can and cannot resolve | Wave theory |
| **Binley & Kemna** and similar review articles on electrical resistivity tomography | research | Current practice in ERT acquisition, inversion and monitoring | Verify bibliographic details before citing |

## Which one to read, by need

| If you need to… | Start with |
|---|---|
| Apply potential theory to real anomalies | Blakely, then Hinze et al. |
| Handle the reference field correctly | IGRF/WMM documentation first, then Campbell |
| Understand an MT response | Simpson & Bahr; Chave & Jones for depth |
| Choose a field method for a shallow target | Reynolds or Everett |
| Know what the *data* are, rather than the method | the dataset documentation in [module 26-05](../05-open-datasets-catalogue/README.md) |

## How to cite these works

For model documentation (IGRF, WMM, gravity models, global relief models) cite
the *version* and the year of release, not merely the institution: coefficients
and conventions change with each generation, and a result computed with one
generation is not reproducible with another.

## Assumptions and regime of validity

- Method books written before about 1990 describe processing that is still
  conceptually valid but numerically dated; use them for ideas and modern papers
  for algorithms.
- SEG edited volumes remain in copyright; cite them, do not reproduce tables.
- "Interpretation heuristics" (depth rules, half-width estimates) are
  model-dependent; the books generally say so, and users generally ignore it.
