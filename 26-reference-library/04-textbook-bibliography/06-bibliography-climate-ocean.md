# Annotated Bibliography: Climate, Atmosphere and Ocean

Textbooks for the atmosphere–ocean system, plus the assessment-report literature,
which is a different kind of source and needs to be cited differently.

## How to use this page

Grouped as: atmospheric thermodynamics and dynamics, radiation, boundary layer,
ocean physics, air–sea interaction and climate, and the assessment reports. The
assessment reports are listed separately because they are multi-authored
syntheses with their own citation conventions and their own review status —
neither textbooks nor primary papers.

**Honesty statement about citations.** Every work listed is real. Volume, page
and report-chapter details are omitted rather than guessed; verify them before
citing. Editions are indicative.

*Provenance:* bibliographic reference only. No copyrighted text is reproduced.

## Atmospheric thermodynamics and dynamics

| Work | Level | Use it for | Not for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Wallace & Hobbs**, *Atmospheric Science: An Introductory Survey* (Academic Press/Elsevier; 2nd ed. 2006) | intermediate | The best single-volume undergraduate survey: thermodynamics, dynamics, radiation, clouds, climate | Research-level derivations |
| **Holton & Hakim**, *An Introduction to Dynamic Meteorology* (Academic Press/Elsevier; 5th ed. 2013) | advanced | The standard dynamical meteorology text: balanced flows, quasi-geostrophy, waves, instability | Ocean dynamics, which it mentions only in passing |
| **Vallis**, *Atmospheric and Oceanic Fluid Dynamics* (Cambridge University Press; 2nd ed. 2017) | research | The unifying modern treatment of atmosphere and ocean dynamics together, including instabilities, turbulence and wave–mean-flow interaction | A first course in meteorology |
| **Pedlosky**, *Geophysical Fluid Dynamics* (Springer; 2nd ed. 1987) | graduate | The classical scaling-based treatment: rotation effects, Rossby waves, baroclinic instability | Modern data analysis |
| **Salby**, *Fundamentals of Atmospheric Physics* (Academic Press; 1996) | advanced | Atmospheric radiation, thermodynamics, chemistry and dynamics in one tightly written volume | Oceanography |
| **Stull**, *An Introduction to Boundary Layer Meteorology* (Kluwer/Springer; 1988) | advanced | The boundary layer: turbulence closures, similarity theory, stability classification | Free-tropospheric dynamics |
| **Garratt**, *The Atmospheric Boundary Layer* (Cambridge University Press; 1992) | research | A complementary, more theoretical treatment of the same subject | Measurement practice |

## Radiation and climate physics

| Work | Level | Use it for | Not for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Liou**, *An Introduction to Atmospheric Radiation* (Academic Press; 2nd ed. 2002, later editions exist) | advanced | The standard text on radiative transfer, scattering and satellite retrieval | Energy-balance policy questions |
| **Goody & Yung**, *Atmospheric Radiation: Theoretical Basis* (Oxford University Press; 2nd ed. 1989) | research | The rigorous development of the transfer equation and band models | A first exposure; assumes fluency in the mathematics |
| **Petty**, *A First Course in Atmospheric Radiation* (Sundog Publishing; 2nd ed. 2006) | intermediate | A friendly, physically careful introduction to the same material | Advanced band modelling |
| **Hartmann**, *Global Physical Climatology* (Elsevier; 2nd ed. 2016) | advanced | The energy balance, feedbacks and climate sensitivity at textbook level | Regional projection detail |
| **Peixoto & Oort**, *Physics of Climate* (American Institute of Physics; 1992) | research | The observational climatology of energy, water and momentum budgets in quantitative form | Modern model intercomparison |
| **IPCC Assessment Reports** (AR5, 2013; AR6, 2021 and its special reports) | reference | The agreed synthesis of the state of knowledge, with calibrated uncertainty language and extensive references | Original results; it synthesises, it does not report measurements |

## Ocean physics and air–sea interaction

| Work | Level | Use it for | Not for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Gill**, *Atmosphere–Ocean Dynamics* (Academic Press; 1982) | graduate | The classic combined text, especially strong on the equatorial ocean and on the link between dynamics and thermodynamics | Data or observation |
| **Cushman-Roisin & Beckers**, *Introduction to Geophysical Fluid Dynamics* (Academic Press; 2nd ed. 2011) | advanced | A pedagogical route into the same dynamics with modern numerical context | Detailed ocean circulation climatology |
| **Marshall & Plumb**, *Atmosphere, Ocean, and Climate Dynamics* (Academic Press; 2008) | advanced | An explicitly comparative treatment, useful when the atmosphere–ocean analogy matters | Observational oceanography |
| **Talley, Pickard, Emery & Swift**, *Descriptive Physical Oceanography* (Academic Press; 6th ed. 2011) | intermediate | The observational backbone: water masses, circulation, property distributions | Derivation of governing equations |
| **Apel**, *Principles of Ocean Physics* (Academic Press; 1987) | advanced | Waves, tides, acoustics and optics in one volume | Contemporary circulation modelling |
| **Stewart**, *Introduction to Physical Oceanography* (open textbook, various dates; freely available from the author) | beginner–intermediate | A free, accessible introduction suitable for self-study | Research-level treatment |
| **Emery & Thomson**, *Data Analysis Methods in Physical Oceanography* (Elsevier; 3rd ed. 2014) | advanced | Time-series, spectral and spatial analysis with oceanographic worked examples, including the practical pitfalls | Theoretical statistics |
| **Kraus & Businger**, *Atmosphere–Ocean Interaction* (Oxford University Press; 2nd ed. 1994) | research | Fluxes, bulk formulas and the physical basis of air–sea exchange | Modern satellite flux products |

## Tides, sea level and the ocean inverse problem

| Work | Level | Use it for | Not for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Wunsch**, *The Ocean Circulation Inverse Problem* (Cambridge University Press; 1996) | research | Inverting hydrographic and tracer data for circulation, with full attention to uncertainty — the oceanographic counterpart of this curriculum's inverse module | Elementary oceanography |
| **Pedlosky**, *Ocean Circulation Theory* (Springer; 1996) | research | The theoretical account of wind-driven and thermohaline circulation | Data analysis |
| **Pugh & Woodworth**, *Sea-Level Science* (Cambridge University Press; 2014) | advanced | Tides, surges, mean sea level and their observation, including the datum problems | Projections of future sea level |
| **Siedler, Griffies, Gould & Church (eds)**, *Ocean Circulation and Climate* (Academic Press; 2013) | research | A multi-author synthesis of observing systems, circulation and climate | A single coherent pedagogy |

## Which one to read, by need

| If you need to… | Start with |
|---|---|
| Get the thermodynamics of air right | Wallace & Hobbs, then Holton & Hakim |
| Understand a rotating-fluid scaling argument | Pedlosky or Vallis |
| Handle radiation and remote sensing | Petty, then Liou |
| Work with tide-gauge or altimeter sea level | Pugh & Woodworth |
| Invert oceanographic data with error bars | Wunsch |
| Cite the current consensus on climate | the relevant IPCC report, cited by report and chapter |

## How to cite these works

- Textbooks: author, title, edition, publisher, year.
- Assessment reports: cite the report, the year, the specific chapter and the
  chapter authors — not merely "IPCC". The uncertainty language
  ("likely", "very likely") has a defined numerical meaning in those reports and
  must be preserved when quoted.
- Free textbooks: cite the printed or versioned source, with the version you
  used, because they are revised.

## Assumptions and regime of validity

- Textbook "typical" values (lapse rates, albedo, eddy coefficients) are
  ensemble or mid-range values and vary spatially and seasonally.
- GCM and reanalysis products are not textbooks; they are datasets with
  documented uncertainty, catalogued in
  [module 26-05](../05-open-datasets-catalogue/03-datasets-climate.md).
- Copyrighted works are cited only.
