# Module 26-04 — Annotated Textbook Bibliography

**Stages:** advanced → research · **Prerequisites:** none (lookup resource)

Eight annotated lists of the textbooks, monographs, standards and landmark papers
behind this curriculum. Each entry says what the work is for, at what level it is
written, and — just as usefully — what it is *not* for. The purpose is to stop a
reader from buying three books that teach the same thing and missing the one
that answers their question.

Every list is deliberately split by *role* rather than by alphabet: theory,
practice, and data or standards documentation. A gravity textbook, a survey
handbook and an EPSG registry entry are three different kinds of authority, and
conflating them is the commonest source of an over-strong claim.

## Honesty rules used in this module

| Rule | Consequence |
|---|---|
| Real works only | No author, title, journal or publisher is invented |
| No fabricated identifiers | DOIs, URLs and journal volume/page numbers are omitted rather than guessed |
| Editions marked as indicative | The entry says so; verify the edition and year in a library catalogue |
| No reproduced text | These are citations and short original annotations, not excerpts |
| Opinion labelled | Statements of level and usefulness are this repository's opinion |

## How to cite from this module

1. Take the author and title from here; they are stable.
2. **Verify the edition and year** from a library catalogue or the publisher.
3. For journal papers, verify volume and pages from the journal index — this
   module deliberately does not supply them.
4. For standards and registries, cite the code, the version and the access date.
5. For software, cite the version, not merely the project name.

## Mastery checkpoint

This module has no checkpoint. The skill it supports is discriminating between
kinds of source: a textbook establishes what is known, a monograph establishes
what is argued, a standard establishes what is agreed, and a dataset
establishes what was measured. Citing the wrong kind for the wrong claim is a
reviewer-visible error.

## Common pitfalls across this module

1. **Citing a textbook for a measurement.** Textbooks give typical values; a
   specific site needs a specific reference.
2. **Ignoring the edition.** Material moves between editions; page references
   break silently.
3. **Treating an edited volume as a single authority.** Each chapter has its own
   author, and they disagree.
4. **Confusing open access with public domain.** Some works listed here are
   freely readable but still copyrighted; cite, do not copy.
5. **Using a pre-1990 book for numerical practice.** The concepts survive; the
   algorithms and the data do not.
