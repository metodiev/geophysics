# Annotated Bibliography: Seismology and Seismic Exploration

Seismology splits, in practice, into global and exploration traditions with
different notation and different problems. This page keeps the two apart while
noting where they share the same physics. A short list of landmark papers is
included, because some results are better learned from the original than from a
textbook summary.

## How to use this page

Read the "use it for" column as a statement about the reader, not about the
book: a graduate monograph and an exploration handbook can both be excellent and
still not be interchangeable. Papers are listed separately, with an explicit
warning that page numbers and volumes must be verified.

**Honesty statement about citations.** Every work below is a real publication.
No author, title, journal or year is invented. Bibliographic details of journal
articles (volume, pages) are deliberately **omitted** rather than guessed:
verify them in the journal's own index before citing. Editions of books are
indicative; confirm the edition you are using.

*Provenance:* bibliographic reference only. No copyrighted text is reproduced.

## Global seismology: theory and reference texts

| Work | Level | Use it for | Not for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Aki & Richards**, *Quantitative Seismology* (University Science Books; 2nd ed. 2002) | graduate | The standard theoretical reference: elasticity, ray theory, surface waves, normal modes, source representation. Its notation list is the de facto convention | Data processing; it is theory |
| **Dahlen & Tromp**, *Theoretical Global Seismology* (Princeton University Press; 1998) | research | Normal modes, free oscillations, the theory of the Earth's gravest vibrations, and asymptotic methods | A first course; assumes advanced mathematics |
| **Kennett**, *The Seismic Wavefield* (Cambridge University Press; two volumes, 2001 and 2002) | research | Propagation in stratified media and the interpretation of seismograms, by the author of the standard 1-D reference models | Exploration processing |
| **Ben-Menahem & Singh**, *Seismic Waves and Sources* (Springer; 1981) | research | Comprehensive Green's-function and source-theory treatment | Modern computational practice |
| **Chapman**, *Fundamentals of Seismic Wave Propagation* (Cambridge University Press; 2004) | advanced | A carefully paced derivation from elastodynamics to ray theory and WKBJ methods, with exercises | Data interpretation |
| **Červený**, *Seismic Ray Theory* (Cambridge University Press; 2001) | research | The definitive account of ray methods, including anisotropy and inhomogeneous media | Full-waveform methods |
| **Shearer**, *Introduction to Seismology* (Cambridge University Press; 3rd ed. 2019) | intermediate | The best single bridge from undergraduate physics to research seismology: rays, travel times, phases, tomography, source parameters | Exploration-scale acquisition design |
| **Stein & Wysession**, *An Introduction to Seismology, Earthquakes, and Earth Structure* (Blackwell; 2003) | intermediate | Physical understanding of waves, sources and Earth structure, with strong geological context | Detailed mathematical derivations |
| **Lay & Wallace**, *Modern Global Seismology* (Academic Press; 1995) | advanced | The classic modern reference for Earth structure, phases, magnitudes and the Earth's interior | Recent developments in ambient-noise and adjoint methods |

## Seismological practice, data and observatory standards

| Work | Level | Use it for | Not for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Bormann (ed.)**, *New Manual of Seismological Observatory Practice* (IASPEI; various dates and revisions) | advanced | The standard reference for instrument response, station metadata, magnitude determination and observatory practice | Theory of wave propagation |
| **Havskov & Ottemöller**, *Routine Data Processing in Earthquake Seismology* (Springer; 2010) | intermediate | Practical processing: picking, location, magnitude computation, with software-oriented workflows | Advanced source theory |
| **Kulhánek**, *Anatomy of Seismograms* (Elsevier; 1990) | intermediate | Recognising phases and artefacts on real records | Quantitative analysis |
| **Udías**, *Principles of Seismology* (Cambridge University Press; 1999, later editions exist) | advanced | A well-balanced text with good historical framing and clear derivations | Exploration-scale methods |
| **Kanamori & Brodsky**, "The physics of earthquakes", *Reports on Progress in Physics* (2004) | research | A readable review tying source physics to observation and to friction laws | A textbook substitute |

## Seismic exploration: acquisition, processing and interpretation

| Work | Level | Use it for | Not for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Sheriff & Geldart**, *Exploration Seismology* (Cambridge University Press; 2nd ed. 1995) | advanced | The classic reference for acquisition, theory and interpretation, with an extensive glossary of terms | Modern imaging algorithms |
| **Yilmaz**, *Seismic Data Analysis* (SEG; 2001, two volumes) | advanced | The reference work on processing: deconvolution, velocity analysis, migration and their practical pitfalls | Wave-theory proofs |
| **Telford, Geldart & Sheriff**, *Applied Geophysics* (Cambridge University Press; 2nd ed. 1990) | intermediate | The broadest single-volume survey of applied methods, including seismic, gravity, magnetics, electrical and radiometrics | Depth in any one method |
| **Dobrin & Savit**, *Introduction to Geophysical Prospecting* (McGraw-Hill; 4th ed. 1988) | intermediate | Field practice, survey design and the reasoning behind conventional processing | Modern computational imaging |
| **Kearey, Brooks & Hill**, *An Introduction to Geophysical Exploration* (Blackwell; 3rd ed. 2002) | beginner–intermediate | A gentle, well-organised undergraduate introduction across all exploration methods | Research-level detail |
| **Claerbout**, *Imaging the Earth's Interior* (Blackwell; 1985) | advanced | The conceptual origin of much of modern imaging: the imaging condition, wavefield extrapolation, downward continuation | Modern implementations, but the ideas remain current |
| **Ikelle & Amundsen**, *Introduction to Petroleum Seismology* (SEG; 2005) | advanced | Modern exploration theory with attention to anisotropy and to acquisition geometries | Interpretation workflow |
| **Vermeer**, *3-D Seismic Survey Design* (SEG; 2002) | advanced | Why survey geometry, binning and fold determine what can be imaged | Processing algorithms |
| **Chopra & Marfurt**, *Seismic Attributes for Prospect Identification and Reservoir Characterization* (SEG; 2007) | advanced | Attribute computation and, importantly, when an attribute is misleading | Underlying wave theory |
| **Robein**, *Seismic Imaging: A Review of the Techniques, their Principles, Merits and Limitations* (EAGE; 2010) | advanced | A compact review of the imaging family, with honest discussion of limitations | Step-by-step processing recipes |
| **Ashcroft**, *A Petroleum Geologist's Guide to Seismic Reflection* (Wiley-Blackwell; 2011) | beginner–intermediate | The interpreter's view of what a seismic section does and does not show; excellent for non-specialists | Acquisition or processing detail |
| **Lines & Newrick**, *Fundamentals of Geophysical Interpretation* (SEG; 2004) | intermediate | Practical interpretation exercise material | Theory |

## Landmark papers, and why they are still worth reading

| Work | Contribution | Caution |
|---|---|---|
| **Aki (1969)**, "Analysis of the seismic coda of local earthquakes as scattered waves", *Journal of Geophysical Research* | The scattering interpretation of coda and the coda-normalisation idea | Verify volume and pages before citing |
| **Kanamori (1977)**, "The energy release in great earthquakes", *Journal of Geophysical Research* | Connected radiated energy to earthquake size on a physical footing | Verify bibliographic details |
| **Hanks & Kanamori (1979)**, "A moment magnitude scale", *Journal of Geophysical Research* | The definition of $M_w$ and the reason it does not saturate | Short paper; read it whole |
| **Dziewonski & Anderson (1981)**, "Preliminary reference Earth model", *Physics of the Earth and Planetary Interiors* | The reference 1-D model against which anomalies are measured | Superseded in detail, indispensable as a reference frame |
| **Backus & Gilbert (1967–1968)**, papers on the formalism for geophysical inverse problems, *Geophysical Journal of the Royal Astronomical Society* | Resolution and trade-off analysis, still the conceptual basis of resolution matrices | Notation is dense |
| **Claerbout (1971)**, "Toward a unified theory of reflector mapping", *Geophysics* | Made the imaging condition the centre of migration | Historical notation |
| **Tarantola (1984)**, "Inversion of seismic reflection data in the acoustic approximation", *Geophysics* | The starting point of modern full-waveform inversion | Assumes a linearised, acoustic world |
| **Kennett & Engdahl (1991)**, "Traveltimes for global earthquake location and phase identification", *Geophysical Journal International* | The travel-time tables behind routine location | Verify edition of the tables used in software |
| **Wessel & Smith (1998)**, announcement of the Generic Mapping Tools, *EOS, Transactions AGU* | The plotting and mapping toolchain used across this curriculum | Check the current version in the software's own documentation |
| **Crotwell, Owens & Ritsema (1999)**, "TauP: flexible seismic travel-time and ray-path utilities", *Seismological Research Letters* | The algorithm behind the TauP calculators used in this curriculum; the DOI is registered in [tools/verified_links.tsv](../../tools/verified_links.tsv) | — |

## How to cite these works

Textbooks: author, title, edition, publisher, year. Journal articles: author,
year, title, journal, volume, pages. This page supplies the first four items for
papers and deliberately omits volume and pages, because those are the details
most easily misremembered. Take them from the journal index.

## Assumptions and regime of validity

- Exploration and global seismology use different conventions for the same
  quantities (velocity as $v$ or $V$, depth positive downward or upward, travel
  time or two-way time). Check the book's notation list.
- Comparisons of methods across books should be made on the same dataset;
  published comparison figures are almost always illustrative.
- Copyrighted material is cited, never reproduced.
