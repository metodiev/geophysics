# Symbol Handbook: Notation Used Throughout

A lookup table. Symbols are grouped by domain. Where a symbol is **overloaded**
(means different things in different subfields), that is flagged explicitly,
because symbol collisions are a real source of confusion when moving between
textbooks.

## How to use this page

Search for the symbol or the quantity. If a lesson uses a symbol not listed
here, that is a gap in this handbook — report it.

**A caution about symbols in geophysics.** The same letter frequently carries
different meanings in different subfields. Three collisions are common enough to
warn about up front:

| Symbol | Collision | Resolution |
|---|---|---|
| $k$ | wavenumber (waves) vs permeability (hydrogeology) vs thermal conductivity (heat flow) | Context, and the units: wavenumber is m⁻¹, permeability m², conductivity W m⁻¹ K⁻¹ |
| $Q$ | seismic quality factor (dimensionless) vs heat flow / heat quantity (W) vs quadrupole moment | Context. The quality factor is dimensionless, which distinguishes it immediately |
| $\rho$ | mass density (kg/m³) vs electrical resistivity (Ω·m) vs reflection coefficient | Context and units. Resistivity is sometimes written $\rho_e$ to avoid this |

## Common mistakes with notation

1. **Assuming a symbol means the same thing across subfields.** Check the table above before importing an interpretation.
2. **Confusing $\omega$ (rad/s) and $f$ (Hz).** They differ by $2\pi$; using one where the other belongs is a factor-of-6.3 error, which is large enough to make a "discovery".
3. **Confusing $\mu$ the shear modulus with $\mu$ the magnetic permeability.** Both appear in elasticity *and* electromagnetism, sometimes in the same paper on seismoelectrics.
4. **Treating $\rho$ as density in a hydrogeology paper** where it may mean resistivity. Units are the arbiter: kg/m³ vs Ω·m.
5. **Mixing $Q$ the quality factor with $Q$ heat.** The quality factor is dimensionless — if your "Q" has units, it is not a quality factor.

## Further reading

- **BIPM (2019). *The International System of Units (SI)* (9th ed.).** The
  authoritative source for unit symbols and their typography. Level: reference.
- **IUPAC (2007). *Quantities, Units and Symbols in Physical Chemistry*
  ("Green Book", 3rd ed.).** Level: reference. The best single source for
  resolving conflicting symbol conventions in the physical sciences.
- **IAGA (2010 and later). *International Geomagnetic Reference Field: the
  eleventh generation* and subsequent revisions**, in *Geophysical Journal
  International*. Level: advanced. Defines the geomagnetic symbol conventions
  used in folder 08.
- **Aki, K., & Richards, P. G. (2002). *Quantitative Seismology* (2nd ed.).**
  Level: graduate. Its notation list is the de facto standard for seismology;
  worth consulting when a seismology symbol confuses you.
