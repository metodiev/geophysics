# Glossary: T-Z

Terms beginning with **T** through **Z**. Letters X, Y and Z have few genuine
geophysical terms, so those sections include the composite entries that are
actually used in practice (for example the geomagnetic X, Y, Z components, which
are defined under *G* and cross-referenced here).

## How to use this page

Search for the term or acronym. Entries marked **overloaded** carry more than
one accepted meaning; the entry lists them, because choosing the wrong one
produces a plausible but wrong number. Values are typical or nominal, not
measurements.

*Provenance:* no measured data appear on this page. Definitions are summarised
from standard geophysical usage; no copyrighted text is reproduced.

## T

| Term | Definition | Units / typical values |
|---|---|---|
| Temperature gradient | Rate of change of temperature with depth or height; the driving quantity for heat flow, computed as the product of gradient and thermal conductivity. | K/m or °C/km |
| Tensor | Quantity whose components transform according to a definite rule under rotation: stress, strain and the elasticity tensor are the geophysical standbys. | Component-dependent |
| Terrain correction | Gravity correction for the excess or deficit of mass caused by topography near the station relative to an infinite slab. Always positive, and significant in hilly terrain. | mGal |
| Thermal conductivity | Ability of a material to conduct heat: $q = -k\nabla T$. | W m⁻¹ K⁻¹; rocks typically 1.5–4 (nominal) |
| Thermal diffusivity | Ratio of conductivity to volumetric heat capacity, $\kappa = k/(\rho c_p)$, governing the speed at which temperature changes propagate. | m²/s; crustal values of order 10⁻⁶ (nominal) |
| Thermal expansivity | Fractional volume change per kelvin of temperature change; the driver of thermal convection. | K⁻¹; mantle silicates of order 10⁻⁵ (nominal) |
| Thermal inertia | Property controlling the amplitude and phase of the surface-temperature cycle's penetration: $\sqrt{k\rho c_p}$. | J m⁻² K⁻¹ s⁻¹ᐟ² |
| Thermal remanent magnetisation (TRM) | Magnetisation acquired as a rock cools through its Curie temperature; the recorder of the paleomagnetic field. | A/m |
| Thermocline | Layer of strong vertical temperature gradient separating surface from deep ocean; it suppresses vertical mixing and is a key feature in acoustic propagation and in climate dynamics. | °C per m; depth in m |
| Thrust fault | Contractional fault with shallow dip in which the hanging wall moves up and over the footwall. | Dip in degrees |
| Tide gauge | Instrument recording sea level relative to a fixed local benchmark; its record contains the ocean tide, storm surge, and vertical land motion, which must be separated. | m relative to the gauge datum; rate in mm/yr |
| Tidal correction | Gravity correction for the solid-Earth tide and, in marine work, the ocean tide and the platform's response to it. | mGal or µGal |
| Tide (solid Earth, ocean) | Periodic deformation of the solid Earth or of the ocean surface in response to the tide-generating potential of the Moon and Sun; the solid-Earth tide can move the ground by tens of centimetres. | Displacement in m; potential in m²/s² |
| Time-lapse (4D) seismic | Repeated seismic surveys over the same area, differenced to image changes caused by production, injection or storage. Repeated, not absolute, measurements are what is compared, so repeatability sets the detection limit. | Difference amplitude relative to baseline |
| Time series | Sequence of values indexed by time; its analysis involves stationarity, sampling, and the assumption that the record is representative of the process. | Same units as the sampled quantity |
| Tomography | Imaging of a medium from many travel-time or amplitude measurements along crossing paths; local, regional, global and ambient-noise variants differ in data and in resolution. | Velocity in m/s; anomalies in % of a reference model |
| Topography | Surface relief above a datum; its mass must be accounted for in precise gravity work and its shape affects seismic station response. | m |
| Total field | In magnetics, the magnitude of the field vector, measured by a scalar magnetometer such as a proton-precession or optical-pump instrument. Anomalies are usually tiny fractions of the total field. | nT |
| Trace | A single recorded channel of seismic data, typically for one source–receiver pair. | Amplitude in instrument counts after scaling; time in s |
| Transfer function | Frequency-domain description of a linear system's response. **Overloaded**: instrument response, site response, and MT impedance are all sometimes called transfer functions; state which. | Complex; frequency in Hz |
| Transient EM (TDEM) | Electromagnetic method that switches a transmitter current off and measures the decaying secondary field, giving broad depth coverage in a single sounding. | Time in ms or µs; field derivative in V/m² or V/(A m²) |
| Transmissivity | Product of hydraulic conductivity and saturated thickness, the aquifer's ability to transmit water. | m²/s |
| Transmission coefficient | Fraction of amplitude (or energy, state which) transmitted across an interface; complementary to the reflection coefficient in the amplitude sense at normal incidence. | Dimensionless |
| Transverse isotropy | Anisotropy with a single symmetry axis, the natural model for finely layered rock (VTI for a vertical axis, HTI for horizontal). | Thomsen parameters dimensionless |
| Travel time | Time for a wave to travel from a source to a receiver. Its reciprocal, the slowness, is often the more convenient variable. | s (or two-way time in reflection work) |
| Tremor | Continuous, low-amplitude seismic signal, distinguished from earthquakes by its lack of impulsive onset; tectonic and volcanic tremor have distinct frequency content. | Amplitude in m/s; frequency in Hz |
| Triaxial (seismometer) | Three-component sensor recording two horizontal and one vertical axis, which is the minimum needed to resolve particle motion direction. | Three channels in m/s |
| Tsunami | Long-wavelength ocean wave generated by displacement of the seafloor or by a mass entering the water; its deep-ocean wavelengths make it barely detectable at sea but strongly amplified in shallow water. | Wavelength in km; run-up in m |
| Tube wave | Stoneley-type wave guided along a borehole fluid column; used in VSP work but also a source of noise. | Speed in m/s |
| Turbulence | Chaotic, three-dimensional, dissipative fluid motion that transports heat, momentum and tracers far more efficiently than molecular diffusion. | Dimensionless measures: Reynolds, Richardson numbers |
| Two-way time (TWT) | Vertical time for a wave to travel down and back from a reflector; the depth axis of most reflection seismic data. Converting TWT to depth requires a velocity model, which is itself uncertain. | s |

## U

| Term | Definition | Units / typical values |
|---|---|---|
| Uncertainty | Quantified doubt in a value, separated into *random* (reducible by repetition) and *systematic* (not). Report the coverage factor or confidence level, e.g. a standard uncertainty multiplied by $k=2$ for an approximate 95 % interval. | Same units as the value |
| Unconformity | Surface representing a gap in the geological record, often a strong and laterally extensive seismic reflector because of the impedance contrast it produces. | Time in s or depth in m |
| Upgoing, downgoing | Direction of propagation of wavefield components separated, for example, in VSP processing or in a "up-down" decomposition of ocean-bottom recordings. | Direction label |
| Uplift | Rock or surface movement upward, whether tectonic, isostatic or induced. Distinguish from exhumation, which is removal of overburden by erosion. | mm/yr |
| UTM | Universal Transverse Mercator: a global system of 60 transverse Mercator zones covering 6° of longitude each, with defined scale factor and false easting. Handy for local surveys, but each zone has its own coordinates. | Eastings/northings in m |
| Upward continuation | Stable extrapolation of a potential field to a higher level, used for regional–residual separation and to compare surveys flown at different heights. | Elevation change in m |
| Undulation (geoid) | Height of the geoid above the reference ellipsoid, $N$; needed to convert between GNSS (ellipsoidal) and orthometric heights. | m |

## V

| Term | Definition | Units / typical values |
|---|---|---|
| Vadose zone | Unsaturated zone between the ground surface and the water table, where water is held at negative pressure and where resistivity and GPR respond to moisture. | Depth in m; water content m³/m³ |
| Variance, covariance | Variance is the mean squared deviation from the mean; covariance measures joint variation of two quantities. The covariance matrix is the core object of least-squares and Bayesian inversion. | units² |
| Variogram | Function describing how the dissimilarity between samples grows with separation distance; the input to kriging. | units² versus m |
| Velocity | **Overloaded in processing.** Interval velocity is a layer property; RMS and stacking velocities are parameters that flatten reflections; phase and group velocity describe dispersion. Always state which. | m/s |
| Velocity analysis | Estimation of stacking or migration velocities from semblance or from travel-time curvature, usually performed on CMP gathers. | m/s; semblance dimensionless in [0, 1] |
| Vertical seismic profile (VSP) | Seismic recording with receivers down a borehole and a source at the surface, giving a direct measurement of the downgoing wavefield, of velocity with depth, and of the seismic-to-well tie. | Depth in m; time in s |
| Viscosity | **Overloaded.** Dynamic viscosity $\eta$ is the constant in Newton's law of viscous stress; kinematic viscosity $\nu = \eta/\rho$. Mantle viscosities are nominal by orders of magnitude and are inferred, not measured. | Pa·s (dynamic); m²/s (kinematic) |
| Void ratio | Ratio of void volume to solid volume; related to porosity by $e = \phi/(1-\phi)$. Used in geotechnical work. | Dimensionless |
| Volumetric water content | Volume of water per unit bulk volume of soil or rock; the quantity measured by most moisture sensors and by GPR velocity estimates. | m³/m³ |
| Vorticity | Curl of the velocity field; in geophysical fluid dynamics, potential vorticity is the conserved quantity that organises large-scale flow. | s⁻¹ |
| $v_p/v_s$ ratio | Ratio of P- to S-wave speed, diagnostic of lithology and of fluid saturation; lithology-dependent, typically between about 1.6 and 2.0 for common rocks (nominal). | Dimensionless |

## W

| Term | Definition | Units / typical values |
|---|---|---|
| Water table | Surface at which pore-water pressure equals atmospheric pressure; the top of the saturated zone. | Depth in m; head in m |
| Water equivalent | Depth of water obtained by melting a snow or ice sample; used to compare snowpack, precipitation and glacier mass balance. | mm or m |
| Wave packet | Localised disturbance built from a band of frequencies, whose envelope travels at the group velocity. | m or s |
| Wavelength | Distance between successive crests of a wave: $\lambda = v/f$ for a monochromatic wave. Resolution arguments are almost always wavelength arguments. | m |
| Wavenumber | Number of cycles per unit distance, $k = 2\pi/\lambda$. **Overloaded** with permeability and thermal conductivity; units (m⁻¹) identify it. | m⁻¹; sometimes cycles/m (then symbol $\nu$ or $f_k$) |
| Wavefront | Surface of constant phase, normal to rays in an isotropic medium. | Geometric |
| Wavelet | **Overloaded.** (i) The source pulse or its effective form in a seismic trace; (ii) the analysing function in a wavelet transform. The two are unrelated uses of the same word. | Amplitude versus time |
| Wavelet transform | Time–frequency decomposition using scaled and shifted copies of a parent function, giving multiresolution analysis of non-stationary series. | Coefficients per scale |
| Weathering layer | Low-velocity, low-resistivity near-surface layer produced by weathering; it dominates seismic static corrections and shallow geotechnical site characterisation. | Thickness in m; velocity in m/s |
| Weighting | Assignment of unequal importance to data or to model parameters in an inversion, usually to reflect uncertainty. The weights define the norm the inversion actually minimises. | Dimensionless or inverse variance |
| Well log | Continuous downhole measurement of a physical property (natural gamma, resistivity, sonic, density, neutron, and many others), depth-indexed. | Measurement-specific (API, Ω·m, µs/ft, g/cm³) |
| Well tie | Correlation of a synthetic seismogram computed from logs with the surface seismic data, establishing the depth-to-time mapping. | Time shift in ms |
| White noise | Random signal with constant power spectral density over the band of interest; added as a stabilising term in deconvolution and in inversion. | units²/Hz |
| Wiener filter | Least-squares optimum filter for a stated objective, e.g. shaping a wavelet to a desired output or predicting and removing multiples. | Dimensionless coefficients |
| Wind stress | Tangential force per unit area exerted by wind on the ocean surface, the primary driver of surface circulation. | N/m² |
| Window, taper | Finite segment of data used for spectral or inversion purposes; the taper reduces leakage from the abrupt truncation but changes the effective resolution. | samples or s |

## X, Y, Z

| Term | Definition | Units / typical values |
|---|---|---|
| X, Y, Z (geometric) | Cartesian components of a vector; in this repository $z$ is positive downward in potential-field and hydrogeologic contexts, but many seismology texts take $z$ upward. | m |
| X, Y, Z (geomagnetic) | North, east and vertical components of the geomagnetic field vector; vertical is normally taken positive downward in geomagnetic models, so a negative $Z$ means a downward-directed field in some conventions. Check the sign convention of the model you use. | nT |
| Xenolith | Rock fragment carried to the surface by magma, providing direct samples of otherwise inaccessible deep material. | Size in cm; composition descriptive |
| Yield (explosion monitoring) | Energy release of an explosion, estimated from seismic magnitude using empirical relations that depend on emplacement conditions; estimates carry large, sometimes unstated, uncertainty. | kilotons (kt) TNT equivalent, or J |
| Younger Dryas | Rapid return to near-glacial conditions near the end of the last deglaciation, a standard example of abrupt climate change. | Time in ka |
| Zero-offset (section, stack) | Data or a stacked section displayed as if source and receiver coincided, which removes the offset dependence but also removes offset information. | None |
| Zoeppritz equations | Exact solution for plane-wave reflection and transmission coefficients at a welded interface between two elastic half-spaces, the reference against which AVO approximations are checked. | Dimensionless |
| Z-transform | Discrete-time counterpart of the Laplace transform, turning convolution into multiplication of polynomials; used in filter design and in cepstral analysis. | Dimensionless in complex variable $z$ |
| Zone of investigation | Depth range from which a method actually returns signal. It is method- and site-specific and is not the same as the maximum depth of a model grid. | m |
