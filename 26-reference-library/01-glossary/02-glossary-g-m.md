# Glossary: G-M

Terms beginning with **G** through **M**, alphabetically within each letter.
Definitions are working definitions for this repository: enough to resolve a word
you met, not enough to teach the subject.

## How to use this page

Search for the term or acronym. Where a term is **overloaded** — *impedance*,
*magnitude*, *moment*, *moveout* — that is stated in the entry, because reading
the wrong meaning silently produces a correct-looking calculation.

Numerical values are marked as **typical** or **nominal**. Typical means "a
value you may see for a general setting in a textbook"; it is not a
measurement and must not be cited as one for a specific site.

*Provenance:* no measured data appear on this page. Definitions are summarised
from standard geophysical usage; no copyrighted text is reproduced.

## G

| Term | Definition | Units / typical values |
|---|---|---|
| Geodesy | The science of measuring and representing the shape, gravity field and rotation of the Earth, including the definition of coordinate and height systems. | None |
| Geodetic datum | The reference ellipsoid, origin and tie points on which coordinates in a given system depend. Two datums give different coordinates for the same physical point, so a datum must be stated with every coordinate. | Parameters in m and dimensionless flattening |
| Geoid | The equipotential surface of the Earth's gravity field that best fits mean sea level, extended under the continents. It is a physical surface, not an ellipsoid, and it undulates relative to the ellipsoid by roughly −100 m to +100 m globally (typical range; the exact value varies with the model). | Geoid undulation $N$ in m |
| Geomagnetic elements | The field is described by seven related quantities: north $X$, east $Y$, vertical $Z$, horizontal $H$, total intensity $F$, declination $D$ and inclination $I$. | $X,Y,Z,H,F$ in nT; $D,I$ in degrees |
| Geophone | Sensor that converts ground velocity or acceleration into a voltage, usually a moving coil (velocity) or piezo/silicon (acceleration). | Sensitivity in V/(m/s); natural frequency in Hz |
| Geothermal gradient | Rate of increase of temperature with depth. Continental **typical** values are near 25–30 °C/km, but the range across settings is large and regional heat flow must be measured, not assumed. | °C/km or K/km |
| Geotherm | Temperature-versus-depth profile, either measured or computed from a heat-flow model. | °C or K versus km |
| Geothermometry | Estimation of past temperatures from mineral compositions or from borehole temperature profiles (a paleoclimate application). | °C |
| Glacial isostatic adjustment (GIA) | Ongoing vertical and horizontal motion of the solid Earth in response to loading and unloading by ice sheets, including the present-day rebound of formerly glaciated regions. | mm/yr |
| Glacier | Persistent body of ice, moving under its own weight, that records and modifies climate and leaves geomorphic and isostatic signals. | Thickness in m; mass balance in m of water equivalent per year |
| GNSS | Global Navigation Satellite Systems (GPS, GLONASS, Galileo, BeiDou): satellite constellations used for positioning and for measuring ground motion, water vapour and ionospheric delay. | Position in m; velocities in mm/yr |
| GPR | Ground-penetrating radar: reflection profiling using radio-frequency (typically 10 MHz–2 GHz) electromagnetic pulses. Penetration falls sharply in wet, conductive ground. | Frequency in MHz; velocity in m/ns |
| Gradient | Vector of partial derivatives, $\nabla$, giving the direction and magnitude of steepest increase of a scalar field. | Units of the field per metre |
| Grain density | Density of the solid mineral grains, excluding pore space. Contrast with bulk density. | kg/m³; typical mineral value near 2650–2700 for quartz-rich rock |
| Gravimeter | Instrument measuring gravity. *Relative* meters measure differences between stations; *absolute* meters measure $g$ directly; superconducting meters record continuous time variations. | Reading in mGal or µGal |
| Gravitational constant | Newtonian constant of gravitation $G$. The current CODATA value is $6.674\,30(15)\times10^{-11}$ m³ kg⁻¹ s⁻²; the relative uncertainty is about $2\times10^{-5}$, making $G$ the least precisely known fundamental constant. | $6.674\,30(15)\times10^{-11}$ m³ kg⁻¹ s⁻² |
| Gravity anomaly | Difference between observed gravity (reduced to a datum) and normal gravity on the reference ellipsoid. Always state which reduction: free-air, Bouguer, isostatic, or a residual after regional removal. | mGal |
| Gravity gradient | Derivative of gravity with respect to position; the gradient tensor has independent components that are measured by gradiometers and used in airborne and satellite surveys. | E (1 E = 10⁻⁹ s⁻²) for gradients; mGal/m |
| Green's function | Response of a linear system to a unit impulse. If the Green's function is known, the response to any source follows by convolution. | Units of the response per unit source |
| Grid versus mesh | *Grid* usually means a regular array of values or cells (raster); *mesh* usually means a computational discretisation, often irregular. The two are not the same object. | Cell size in m |
| Ground motion | Shaking of the ground, described by displacement, velocity or acceleration time series or by their response spectra. | m, m/s, m/s²; response spectrum in m/s² or g |
| Groundwater | Water stored in and moving through the saturated zone of the subsurface. | Volume in m³; head in m |
| Group velocity | Speed at which the envelope of a wave packet travels, $v_g = d\omega/dk$; distinct from the phase velocity $v = \omega/k$. It is the phase velocity that is measured by tracking a single crest, and the two differ whenever there is dispersion. | m/s |
| Gutenberg–Richter relation | Empirical frequency–magnitude relation for earthquake populations, $\log_{10}N = a - bM$. The $b$-value is dimensionless and close to 1 for many regional catalogues, but it varies with tectonic setting and catalogue completeness. | $b$ dimensionless; $N$ is a count |
| Gyre | Large-scale closed ocean circulation pattern; the subtropical gyres are wind-driven. | Transport in Sv (1 Sv = 10⁶ m³/s) |

## H

| Term | Definition | Units / typical values |
|---|---|---|
| Half-space | Idealised uniform medium extending to infinity in one direction. The homogeneous half-space is the reference model of most first-pass interpretations in DC resistivity, refraction, and heat flow. | Resistivity in Ω·m; speed in m/s |
| Harmonic (spherical) | Term of a spherical-harmonic expansion, characterised by degree $n$ and order $m$; used to describe the global gravity and magnetic fields. | Coefficients dimensionless or in nT for the field |
| Heat capacity | Energy required to raise the temperature of a body by one kelvin. Specific heat capacity is per unit mass. | J/K; specific: J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹ |
| Heat flow | Heat flux density through a surface. Continental and oceanic **typical** values are of order 50–100 mW/m², with wide variation; measure rather than assume for a specific region. | mW/m² (= 10⁻³ W/m²) |
| Heat production | Rate at which radiogenic heating releases energy per unit volume, dominated by decay of U, Th and K in crustal rocks. | µW/m³ |
| Height (orthometric, ellipsoidal) | Orthometric height is measured above the geoid along the plumb line; ellipsoidal height is measured above the reference ellipsoid. They differ by the geoid undulation, so a GNSS height is not a sea-level height. | m |
| Heterogeneity versus anisotropy | *Heterogeneity*: properties vary from point to point. *Anisotropy*: properties vary with direction at a point. Mixing them up invalidates inversions that depend on the distinction. | Dimensionless descriptors |
| Hooke's law | Linear relation between stress and strain in an elastic solid, $\sigma_{ij} = C_{ijkl}\varepsilon_{kl}$. Valid only for small strains and below the elastic limit. | Pa |
| Hot spot | Long-lived volcanism that is not explained by plate-boundary processes, commonly interpreted as the surface expression of a mantle plume. | None |
| HVSR (horizontal-to-vertical spectral ratio) | Ratio of horizontal to vertical spectral amplitude of ambient vibration at a single station, whose peak frequency is used to estimate site resonance. The amplitude is not a direct site-amplification factor, despite frequent misuse. | Ratio dimensionless; frequency in Hz |
| Hydraulic conductivity | Constant of proportionality in Darcy's law relating specific discharge to hydraulic gradient; depends on both the medium and the fluid. | m/s |
| Hydraulic diffusivity | Ratio of transmissivity to storativity, describing how pressure disturbances propagate in an aquifer. | m²/s |
| Hydraulic head | Sum of pressure head and elevation head, expressed as an equivalent height; the driving potential in Darcy's law. | m |
| Hydrograph | Time series of stream discharge or water level, used to infer catchment response and recharge processes. | m³/s or m |
| Hyperbolic (moveout) | Shape of the reflection travel-time curve in a constant-velocity medium, $t^2 = t_0^2 + x^2/v^2$. In a layered earth the curve is approximated by the root-mean-square velocity. | s; velocity in m/s |
| Hysteresis | Dependence of a state on its history: the soil-moisture characteristic curve and the magnetisation curve are the two standard examples in geophysics. | Depends; magnetic hysteresis in A/m versus T |

## I

| Term | Definition | Units / typical values |
|---|---|---|
| IGRF | International Geomagnetic Reference Field: a series of spherical-harmonic models of the main geomagnetic field, revised every five years, with predictive secular-variation coefficients. The WMM is a related operational model used for navigation. | Coefficients in nT; field in nT |
| Ill-posed problem | Inverse problem in which the solution does not exist, is not unique, or does not depend continuously on the data. Regularisation is how ill-posedness is managed. | None |
| Impedance | **Overloaded.** (i) Mechanical or acoustic impedance $Z = \rho v$; (ii) electrical impedance, the complex ratio of voltage to current; (iii) magnetotelluric impedance, the complex tensor relating electric and magnetic field components. The unit tells you which: kg m⁻² s⁻¹, Ω, or Ω respectively. | See above |
| Inclination (magnetic) | Angle of the field vector below the horizontal; positive downward. Together with declination it defines the field direction. | Degrees |
| Induced polarisation (IP) | Temporary storage of charge in a rock when current is applied, measured as a decaying voltage after current shutoff (time domain) or as a phase shift (frequency domain). | Chargeability in mV/V; phase in mrad |
| Induction number | Dimensionless group comparing the size of a conductor with the electromagnetic skin depth; small values justify low-frequency approximations. | Dimensionless |
| Infiltration | Downward entry of water into soil from the surface. | mm/h or m/s |
| InSAR | Interferometric synthetic aperture radar: measurement of ground displacement along the line of sight from the phase difference between radar images. | Displacement in mm; phase in radians |
| Insolation | Solar radiation incident on a surface. | W/m² |
| Interferometry | Extraction of information from the relative phase of two wavefields. In seismology, ambient-noise interferometry recovers an estimate of the Green's function between two receivers from long recordings of noise. | Phase in radians; travel times in s |
| Interpolation | Estimation of values between known samples, as distinct from extrapolation beyond them. The choice of method encodes assumptions about smoothness. | Same units as the sampled field |
| Inversion | Estimation of model parameters from data. The geophysical inverse problem is nearly always underdetermined and noisy, so a solution is a statement about a chosen misfit, a chosen prior and a chosen parametrisation. | Model parameters carry their own units |
| Isostasy | State in which the Earth's lithosphere floats on the weaker mantle, so that pressure at depth is equalised over broad regions. Airy (variable crustal thickness) and Pratt (variable density) are the two textbook models. | Mass deficit in kg/m² |
| Isotropic | Having the same properties in all directions. Contrast with anisotropic. | Dimensionless descriptor |

## J

| Term | Definition | Units / typical values |
|---|---|---|
| Jacobian | Matrix of partial derivatives of data with respect to model parameters, $J_{ij} = \partial d_i/\partial m_j$; the discrete form of the sensitivity operator. | Units of data per unit of model parameter |
| Joint inversion | Simultaneous inversion of two or more datasets for a shared model, exploiting the fact that different physics constrain different aspects of the same subsurface. | None |
| Joule | SI unit of energy: 1 J = 1 N·m = 1 kg m²/s². | J |
| Julian day | Continuous count of days, used to avoid date ambiguities in long time series. | days |

## K

| Term | Definition | Units / typical values |
|---|---|---|
| Kappa | High-frequency spectral decay parameter of ground motion at a site, empirical and site-specific. **Overloaded**: $\kappa$ also denotes thermal or hydraulic diffusivity in other contexts. | s (seismological $\kappa$) |
| Karst | Landscape and aquifer formed by dissolution of soluble rock, with conduits that violate the continuum assumptions of standard flow models. | None |
| Kernel | Function weighting which parts of a model affect which data. In potential fields the kernel is the spatial sensitivity; in a matrix formulation it is the row of the sensitivity matrix. | Depends |
| K index | Three-hourly index of geomagnetic disturbance at a single observatory. The planetary equivalent is $K_p$; derived indices include $a_p$ and $A_p$. | Dimensionless (K); nT (a_p, A_p) |
| Kirchhoff migration | Migration implemented by summing seismic energy along travel-time curves computed from a velocity model. Handles steep dips and large velocity contrasts well, but has a poorer amplitude behaviour than wave-equation methods. | None |
| Kriging | Geostatistical interpolation that minimises the estimation variance given a modelled spatial covariance (variogram). | Same units as the field; variance in units squared |
| Kriging variance | Estimated squared error of a kriged value; a property of the sampling geometry, not of the data values. | units² |

## L

| Term | Definition | Units / typical values |
|---|---|---|
| Laplace's equation | $\nabla^2 V = 0$; satisfied by a potential in a source-free region, which is why potential fields can be continued analytically. | None |
| Laplacian | Second-order differential operator $\nabla^2$, the divergence of the gradient. | Units of the field per m² |
| Lapse rate | Rate at which temperature falls with height in the atmosphere. The dry adiabatic lapse rate is about 9.8 K/km; the moist rate is smaller because latent heat is released. | K/km |
| Latent heat | Energy absorbed or released per unit mass during a phase change, for example of water vapour condensation. | J/kg |
| Larmor frequency | Precession frequency of a nuclear spin in a magnetic field, $f = \gamma B/2\pi$; the basis of proton-precession magnetometers and of borehole nuclear magnetic resonance. | Hz; proton gyromagnetic ratio $\gamma_p \approx 42.58$ MHz/T |
| Lithology | Description of rock type and composition as observed in core, cuttings or logs. | None |
| Lithosphere | Outer rigid shell that behaves elastically on geological timescales: crust plus uppermost mantle. Its *effective elastic thickness* is often less than its seismological thickness. | Thickness in km; $T_e$ in km |
| Lithostatic pressure | Pressure due to the weight of overlying rock, $\rho g z$ for constant density; sets the background stress state that pore pressure modifies. | Pa |
| Local magnitude ($M_L$) | "Richter" magnitude, defined from the maximum amplitude recorded by a specified Wood–Anderson instrument at a stated distance. Saturation above about magnitude 6 makes it unusable for large events. | Dimensionless |
| Love number | Dimensionless coefficient ($h$, $k$, $l$) describing the body-tide and load response of the Earth; used to convert between tidal potential, displacement and gravity change. | Dimensionless |
| Love wave | Horizontally polarised surface wave in which particle motion is transverse and horizontal; requires a layered or laterally varying structure to exist. | Speed in m/s |
| Low-velocity zone | Depth interval in which wave speed is lower than above it; classically the asthenosphere, where partial melting and temperature effects dominate. | Depth in km; velocity in m/s |
| Lag | Time shift between two series at which a cross-correlation or cross-covariance is evaluated; the lag of maximum correlation is an estimate of the time offset between them. | s (or samples) |

## M

| Term | Definition | Units / typical values |
|---|---|---|
| Magnetic anomaly | Difference between the observed magnetic field and a modelled reference field (usually IGRF) after removal of a stated datum or trend. | nT |
| Magnetic field strength versus flux density | In SI, $H$ is magnetic field strength (A/m) and $B$ is magnetic flux density (T); they are related by $B = \mu H$. Geophysics normally measures and maps $B$, in nT. | $H$ in A/m; $B$ in T or nT |
| Magnetic permeability | Constant relating flux density to field strength; the vacuum value is $\mu_0 \approx 1.2566\times10^{-6}$ H/m. | H/m |
| Magnetic susceptibility | Degree to which a material becomes magnetised in an applied field, $\mathbf{M} = \chi \mathbf{H}$. **Convention trap:** the SI value is dimensionless, whereas the CGS value is larger by a factor $4\pi$ and is often quoted without units. | Dimensionless (SI) |
| Magnetisation | Magnetic dipole moment per unit volume. *Induced* magnetisation is proportional to the applied field; *remanent* magnetisation persists after the field is removed and is a geological recording. | A/m |
| Magnetotellurics (MT) | Passive electromagnetic method using natural time variations of the magnetic field to estimate the electrical conductivity of the subsurface, via a frequency-dependent impedance. | Impedance in Ω; period in s |
| Magnitude | **Overloaded.** A family of dimensionless logarithmic scales: local ($M_L$), body-wave ($m_b$), surface-wave ($M_s$), moment ($M_w$). They agree imperfectly, and only $M_w$ continues to be meaningful for the largest events. Never write "magnitude" without the scale. | Dimensionless |
| Mantle | Silicate shell between the crust and the core, some 2900 km thick (nominal); convecting on geological timescales and the source of most magmatism. | Depth in km |
| Mantle plume | Upwelling of anomalously hot mantle, hypothesised to explain hot spots and large igneous provinces; its existence and form remain debated in detail. | None |
| Marine gravity | Gravity measured from a ship or from satellite altimetry. Shipborne data need Eötvös and other platform corrections; altimetric gravity is derived from sea-surface slope. | mGal |
| Maxwell's equations | Four equations relating electric and magnetic fields to charges and currents; the starting point for every electromagnetic method and for GPR. | Fields in V/m and T |
| Mean versus median | The mean is the arithmetic average and is sensitive to outliers; the median is the middle value and is robust. Report which one you use, along with a measure of spread. | Same units as the data |
| Median filter | Nonlinear filter that replaces each sample by the median of a window; effective at removing spikes without the ringing a linear filter introduces. | Same units as the data |
| Mercator projection | Conformal cylindrical projection preserving local shape; exaggerates area strongly at high latitude, so it must not be used for global area comparison. | None |
| Mesh | Computational discretisation of a domain: structured (regular grid) or unstructured (triangles, tetrahedra, Voronoi cells). | Element size in m |
| Migration | Seismic imaging operation that moves reflection energy to its true subsurface position and collapses diffractions, using a velocity model. | None |
| Misfit (objective) function | Scalar measure of disagreement between observed and predicted data, minimised by an inversion. Its definition (L2, L1, correlation, Wasserstein) determines which data features dominate the solution. | units² or normalised |
| Moho | Mohorovičić discontinuity, the seismic boundary between crust and mantle, defined by the jump in P-wave speed. | Depth in km; jump of order 1 km/s (typical) |
| Moment magnitude ($M_w$) | Magnitude computed from the seismic moment: $M_w = \tfrac{2}{3}(\log_{10}M_0 - 9.1)$ with $M_0$ in N·m. It does not saturate. | Dimensionless |
| Moment tensor | Symmetric $3\times3$ tensor (six independent components) describing the equivalent force system of a seismic source. Its decomposition into isotropic, double-couple and compensated linear vector dipole parts is the standard interpretive step. | N·m |
| Moveout | **Overloaded.** Change of arrival time with source–receiver offset (normal moveout, NMO); also change of travel time with dip (dip moveout, DMO). | s |
| Multi-channel analysis of surface waves (MASW) | Method that measures surface-wave dispersion from a linear array and inverts it for a 1-D shear-speed profile. | Velocity in m/s; frequency in Hz |
| Multiple | Seismic energy that has reflected more than once before reaching the receiver, from the surface, the seafloor, or an internal boundary. Multiples obscure primaries and are suppressed by deconvolution or radon filtering. | Arrival times in s |
| Muting | Deliberate zeroing of parts of a seismic gather, for example to remove stretched far-offset energy before stacking. | None |

## Caveat

Values above are typical or nominal orders of magnitude, not site measurements.
Where a term is defined in a standards document — geoid, datum, magnitudes,
geomagnetic models — quote the responsible body's definition in publication.
