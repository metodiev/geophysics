# Glossary: N-S

Terms beginning with **N** through **S**, alphabetically within each letter.
The letter range is the largest in the glossary, because most quantities used in
practice start with P, R or S (potential, resistivity, reflection, speed,
strain, stress).

## How to use this page

Search for the term or acronym. Entries marked **overloaded** carry more than
one meaning in common use; the entry states them all rather than choosing
silently. Numerical values are typical or nominal values, not measurements.

*Provenance:* no measured data appear on this page. Definitions are summarised
from standard geophysical usage; no copyrighted text is reproduced.

## N

| Term | Definition | Units / typical values |
|---|---|---|
| Nafe–Drake curve | Empirical relation between P-wave speed and density for common crustal and mantle rocks, used to build initial models. It is a statistical summary, not a law, and it fails for ore bodies and for rocks with unusual mineralogy. | Speed in km/s; density in g/cm³ or kg/m³ |
| Nappe | Large recumbent fold sheet transported far over its substrate by thrusting. | Displacement in km |
| Natural gamma (log) | Borehole measurement of natural gamma radiation, dominated by potassium, uranium and thorium in clays. Used to identify shale volume, since clean sands are usually low. | API units (instrument-defined) |
| Neutron (log) | Borehole measurement of hydrogen index, interpreted mainly as porosity in fluid-filled rock. | Porosity units or % (synthetic API scale) |
| Noise | **Overloaded.** *Random* noise is uncorrelated with the signal (instrument self-noise, thermal noise); *coherent* noise is organised and can mimic signal (multiples, ground roll, microseisms, nearby machinery). *Ambient noise* is the background wavefield used deliberately in interferometry. | Same units as the signal; often quoted as a spectral density |
| Non-uniqueness | Property of an inverse problem in which several different models fit the data equally well. It is a fact about the physics and the data coverage, not a failure of the algorithm. | None |
| Normal fault | Extensional fault in which the hanging wall moves down relative to the footwall. | Dip, strike and rake in degrees |
| Normal gravity | Gravity of the reference ellipsoid as a function of latitude, given by a closed formula (Somigliana). It is a defined surface gravity, not a measured field. | m/s² or mGal; varies by about 0.05 m/s² from pole to equator |
| Normal moveout (NMO) | Increase of reflection travel time with offset relative to zero offset, approximately $\Delta t \approx x^2/(2t_0v_{rms}^2)$. Correcting it stretches far-offset wavelets, which is why far offsets are often muted. | s |
| Normal stress | Component of traction perpendicular to a surface, positive in tension in this repository unless stated otherwise. | Pa |
| Normal mode | Standing-wave mode of the whole Earth, or of a waveguide. Also called a free oscillation for the global case. | Period in s |
| Nyquist frequency | Highest frequency that can be represented unambiguously for a given sampling interval: $f_N = 1/(2\Delta t)$. Content above it aliases. | Hz |

## O

| Term | Definition | Units / typical values |
|---|---|---|
| Objective function | Synonym for misfit function: the scalar quantity an inversion minimises. | units² |
| Offset | Horizontal or slant distance between source and receiver; the *offset domain* is the arrangement of data as a function of offset rather than midpoint. | m |
| Ohm's law (differential form) | $\mathbf{J} = \sigma\mathbf{E}$, the constitutive relation underlying every electrical and electromagnetic method. In circuit form, $V = IR$. | S/m, V/m, A/m² |
| Operator | Rule mapping one function to another; in this repository, a linear operator is usually represented by a matrix and can be inverted or regularised. | Depends |
| Optical depth | Dimensionless measure of how much radiation is attenuated along a path, the integral of extinction coefficient over distance. | Dimensionless |
| Overburden | Material above a target horizon, a reservoir or an orebody; its properties must be known to correct for its effects. | Depth in m |
| Overdetermined, underdetermined | Having more, or fewer, independent data than model parameters. Underdetermined problems require additional constraints (a prior or regularisation) to give a unique answer. | None |
| Overpressure | Pore-fluid pressure above hydrostatic, usually produced by rapid burial or by fluid generation. | Pa or as a fraction of lithostatic pressure |
| Overtone | Higher mode of a normal-mode or waveguide set, with additional nodal planes in depth. | Frequency in Hz; period in s |

## P

| Term | Definition | Units / typical values |
|---|---|---|
| P wave | Compressional body wave; particle motion parallel to propagation. Fastest body wave, and the first arrival on most records. | $v_p$ in m/s or km/s |
| Paleomagnetism | Study of the remanent magnetisation locked into rocks, which records the field direction at the time of formation and underpins plate reconstructions. | Direction in degrees; intensity in A/m |
| Apparent polar wander | Path traced by the paleomagnetic pole of a continent through time, reflecting both true polar motion and continental drift. | Latitude/longitude in degrees |
| Parameter | Quantity of the model that inversion estimates. Its choice — and its spatial discretisation — is a modelling decision that shapes the result. | Model-dependent |
| Partial melt | Fraction of rock that is molten; strongly reduces seismic velocity and electrical resistivity. | Melt fraction dimensionless (0–1) |
| Particle motion | Trajectory of a material point during wave passage, used to identify wave type (linear for P, orthogonal for S, retrograde elliptical for Rayleigh). | m or m/s |
| Passive seismic | Recording without a controlled source: earthquakes, ambient noise, or microtremor. | None |
| Peak ground acceleration (PGA) | Maximum absolute acceleration during ground shaking; a standard engineering hazard parameter. | m/s² or fraction of $g_n$ |
| Period | Time for one cycle of an oscillation. | s; period is the reciprocal of frequency in Hz |
| Permeability (intrinsic) | Property of a porous medium describing how easily it transmits fluid, independent of the fluid. The darcy is the traditional unit: 1 D = 9.869 233×10⁻¹³ m². | m²; commonly quoted in darcy (D) or millidarcy (mD) |
| Perturbation | Small change from a reference model; linearised inverse theory assumes the relationship between a perturbation and its data effect is linear. | Same units as the quantity perturbed |
| Phase | **Overloaded.** (i) Position in the cycle of a wave, in radians; (ii) a distinct substance or mineral assemblage (e.g. "the olivine phase"); (iii) a stage of a project. Context disambiguates entirely. | rad or degrees for (i) |
| Phase velocity | Speed at which a constant-phase point (a crest) travels; $v = \omega/k$. Differs from group velocity whenever dispersion is present. | m/s |
| Plane wave | Idealisation in which surfaces of constant phase are planes, valid far from a source relative to wavelength. | Propagation direction as a unit vector |
| Plate | Rigid outer shell fragment whose motion relative to its neighbours is described by an Euler pole and a rate. | Rate in mm/yr or °/Ma |
| Poisson's ratio | Ratio of lateral contraction to axial extension in uniaxial stress: $\nu = (v_p^2 - 2v_s^2)/[2(v_p^2 - v_s^2)]$. Values cluster near 0.25 for many rocks; near 0.5 in fluid-saturated, poorly consolidated material. | Dimensionless |
| Pore pressure | Pressure of the fluid in the pore space; controls effective stress and therefore compaction, failure and velocity. | Pa |
| Porosity | Fraction of rock volume occupied by pores. Total porosity includes isolated pores; *effective* porosity counts only connected pores and is the one relevant to flow. | Dimensionless (0–1) or % |
| Poroelasticity | Theory coupling fluid diffusion and solid deformation (Biot), the framework for velocity dispersion, attenuation and induced-seismicity modelling. | Coupled; permeability in m² |
| Potential field | Field derived from a scalar potential satisfying Laplace's or Poisson's equation: gravity and magnetics are the two geophysical examples. | m²/s² (gravity potential); nT or mGal for the measured field |
| Potential temperature | Temperature a fluid parcel would have if brought adiabatically to a reference pressure; the variable that removes adiabatic effects and reveals real heating and mixing. | K |
| Power spectrum | Squared magnitude of the Fourier transform, per unit frequency. Averaging (Welch) reduces its variance at the cost of resolution. | units²/Hz |
| Precision | Reproducibility of repeated measurements under stated conditions; quantified by standard deviation or repeatability limits. Distinct from accuracy. | Same units as the measurement |
| Prestack | Data before summation over offsets; the domain in which velocity analysis, AVO and prestack depth migration are performed. | None |
| Primary | Reflection that has travelled from the source to a single reflector and back (or through a simple path), as distinct from a multiple. | Arrival time in s |
| Probabilistic inversion | Inversion that treats model and data as random variables and returns a posterior distribution rather than a single best model. | Parameters and their uncertainties |
| Profile | A line of measurements along the surface; the 2-D geometry for which most interpretation tools are built. | Station spacing in m |
| Projection (map) | Rule mapping positions on the ellipsoid to a plane, with unavoidable distortion of distance, area, shape or direction. State the projection and the datum with every map. | None |
| Prospecting | Historical term for the search for economic minerals, oil or water by geophysical and geological methods. | None |
| Pseudosection | In DC resistivity and IP, an apparent-property cross-section plotted against a pseudodepth defined by the electrode geometry. It resembles a geological section but is a data display, not an image of the subsurface. | Ω·m for resistivity |
| Pull-up, push-down | Migration artefacts in which a high- or low-velocity anomaly above a reflector shifts its image upward or downward. | s or m of apparent displacement |
| Pycnocline | Layer of strong vertical density gradient in the ocean, which suppresses vertical mixing. | kg/m³ per m |

## Q

| Term | Definition | Units / typical values |
|---|---|---|
| Quality factor ($Q$) | **Overloaded.** In seismology and rock physics, the dimensionless inverse of fractional energy loss per cycle: large $Q$ means low attenuation. In heat-flow writing, $Q$ often denotes heat flux or heat quantity. Units settle the ambiguity: a genuine quality factor is dimensionless. | Dimensionless (typical crustal values 50–1000, nominal) |
| Quality control (QC) | Documented checks applied to data before use: geometry, timing, gains, polarity, repeatability. QC records are part of provenance. | None |
| Quasi-static | Approximation in which time derivatives are neglected because the process is slow compared with the relevant relaxation time. | Dimensionless criterion, e.g. $\omega\tau \ll 1$ |
| Quaternary | The most recent geological period (about the last 2.6 Ma), during which ice ages and sea-level changes dominate the sedimentary and geodetic record. | Time in Ma |
| Quiet day | Geomagnetically undisturbed day used as a reference when isolating disturbance fields or solar-quiet baselines for MT processing. | K index of 2 or less (conventional) |

## R

| Term | Definition | Units / typical values |
|---|---|---|
| Radar | Active sensing with radio waves; SAR provides the imagery used in InSAR, and GPR is its near-surface, ground-coupled form. | Frequency band (L, C, X); wavelength in cm |
| Radiation pattern | Directional dependence of energy radiated by a source; for a seismic double couple it is four-lobed, which is what makes fault-plane solutions possible. | Relative amplitude (dimensionless) |
| Radiogenic heat | Heat released by radioactive decay of U, Th and K; a major contributor to continental heat flow. | µW/m³ |
| Radon transform | Integral transform summing data along lines of a chosen shape, used to separate linear and hyperbolic events (ground roll, multiples) in seismic processing. | Slowness in s/m |
| Rake | Angle describing slip direction on a fault plane, measured within the plane from the strike direction. | Degrees |
| Raster, vector | Two data models in GIS: raster is a regular grid of cells; vector is points, lines and polygons. Analysis operations are specific to each. | Raster: cell size in m |
| Ray parameter | Along-ray constant in a laterally homogeneous medium: $p = \sin i / v$. Its constancy is Snell's law in ray form. | s/m or s/° |
| Rayleigh wave | Surface wave with retrograde elliptical particle motion in the vertical plane and exponential decay with depth. The most damaging wave at moderate distance for typical building periods. | Speed in m/s |
| Rayleigh number | Dimensionless ratio of buoyancy-driven convection to diffusive transport: $Ra = \alpha g \Delta T d^3/(\kappa\nu)$. Order $10^3$ marks the onset of convection. | Dimensionless |
| Receiver function | Time series formed by deconvolving the vertical from the horizontal component of a teleseismic P coda, isolating P-to-S conversions beneath a station. | Amplitude dimensionless; time in s |
| Recurrence interval | Average time between events of a stated size on a fault or in a catalogue; its reciprocal is the rate. | Years |
| Reduction (gravity) | Any of the corrections applied to a raw gravity reading to remove known effects (instrument drift, tides, elevation, terrain, isostasy), each with a stated density or model. | mGal |
| Reference frame | Set of station coordinates and velocities with a defined origin and scale, e.g. a realisation of ITRF or of a regional frame; coordinates are meaningless without it. | Positions in m; velocities in mm/yr |
| Reflection coefficient | Ratio of reflected to incident amplitude at an interface. At normal incidence, $R = (Z_2 - Z_1)/(Z_2 + Z_1)$. | Dimensionless |
| Reflectivity (seismic) | Series of reflection coefficients as a function of two-way time or depth; the target of seismic inversion. | Dimensionless |
| Refraction | Change of propagation direction when a wave crosses an interface, described by Snell's law; also the exploration method that measures travel times of head waves from a refractor. | Angle in degrees |
| Refractor | Interface with an increase of velocity with depth that returns head waves to the surface, the target of refraction surveys. | Speed in m/s |
| Regolith | Loose, weathered material above bedrock, usually of low seismic velocity and variable thickness. | Thickness in m |
| Regional–residual separation | Division of a potential field into a long-wavelength "regional" and a shorter-wavelength "residual"; the result depends entirely on the method (polynomial fit, upward continuation, spectral filtering), so the method must be stated. | nT or mGal |
| Reliability (metric) | Precision of a measurement divided by its magnitude, or the reproducibility limit of a repeated measurement; report the definition used. | % or dimensionless |
| Remanent magnetisation | Permanent magnetisation acquired when a rock formed or was altered; the carrier of paleomagnetic information and a common source of interpretation error if treated as induced. | A/m |
| Remote sensing | Measurement of the Earth's surface or atmosphere from a distance, typically by reflected solar radiation, thermal emission or radar. | Radiance in W m⁻² sr⁻¹ µm⁻¹ |
| Repeatability, reproducibility | Repeatability is agreement of repeated measurements in the same laboratory by the same method; reproducibility is agreement across laboratories or operators. Both are quantified by a standard deviation with a stated protocol. | Same units as the measurement |
| Resolution | Ability to distinguish neighbouring features: vertical resolution comes from bandwidth, lateral resolution from aperture and from the first Fresnel zone. | Vertical: m or s; lateral: m |
| Resonance | Amplification at frequencies where a system's response peaks; site resonance in sedimentary basins is the standard seismological example. | Frequency in Hz; amplification factor dimensionless |
| Response function | **Overloaded.** The transfer function relating input to output of an instrument; also the site response (amplification) of a soil column; also the impulse response of a medium. | Dimensionless or V per unit input |
| Response spectrum | Peak response of a family of single-degree-of-freedom oscillators to a ground-motion record, plotted against period. | Acceleration in m/s² or g |
| Reverberation | Repetitive reflection (ringing) within a waveguide such as a water layer or a coal seam. | Period in s |
| Ricker wavelet | Zero-phase wavelet defined by its dominant frequency, standard in synthetic seismograms and in wavelet estimation. | Time in s; frequency in Hz |
| Ridge push | Plate-driving force arising from the elevated, gravitationally unstable lithosphere at mid-ocean ridges. | Force per unit length in N/m |
| Rigidity | Synonym for shear modulus: resistance to shear deformation at constant volume. | Pa |
| RMS (root mean square) | Square root of the mean of the squares, a standard amplitude measure for oscillating signals; for a sinusoid it is $1/\sqrt{2}$ of the peak. | Same units as the signal |
| RMS velocity | Offset-squared-weighted average velocity used to flatten reflection hyperbolae in a layered medium; a stacking parameter, not a physical layer velocity. | m/s |
| Rock physics | Study and modelling of the relations between rock properties (porosity, saturation, mineralogy, texture) and their measurable elastic, electrical and magnetic responses. | None |
| Rotation rate of the Earth | Nominal value $\Omega \approx 7.292\,115\times10^{-5}$ rad/s; used in the Coriolis parameter and in the Eötvös correction, with small observed variations that are themselves measured by geodesy. | rad/s |
| Run-up | Maximum vertical height above a reference level reached by a tsunami on land. | m |

## S

| Term | Definition | Units / typical values |
|---|---|---|
| S wave | Shear body wave; particle motion perpendicular to propagation and polarised either in the vertical plane (SV) or horizontally (SH). Slower than P, and unable to propagate through liquids. | $v_s$ in m/s or km/s |
| Salinity | Mass of dissolved salts per mass of seawater, now expressed through measured conductivity on the practical salinity scale. | Dimensionless (g/kg historically); PSU |
| Sample rate | Number of samples per second recorded by a digitising system; it fixes the Nyquist frequency. | samples/s (Hz) |
| Sampling theorem | Statement that a band-limited signal is fully determined by samples taken at more than twice its highest frequency. | None |
| SAR | Synthetic aperture radar: radar imaging that synthesises a long antenna from a moving platform, giving fine along-track resolution. | Wavelength in cm; resolution in m |
| Saturation | Fraction of the pore space occupied by a given fluid; water saturation and oil saturation sum to one in a two-phase system. | Dimensionless (0–1) |
| Scalar, vector, tensor | Order-0, order-1 and order-2 (or higher) quantities; whether a property must be rotated as one or the other matters in anisotropic media. | None |
| Scattering | Redirection of wave energy by inhomogeneities smaller than or comparable to the wavelength; the mechanism producing coda and the reason attenuation is not purely absorption. | None |
| Sea level (mean) | Time-averaged height of the sea surface at a tide gauge, relative to a stated local or geodetic datum. Trends differ from place to place because land also moves. | m; trends in mm/yr |
| Sea surface temperature (SST) | Temperature of the ocean's uppermost layer, from in-situ, satellite or reanalysis sources, each with its own sampling and bias characteristics. | °C |
| Secular variation | Slow change of the geomagnetic field with time (decadal), different in origin from the rapid external variations and from geological-scale field changes. | nT/yr |
| Sedimentary basin | Region of long-lived subsidence filled with sediment, the standard setting for hydrocarbon and groundwater exploration and for most reflection seismology. | Thickness in km |
| Seismic moment | Scalar measure of earthquake size: $M_0 = \mu \bar{A}\bar{D}$, with shear modulus $\mu$, fault area $A$ and average slip $D$. It is the quantity $M_w$ is derived from. | N·m (often written dyn·cm in older literature) |
| Seismic reflection | Method using reflected energy to image structure; in exploration it is the highest-resolution deep method, and its images are of impedance contrasts, not of lithology. | Two-way time in s |
| Seismic refraction | Method using travel times of head waves and first arrivals to determine velocity structure; insensitive to thin layering and low-velocity zones. | m/s |
| Seismic velocity | Speed of wave propagation through rock; controlled by the elastic moduli, density, porosity and pore fluid. Written $v_p$ and $v_s$; sometimes as $V_P$, $V_S$ in the literature. | m/s or km/s |
| Seismogram | Record of ground motion with time. A *seismograph* (or seismometer plus recorder) is the instrument. | m, m/s or m/s²; time in s |
| Seismotectonics | Study of the relationship between seismicity and active geological structure. | None |
| Sensitivity kernel | Function describing how much a single datum is affected by each part of the model; the basis for resolution analysis and for experimental design. | Data units per model unit |
| Shadow zone | Region of the Earth that receives no direct P or S energy from a given earthquake because of refraction and of the core; its geometry constrains core radius. | Angular distance in degrees; depth in km |
| Shear modulus | Resistance to shear deformation; $\mu = \rho v_s^2$ for an isotropic elastic solid. Also called the second Lamé parameter. | Pa |
| Shear-wave splitting | Splitting of an S wave into two orthogonally polarised arrivals with different speeds, diagnostic of anisotropy; the fast direction is interpreted as the fabric or stress direction. | Delay time in s or ms; fast direction in degrees |
| Shuey approximation | Widely used small-angle linearisation of the AVO reflection coefficient in terms of intercept, gradient and curvature, expressed with $v_p$, $v_s$ and density contrasts. | Dimensionless |
| Signal-to-noise ratio | Ratio of signal power (or amplitude) to noise power (or amplitude), often in decibels. State whether power or amplitude, since the two differ by a factor of two in dB. | dB or dimensionless |
| Skin depth | Depth at which an electromagnetic field has decayed to $1/e$ of its surface value: $\delta = \sqrt{2/(\omega\mu\sigma)}$. It sets the depth of investigation of EM methods. | m |
| Slowness | Reciprocal of phase velocity, $s = 1/v$, the natural variable of ray theory; the slowness vector's components are the ray parameters conjugate to distance. | s/m |
| Snell's law | $\sin i_1/v_1 = \sin i_2/v_2 = p$: the ray parameter is conserved across an interface. | Dimensionless ratio of speeds |
| Soil moisture | Volumetric water content of the unsaturated zone; strongly controls GPR velocity and electrical resistivity. | m³/m³ or % |
| Source time function | Time history of the source, whose duration controls the corner frequency of the far-field spectrum. | N·m/s for a moment-rate function; duration in s |
| Spectral analysis | Estimation of the frequency content of a signal; the choice of window and taper changes the estimated spectrum, so both must be reported. | Spectrum in units²/Hz |
| Spectrogram | Time–frequency display of spectral amplitude, whose trade-off between time and frequency resolution is set by the window length. | Amplitude in dB |
| Spherical harmonic | Basis function on the sphere used for global gravity, magnetic and geoid models. | Dimensionless coefficients or nT |
| Stack | Summation of traces to increase signal-to-noise ratio; a stack of $N$ traces reduces random noise by $\sqrt{N}$. | Same units as the traces |
| Standard deviation | Measure of the spread of a distribution, the square root of the variance. Distinguish the population value $\sigma$ from the sample estimate $s$. | Same units as the data |
| Standard error | Standard deviation of an estimator, decreasing as $1/\sqrt{N}$ for independent samples; the basis of confidence intervals. | Same units as the estimate |
| Static correction | Time shift applied to a trace to remove the effect of near-surface velocity and elevation variation before stacking. | s or ms |
| Station | A recording site with one or more instruments, identified in an FDSN network/station/location/channel code. | Code; coordinates in degrees |
| Stereonet | Equal-area or equal-angle projection used to plot and analyse orientations of planes and lines in structural geology. | None |
| Stokes coefficients | Spherical-harmonic coefficients $C_{lm}$, $S_{lm}$ of the gravity field, whose time variations are used to measure mass redistribution. | Dimensionless |
| Strain | Dimensionless measure of deformation: the change in length or shape per unit length. | Dimensionless; usually reported in µε (microstrain) |
| Strain rate | Rate of change of strain; the parameter that decides brittle versus ductile behaviour. | s⁻¹ |
| Stratigraphy | Description and correlation of layered rock successions, the framework within which geophysical interpretations are placed. | None |
| Stream function | Scalar function whose derivatives give a divergence-free 2-D velocity field; used for mantle flow and for incompressible fluid problems. | m²/s |
| Stress | Force per unit area acting on a surface; a tensor quantity with six independent components. *Stress drop* is the decrease in shear stress during an earthquake. | Pa; in situ values of order 10–100 MPa at seismogenic depth (nominal) |
| Strike, strike-slip | *Strike* is the compass direction of the line of intersection of a plane with the horizontal; a *strike-slip* fault has predominantly horizontal slip. | Degrees |
| Subduction | Underthrusting of one lithospheric plate beneath another, producing the deepest earthquakes, arc volcanism and the largest earthquakes. | Convergence rate in mm/yr |
| Subsidence | Downward motion of the ground or of a basin, whether tectonic, thermal, or induced by fluid extraction. | mm/yr |
| Superposition | Principle that the response of a linear system to a sum of inputs is the sum of the responses; the justification for convolution-based modelling and for Born approximation. | None |
| Surface wave | Wave guided by a free surface or an interface, with amplitude decaying with depth: Rayleigh and Love waves in seismology, interface waves in acoustics. | Speed in m/s |
| Survey design | Choice of geometry (line spacing, offsets, azimuths, station spacing) to meet resolution and depth objectives. Design determines what interpretations are possible. | Spacing in m |
| Swath mapping | Bathymetric mapping with a fan of beams, giving continuous coverage of a strip of seafloor. | Beam angle in degrees; depth in m |
| Synthetic seismogram | Seismogram computed from a model rather than recorded, used to test processing and to tie logs to seismic data. | Amplitude in the model's units; time in s |
| SI | Système International d'Unités, the unit system used in this repository; base units m, kg, s, A, K, mol, cd. | None |

## Caveat

Typical values in this page are orders of magnitude for orientation only. In
particular, values for $Q$, permeability, stress and sea-level trend vary by
orders of magnitude between sites and must be measured or cited from a specific
study, never taken from a general glossary.
