# Glossary: A-F

Terms beginning with **A** through **F**, alphabetically within each letter.
Entries are one to three sentences: enough to resolve a word you met in a
lesson or a paper, not enough to teach the topic. Where a term is taught
somewhere in this repository, the lesson is linked from the vocabulary note at
the end of the page.

## How to use this page

Search for the word (or its acronym) with your browser's find function. If a
term you need is missing, that is a gap in this glossary — record it and report
it. Entries that carry a numerical value give a *typical* or *conventional*
value only; they are marked as such, and are not substitutes for a measurement.

**Overloaded words are flagged.** Words such as *anomaly*, *fold*, *phase* and
*dispersion* mean different things in different subfields. This page says so
explicitly rather than picking one meaning silently.

*Provenance:* this page contains no measured data. Every entry is a definition
summarised from standard geophysical usage; no value is invented, and no
copyrighted text is reproduced.

## A

| Term | Definition | Units / typical values |
|---|---|---|
| Absolute gravimeter | Instrument that measures the local acceleration of gravity directly, by timing a free-falling test mass, rather than by comparison with a reference station. | Reading in m/s², reported in mGal or µGal (1 mGal = 10⁻⁵ m/s²) |
| Acceleration (ground motion) | Rate of change of ground velocity. In engineering seismology the peak ground acceleration (PGA) is the quantity most directly tied to damage. | m/s²; often quoted as a fraction of standard gravity $g_n$ |
| Accuracy versus precision | *Accuracy* is closeness to the true value; *precision* is repeatability of repeated measurements. A precise instrument can be biased; an accurate one can be noisy. | Both are reported in the same units as the quantity, or as an uncertainty |
| Acoustic impedance | Product of density and wave speed along a wave path: $Z = \rho v$. Contrast in $Z$ across an interface controls reflection strength. | kg m⁻² s⁻¹; older literature uses the rayl, numerically identical |
| Adiabatic | Describes a process with no heat exchange with the surroundings. Adiabatic ascent of air cools because of expansion, not heat loss. | Dimensionless descriptor; see *lapse rate* |
| Advection | Transport of a property by the mean motion of a fluid, as distinct from diffusion, which transports it down a gradient. | Depends on the property (e.g. K/s for temperature) |
| Airborne survey | Geophysical survey flown on an aircraft, typically magnetics, radiometrics, or time-domain EM, with flight lines and perpendicular tie lines for levelling. | Line spacing and terrain clearance in m; field in nT or µV |
| Airy isostasy | Model in which topographic loads are supported by a crustal root of lower density, so that pressure at some compensation depth is equal in columns of different elevation. Contrast with Pratt isostasy, which varies density instead. | Root depth in km; densities in kg/m³ |
| Aliasing | Appearance of a false low frequency when a signal containing frequencies above the Nyquist frequency is sampled. The energy is folded, not lost. | The alias appears at the frequency $f_N - (f - f_N)$ with $f_N = 1/(2\Delta t)$ |
| Allochthon | Rock mass that has been transported from its place of origin by faulting. Its counterpart is *autochthonous*, meaning in place. | None |
| Amplitude | Magnitude of the peak (or another stated measure, such as RMS) of an oscillating quantity. "Amplitude" alone is ambiguous until the measure is stated. | Same units as the quantity |
| Amplitude versus offset (AVO) | Analysis of how reflection amplitude varies with source–receiver offset, used to constrain the contrast in P-wave speed, S-wave speed and density. The first-order behaviour is linear in $\sin^2\theta$ and is described by an intercept and a gradient. | Amplitude dimensionless after normalisation; angle in degrees |
| Anelasticity | Departure from perfectly elastic behaviour in which energy is dissipated during cyclic loading. Described by the quality factor $Q$. | $Q$ dimensionless; $1/Q$ is a fractional energy loss per cycle |
| Anisotropy (seismic) | Directional dependence of wave speed, caused by aligned minerals, layered bedding, or aligned fractures. Vertical transverse isotropy (VTI) is the common layered case. Thomsen's parameters $\varepsilon$, $\delta$, $\gamma$ quantify the strength of the anisotropy. | Dimensionless parameters; speeds in m/s |
| Anomaly | **Overloaded.** In potential fields, the difference between an observed field and a chosen reference or regional field. In gravity, the word usually means one of a family of reductions: free-air, Bouguer, isostatic. The word always implies a choice of reference, which must be stated. | mGal for gravity, nT for magnetics |
| Apparent resistivity | Resistivity value computed from a measured resistance by assuming a homogeneous half-space; it is an average over the volume actually sampled, not a material property. | Ω·m; symbol $\rho_a$ |
| Aquifer | Permeable rock or sediment that yields usable quantities of water to a well or spring. An *aquitard* transmits water slowly; an *aquiclude* is effectively impermeable for the purpose at hand. | Hydraulic conductivity $K$ in m/s |
| Archie's law | Empirical relation between the resistivity of a saturated clean sand, its porosity and the resistivity of the pore fluid. Written $\rho = a\rho_f \phi^{-m}$, with $a$ near 1 and cementation exponent $m$ near 2 for many sandstones. | $\rho$ in Ω·m, $\phi$ dimensionless |
| Array (seismic) | A group of sensors with a known geometry, analysed collectively so that the signal's direction and speed can be estimated from the time differences between elements. | Aperture in m; element spacing in m |
| Aspect ratio | Ratio of a body's dimensions, for example a magma body's width to its height, or a basin's width to depth. | Dimensionless |
| Asthenosphere | Weak, low-viscosity, low-seismic-velocity layer beneath the lithosphere, over which plates can move. Not a chemical layer: a mechanical one. | Depth extent nominally ~100–300 km; viscosity is nominal |
| Attenuation | Loss of amplitude with distance beyond geometric spreading, caused by absorption and scattering. Characterised by the quality factor $Q$; a wavelet's high frequencies decay fastest. | $Q$ dimensionless; attenuation coefficient $\alpha$ in m⁻¹ or dB/m |
| Autocorrelation | Correlation of a signal with itself at a lag $\tau$; reveals periodicity and is the basis of the Wiener filter used for predictive deconvolution. | Dimensionless if normalised; lag in s |
| Autoregressive model | Time-series model in which the present value is a weighted sum of past values plus a random innovation. | Coefficients dimensionless; innovation in the units of the series |
| Azimuth | Horizontal angle of a direction, measured clockwise from north. | Degrees (°) or radians; the *back-azimuth* is the opposite direction, from receiver to source |

## B

| Term | Definition | Units / typical values |
|---|---|---|
| Band-pass filter | Filter that passes a band of frequencies and suppresses those outside it, defined by corner frequencies. | Corner frequencies in Hz; slopes in dB/octave |
| Basement | Crystalline or strongly metamorphosed rock beneath the sedimentary sequence, treated as an acoustic and electrical contrast in exploration. | None |
| Bathymetry | Measurement of seafloor depth below a datum (usually mean sea level). | m; positive downward |
| Bedrock | Consolidated rock beneath soil or regolith. | None |
| Benioff zone | Inclined zone of deep earthquakes defining a subducting slab. | Depth in km |
| Body wave | Wave that travels through the interior of a medium: a P wave (compressional) or S wave (shear). Contrast with a surface wave, which is confined to a boundary. | Speeds in m/s |
| Body-wave magnitude ($m_b$) | Magnitude determined from the amplitude of a short-period P wave; saturates for large earthquakes and is therefore not used for hazard scaling. | Dimensionless |
| Borehole | Vertical or deviated hole drilled for measurement, sampling or well-logging. | Depth in m |
| Born approximation | Linearisation of scattering that treats the scattered field as produced by the incident field acting on the perturbation alone, valid when the perturbation is weak. | Dimensionless statement of validity; typically $\varepsilon \ll 1$ |
| Bouguer anomaly | Gravity anomaly after removing the free-air and Bouguer-plate effects, using a stated reduction density. Residual structure and mass distributions remain. | mGal; reduction density in kg/m³ |
| Bouguer plate correction | Effect of an infinite horizontal slab of density $\rho$ and thickness $h$: $\Delta g = 2\pi G \rho h$. Numerically $0.04193\rho h$ mGal with $\rho$ in kg/m³ and $h$ in m. | mGal |
| Boussinesq approximation | Treatment of buoyancy in a fluid in which density variations are neglected everywhere except in the gravity-force term. Standard in mantle convection and geophysical fluid dynamics. | Dimensionless; validity requires density variations $\ll$ mean density |
| Bright spot | Anomalously strong reflection amplitude, classically associated with gas-bearing sand in seismic sections; not unique to gas. | Amplitude in dB relative to a reference |
| Bulk density | Mass per unit total volume, including pore fluid. Contrast with grain density, which excludes pore volume. | kg/m³; crustal rocks typically 2000–2900 |
| Bulk modulus | Resistance to volumetric compression: $K = -V\,\partial P/\partial V$. | Pa |

## C

| Term | Definition | Units / typical values |
|---|---|---|
| Caldera | Large volcanic depression formed by collapse following a major eruption. | Diameter in km |
| Capillary pressure | Difference between non-wetting and wetting fluid pressures in a porous medium; controls fluid distribution in partly saturated rock. | Pa |
| Casing | Steel pipe lining a borehole, which strongly affects electrical and EM measurements in the hole. | Diameter in mm or inches; resistivity in Ω·m |
| Causality | Property of a physical response that cannot precede its input. For a wavelet, causality plus minimum-phase assumptions permit phase to be estimated from amplitude. | Dimensionless descriptor |
| Cepstrum | Fourier transform of the logarithm of a spectrum; converts convolution into addition and is used for echo detection and wavelet estimation. | Units of time (quefrency, s) |
| Coda | Later part of a seismogram, after the direct arrivals, composed of scattered energy. The coda of local earthquakes is used to estimate the coda quality factor $Q_c$ by coda-normalisation. | $Q_c$ dimensionless |
| Coherence | Normalised measure of the linear relationship between two signals as a function of frequency, between 0 and 1. Not to be confused with *coherency* used in array processing, or with *coherence* meaning continuity of a reflection across a stacked section. | Dimensionless, in [0, 1] |
| Common midpoint (CMP) gather | Collected traces sharing an approximate midpoint but with differing offsets, used for velocity analysis and stacking. *CDP* (common depth point) is an older synonym; *CRP* (common reflection point) is used when the medium is anisotropic or dipping. | Offsets in m; fold is dimensionless |
| Compaction | Reduction of porosity of sediment under increasing burial load. | Porosity dimensionless; depths in m |
| Compliance | Inverse of stiffness; in rock physics, the strain per unit applied stress. | m²/N or Pa⁻¹ |
| Conductance | Product of conductivity and thickness for a layer; a small number of large conductance values control MT responses. | siemens (S); 1 S = 1 A/V |
| Conductivity (electrical) | Reciprocal of resistivity; ability of a material to carry current. | S/m; reciprocal of Ω·m |
| Constitutive law | Relation between stress and strain (or another driving force and its response) that defines a material's behaviour. | Depends; e.g. Pa per unit strain |
| Continuation (field) | Extrapolation of a potential field upward or downward in space, using the fact that the field satisfies Laplace's equation outside sources. Downward continuation is unstable because it amplifies short wavelengths. | m for elevation change; field in nT or mGal |
| Convolution | Mathematical operation combining two functions, written $y(t) = x(t) \ast w(t)$; the model of a linear, time-invariant measurement. | Units of the product of the inputs |
| Critical angle | Angle of incidence beyond which a refracted wave travels along an interface; the reflected energy becomes total. | $\theta_c = \arcsin(v_1/v_2)$, degrees |
| Crust | Outermost silicate shell above the Moho. *Typical* thickness: continental 30–40 km, oceanic 6–7 km; both vary substantially with tectonic setting, so do not treat these as values to cite in a specific region. | Thickness in km; density in kg/m³ |
| Curie depth | Depth at which temperature reaches the Curie temperature of the dominant magnetic minerals, below which magnetisation is absent; estimated from the spectrum of magnetic anomalies. | km; Curie temperature in °C, mineral-dependent |
| Current density | Electric current per unit cross-sectional area. | A/m² |

## D

| Term | Definition | Units / typical values |
|---|---|---|
| Darcy's law | Empirical linear relation between specific discharge and hydraulic gradient: $q = -K\nabla h$. The constant $K$ is hydraulic conductivity; the intrinsic permeability $k$ is a property of the medium alone, with $K = k\rho g/\mu$. | $q$ in m/s; $K$ in m/s; $k$ in m² |
| Data provenance | Recorded origin and history of a dataset: who made it, when, with what instrument, and what processing it has received. A number without provenance cannot be checked. | None |
| Deconvolution | Inverse filtering that removes the effect of a known or estimated source signature, instrument response, or multiple from data. | Dimensionless filter; output in the units of the convolved quantity |
| Declination (magnetic) | Angle between magnetic north and true north at a place and time. | Degrees; changes by minutes of arc per year |
| DEM, DSM, DTM | Digital Elevation Model (bare or unspecified surface), Digital Surface Model (including vegetation and buildings), Digital Terrain Model (bare ground). The three are not interchangeable. | Elevation in m relative to a stated vertical datum |
| Density | Mass per unit volume. Symbol $\rho$ in this repository; **overloaded** with resistivity in parts of the electrical literature, where $\rho$ also means Ω·m. Units disambiguate. | kg/m³ |
| Depth | In this repository, $z$ is positive downward, matching potential-field practice. Some seismological texts take $z$ positive upward; check before combining formulas. | m or km |
| Detachment | Low-angle normal fault accommodating large extension, often the floor of a rift. | Dip in degrees |
| Dielectric permittivity | Constant relating electric displacement to electric field. Relative permittivity (dielectric constant) for dry rock is a few; for water about 80, which is what makes GPR sensitive to moisture. | F/m; relative permittivity dimensionless |
| Diffraction | Scattering of wave energy by an edge or small inhomogeneity, which smears a reflection into a hyperbolic signature in unmigrated data. Migration collapses it. | Amplitude depends on contrast |
| Diffusivity | Rate at which a quantity spreads by diffusion, for example thermal diffusivity $\kappa = k/(\rho c_p)$. | m²/s |
| Dike, sill | Tabular igneous intrusion cutting across (dike) or parallel to (sill) layering. | Thickness in m; magnetic signature in nT |
| Dilatancy | Volume change accompanying shear deformation of a granular or cracked material; a mechanism for precursory changes in water level and seismic velocity. | Dimensionless strain |
| Dimensional analysis | Checking or deriving relations by requiring dimensional consistency. See the dedicated quick-reference page. | None |
| Dip moveout (DMO) | Correction applied before stack to account for the differing midpoint positions of dipping reflectors at different offsets. | s; applied in the offset domain |
| Dipping-layer refraction | Travel-time curve beyond the critical distance when the refractor dips, giving different apparent velocities in the updip and downdip directions and an asymmetric intercept time. | s (intercept time); m/s (apparent velocity) |
| Direct wave | Wave travelling from source to receiver without interacting with an interface; its travel time grows linearly with offset. | s; speed in m/s |
| Dispersion | **Overloaded.** (i) In surface-wave seismology, the dependence of phase or group velocity on frequency, which is what makes surface waves informative about shear-speed structure. (ii) In numerical modelling, spurious spreading of a waveform because different spatial frequencies travel at slightly different numerical speeds. | Velocity in m/s versus frequency in Hz |
| Displacement | Vector change of position of a material point, symbol $\mathbf{u}$. | m |
| Divergence | Vector operator $\nabla\cdot$; of a flux gives the source strength per unit volume. | Depends on the field |
| Drawdown | Lowering of the water table or potentiometric surface caused by pumping. | m |
| Ductile versus brittle | Whether strain is accommodated by distributed flow (ductile) or by localised fracture and slip (brittle). The transition depends on temperature, pressure, strain rate and mineralogy. | Dimensionless; temperature in K |
| Dynamic range | Ratio between the largest and smallest signal a system can record, usually expressed in decibels. | dB |

## E

| Term | Definition | Units / typical values |
|---|---|---|
| Earthquake | Sudden release of elastic strain energy by slip on a fault, radiating seismic waves. | Energy in J; magnitude dimensionless |
| Effective stress | Stress carried by the solid framework: total stress minus pore pressure (Terzaghi). Effective stress controls friction, compaction and failure. | Pa |
| Elastic moduli | Constants relating stress and strain in a linear elastic solid: Young's modulus $E$, Poisson's ratio $\nu$, bulk modulus $K$, shear modulus $\mu$, Lamé parameters $\lambda,\mu$. Four of the six are independent; any two determine the rest in an isotropic medium. | Pa (except $\nu$, dimensionless) |
| Electric field | Force per unit charge. | V/m |
| Electrical resistivity | Resistance of a unit cube of material to current flow; the reciprocal of conductivity. | Ω·m |
| Electromagnetic induction | Generation of currents, and hence secondary magnetic fields, in a conductor by a time-varying magnetic field. The physical basis of MT, TDEM and FDEM methods. | Currents in A; fields in T or nT |
| Elevation correction (gravity) | Combined free-air and Bouguer reductions bringing a reading to a common datum. | mGal |
| Ellipsoid (reference) | Mathematical surface of revolution used as a horizontal and height datum; the WGS 84 and GRS 80 ellipsoids are the common geodetic choices. Parameters must be stated, since ellipsoids differ. | Semi-axes in m; flattening dimensionless |
| Energy (seismic) | Radiated energy, related to magnitude by an empirical logarithmic relation; the precise coefficients depend on the magnitude scale and on the seismic moment estimate. | J |
| Envelope | Smooth outline of the amplitude of an oscillatory signal; used for attenuation estimation. | Same units as the signal |
| Eötvös | Unit of gravity gradient: 1 E = 10⁻⁹ s⁻². The Eötvös correction removes the effect of a moving platform in marine and airborne gravity. | E (10⁻⁹ s⁻²) |
| Epicentre | Point on the Earth's surface directly above the hypocentre (focus). | Latitude and longitude in degrees |
| Epoch (geodetic) | A stated instant for which coordinates are valid; plate motion makes this essential. | Date |
| Equal-area projection | Map projection preserving relative area at the expense of shape, used for any global map on which densities are compared. | None |
| Equipotential surface | Surface of constant potential; on a static Earth, the geoid is one particular equipotential of gravity. | Potential in m²/s² |
| Euler pole | Rotation axis of the relative motion between two rigid plates; the pole and rate define the whole relative motion. | Latitude/longitude in degrees; rate in °/Ma |
| Evapotranspiration | Combined loss of water to the atmosphere by evaporation and plant transpiration. | mm/day or mm/year |
| Explicit versus implicit (numerical) | Whether a solution update uses only known earlier values (explicit) or requires solving a system at the new time level (implicit). Explicit schemes are limited by a stability condition. | Dimensionless |

## F

| Term | Definition | Units / typical values |
|---|---|---|
| Facies | Body of rock distinguished by its lithology or fossil content, interpreted as recording a particular environment or process. | None |
| Fault plane solution | Description of fault orientation and slip direction derived from the polarity of first motions or from waveform modelling; also called a focal mechanism. | Strike, dip and rake in degrees |
| Fick's law | Diffusion flux proportional to the negative concentration gradient: $\mathbf{J} = -D\nabla c$. | Flux in mol m⁻² s⁻¹; $D$ in m²/s |
| Filter | Operator that modifies the amplitude and phase of the frequency or wavenumber content of data. See the signal-processing folder for design and edge effects. | Response dimensionless or in dB |
| Finite difference, finite element, finite volume | Families of numerical schemes for partial differential equations, distinguished by how the domain is discretised and how derivatives are approximated. | Grid spacing in m; time step in s |
| Flexural rigidity | Resistance of an elastic plate to bending: $D = ET_e^3/[12(1-\nu^2)]$. | N·m; effective elastic thickness $T_e$ in m |
| Focal mechanism | See *fault plane solution*. | Degrees |
| Fold (structural) | Bend in layered rock, anticline or syncline. | Wavelength and amplitude in m |
| Fold (data) | **Overloaded.** The number of traces contributing to one stacked trace, as in "60-fold data". | Dimensionless |
| Forward modelling | Predicting data from a model and comparing with observation; the opposite of inversion. | None |
| Fourier transform | Decomposition of a function into a continuous or discrete sum of sinusoids. Conventions differ in the sign of the exponent and in the placement of $1/2\pi$; this repository uses $e^{-i\omega t}$ forward. | Amplitude spectrum in units·s; phase in rad |
| Fractal | Object or series whose statistics are scale-invariant over a range of scales, described by a fractal dimension. | Dimensionless |
| Free-air anomaly | Gravity anomaly after removing normal gravity and the free-air (elevation) correction only, without removing terrain mass. | mGal |
| Free-air correction | Change of gravity with elevation above the datum, $2g/a \approx 0.3086$ mGal/m using standard gravity and mean Earth radius; it accounts for distance from the Earth's centre, not for mass. | mGal/m |
| Free oscillations | Global standing-wave modes of the Earth excited by very large earthquakes and recorded for days; used to constrain deep structure. | Periods in s (thousands of seconds for the lowest modes) |
| Frequency | Number of cycles per unit time; symbol $f$. Related to angular frequency by $\omega = 2\pi f$. | Hz (s⁻¹); $\omega$ in rad/s |
| Fresnel zone | Region of a reflector whose reflections arrive within half a period of each other, and so interfere constructively; sets the horizontal resolution of unmigrated data. | Radius in m, growing with depth and with wavelength |
| Friction (rate and state) | Frictional resistance along a surface that depends on slip rate and on the history of contact; the framework used to model earthquake nucleation and slow slip. | Coefficient of friction dimensionless |
| Full-waveform inversion (FWI) | Inversion that fits the complete recorded waveform rather than travel times or amplitudes alone, requiring a good starting model because the misfit is highly non-linear. | Velocity in m/s; misfit normalised |
| Fundamental mode | Lowest-frequency mode of a waveguide or normal-mode set; for surface waves, the mode with no internal nodal structure in depth. | Frequency in Hz; velocity in m/s |

## Caveat

Definitions here are working definitions for this repository's lessons. Where a
term has a formal definition in a standards document — for example *geoid*,
*ellipsoid*, or *magnitude scale* — the authoritative source is the responsible
body, and the term should be quoted from there in publication. This page does
not reproduce copyrighted glossaries; the two standard printed glossaries are
listed in the bibliography module.
