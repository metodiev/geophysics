# Exercises — 10-01-05 Vertical and Horizontal Resolution Limits

Attempt before the [solutions](./05-gpr-resolution-solutions.md). Lesson:
[05 Resolution Limits](../05-gpr-resolution.md). All models **SYNTHETIC**.

## ★ Foundational

**★1.** For $v = 0.09993\ \mathrm{m\,ns^{-1}}$ and $f_0 = 200\ \mathrm{MHz}$,
compute $\lambda$, $\lambda/2$, $\lambda/4$ and $\lambda/8$.

**★2.** Compute the first Fresnel radius (two-way $\lambda/2$ convention) at
$d = 1.0\ \mathrm{m}$ for $\lambda = 0.4996\ \mathrm{m}$, both exactly and with
$\sqrt{\lambda d/2}$, and give the percentage difference.

**★3.** Compute the maximum unaliased dip for $\Delta x = 0.125\ \mathrm{m}$ and
$\Delta x = 0.25\ \mathrm{m}$ at $\lambda = 0.4996\ \mathrm{m}$.

## ★★ Applied

**★★4.** Two reflectors are $0.30\ \mathrm{m}$ apart in a medium with
$\lambda = 0.4996\ \mathrm{m}$. Using the demonstration's Rayleigh-type criterion
($0.170\lambda$), state whether they are resolved, and compute the two-way time
separation.

**★★5.** A thin bed has an amplitude $0.35$ of a single reflector at the same
depth. Using the fitted thin-bed slope of $21.32$ per metre, estimate its
thickness and state why the estimate is one-sided.

**★★6.** A $30^\circ$ dipping event is sampled at $\Delta x = 0.30\ \mathrm{m}$
with $\lambda = 0.4996\ \mathrm{m}$. Compute the apparent dip with (5.2), giving
the value of $m$ used.

## ★★★ Research-oriented

**★★★7.** Using the aperture-dependent point-spread widths ($0.951\lambda$ at
$\pm0.5\ \mathrm{m}$, $0.550\lambda$ at $\pm1\ \mathrm{m}$, $0.450\lambda$ at
$\pm2\ \mathrm{m}$, $0.350\lambda$ at $\pm4\ \mathrm{m}$ for $z = 1\ \mathrm{m}$),
estimate the aperture needed to resolve two targets $0.2\ \mathrm{m}$ apart at
$z = 1\ \mathrm{m}$, and state the assumption that limits your extrapolation.

**★★★8.** Argue, with the numbers of this lesson, why raising the antenna
frequency from $200$ to $400\ \mathrm{MHz}$ improves the *vertical* resolution
much more reliably than the *horizontal* one.
