# Exercises — 10-01-01 GPR Physics

Attempt before the [solutions](./01-gpr-physics-solutions.md). Lesson:
[01 GPR Physics](../01-gpr-physics.md). All models **SYNTHETIC**.

## ★ Foundational

**★1.** Compute the wave speed in metres per nanosecond for
$\varepsilon_r = 1$, $4$, $16$ and $80.1$. State the unit of each factor in
$v = c/\sqrt{\varepsilon_r}$.

**★2.** A reflection is picked at $t = 40.0\ \mathrm{ns}$. Convert it to depth
for $v = 0.060$ and $v = 0.075\ \mathrm{m\,ns^{-1}}$.

**★3.** Compute the normal-incidence reflection coefficient and the energy
fractions for $\varepsilon_1 = 4 \to \varepsilon_2 = 25$.

## ★★ Applied

**★★4.** Compute the loss tangent at $f = 100\ \mathrm{MHz}$ for
$\varepsilon_r = 9$ and $\sigma = 2\times10^{-3}\ \mathrm{S\,m^{-1}}$, and decide
whether the low-loss formula $v = c/\sqrt{\varepsilon_r}$ may be used to better
than $1\%$.

**★★5.** A measured speed is $0.07\ \mathrm{m\,ns^{-1}}$ with an uncertainty of
$\pm3\%$. Convert it to a permittivity and state its uncertainty.

**★★6.** The demonstration measures a Ricker spectral mean of
$225.676\ \mathrm{MHz}$ for $f_0 = 200\ \mathrm{MHz}$. Reproduce
$\left(2/\sqrt{\pi}\right)f_0$ and explain, in one sentence, why the mean
exceeds the peak.

## ★★★ Research-oriented

**★★★7.** A $2.0\ \mathrm{m}$ deep reflector is imaged with $\varepsilon_r = 9$
assumed. Compute the two-way time, then the depth error if the true permittivity
is $16$, and state which assumption caused the error.

**★★★8.** At $\tan\delta = 1$ the low-loss speed is $9.87\%$ too high. Estimate
the depth error this produces at $3\ \mathrm{m}$ and state the sign, using the
$d = vt/2$ relation and the *measured* time.
